## Introduction

The alleged MJ-12 authorisation date, **24 September 1947**, sits in an unusually convincing piece of historical scenery. James [Forrestal]({{ 'forrestal/' | relative_url }}) had become the first Secretary of Defense only a week earlier; the National Security Act had just brought the new defence and intelligence structure into operation; the Central Intelligence Agency (CIA) was entering its statutory role; and the first formal meeting of the National Security Council (NSC) would take place **two days later**, on 26 September.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.defense.gov/Multimedia/Biographies/Article-View/Article/571293/james-v-forrestal/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.defense.gov">[defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Historical Office James V. Forrestal &gt; Historical Office &gt; Article View</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Historical OfficeJames V. Forrestal &gt; Historical Office &gt; Article View...</span></span></span>

That timing helps explain why the alleged MJ-12 papers can initially feel plausible. Their supposed secret committee appears not on an arbitrary date but precisely when Truman's administration was reorganising the machinery through which exceptionally sensitive military and intelligence matters might be handled. Even more strikingly, Truman's genuine appointment diary shows **Forrestal and Vannevar Bush meeting the president together at 11:30 a.m. on 24 September**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_k=&amp;page=75" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Truman Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Library Daily Appointments of Harry S. Truman &#124; Harry S. Truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman LibraryDaily Appointments of Harry S. Truman &#124; Harry S. TrumanSeptember 23, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 23, 1947</span></span></span>

Yet chronology is not authentication. Genuine records document the institutional upheaval around 24 September in considerable detail without documenting an MJ-12 organisation. The same historical setting that makes the alleged order look well placed also supplies records against which its claim can be tested.

## Forrestal takes charge of the new defence structure

The reorganisation had been months in preparation. Truman signed the National Security Act on **26 July 1947**, establishing a new framework that included a Secretary of Defense, a National Military Establishment, the NSC and the CIA, while also creating a separate Department of the [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/458989/1947-the-national-security-act-of-1947/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[U.S. Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1947 the national security act of 1947</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">U.S. Air Force History1947 -- The National Security Act of 1947 &gt; Air Force Historical Support Division &gt; Fact Sheets...</span></span></span> The crucial point for the MJ-12 chronology, however, is that much of this machinery moved from legislation to operating institutions in mid-September.

On **17 September**, James V. Forrestal was sworn in as the country's first Secretary of Defense. The Defense Department's historical office notes that Truman actually brought the ceremony forward because of concern that Yugoslavia might move against the disputed city of Trieste, where American and British troops were stationed. Forrestal therefore entered the new post in an atmosphere of immediate international-security pressure rather than as part of a leisurely administrative transition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.defense.gov/Multimedia/Biographies/Article-View/Article/571293/james-v-forrestal/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.defense.gov">[Defense Historical Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Historical Office James V. Forrestal &gt; Historical Office &gt; Article View</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Historical OfficeJames V. Forrestal &gt; Historical Office &gt; Article View...</span></span></span>

The following day brought another burst of institutional change. A Defense Department history records that the National Security Act took complete effect on **18 September**: Kenneth Royall became Secretary of the Army, John Sullivan became Secretary of the Navy and W. Stuart Symington was installed as the first Secretary of the Air Force.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.defense.gov/Portals/70/Documents/other/DODDocsEstandOrg1944-1978.pdf?ver=W5zalSjBJPck3AYUP7jqNQ%3D%3D" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.defense.gov">[Defense Historical Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DODDocsEstandOrg1944 1978</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Historical OfficeTHE DEPARTMENT OF DEFENSESeptember 25, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 25, 2009</span></span></span> Air Force historical records likewise date the establishment of the Department of the Air Force to 18 September.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afhistory.af.mil/FAQs/Fact-Sheets/Article/458985/evolution-of-the-department-of-the-air-force/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afhistory.af.mil">[U.S. Air Force History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

That makes the alleged 24 September directive's addressee particularly well chosen. A memorandum addressed to Forrestal as **Secretary of Defense** could not plausibly have been dated much earlier: he had held that office for only seven days. According to the alleged Truman memorandum, Forrestal was authorised to proceed with an undertaking thereafter called “Operation Majestic Twelve”; the purported 1952 Eisenhower briefing then claimed that Truman had established the group on 24 September on the recommendation of Forrestal and Bush.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

The fit is impressive at the level of institutional vocabulary. But it is important not to reverse the logic. Forrestal's genuine appointment and the genuine creation of the new defence establishment prove that **24 September was an administratively plausible date for a presidential national-security decision**. They do not prove that this particular decision occurred.

## The CIA enters its new statutory role

The intelligence side of the reorganisation makes the timing look still more carefully aligned. Before the National Security Act, Truman already had a central intelligence apparatus: the Central Intelligence Group and National Intelligence Authority had been created under presidential authority in 1946. The 1947 Act placed central intelligence on a permanent statutory footing, creating the CIA under the new national-security structure and transferring functions from its predecessors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp89b00552r000400100002-3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cia rdp89b00552r000400100002 3</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">cia rdp89b00552r000400100002 3</span></span></span>

Official chronologies differ slightly in describing the mechanics of the transition. The CIA marks **18 September 1947** as the date on which the agency officially came into existence, while a State Department documentary history describes the Central Intelligence Group becoming the CIA on 19 September and Rear Admiral Roscoe [Hillenkoetter]({{ 'hillenkoetter/' | relative_url }}) becoming the new agency's first Director of Central Intelligence on 20 September. A contemporary CIA organisational history, meanwhile, tied full operation of the statutory provisions to Hillenkoetter's swearing-in on 26 September.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-the-national-security-act-of-1947/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[cia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ask molly the national security act of 1947</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ask Molly: The National Security Act of 1947 - CIA...</span></span></span> These differences concern the steps of implementation, not the larger point: **the week containing 24 September was the CIA's transition week**.

That detail gives one sentence in the alleged Truman memorandum particular significance. The document says that future decisions should remain with the presidency after discussions with Forrestal, Bush and the **Director of Central Intelligence**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span> In historical terms, that combination is plausible for late September 1947: defence, scientific research and central intelligence really were components of the emerging post-war security system.

There is also a deeper reason the date works narratively. The CIA was not simply a renamed wartime spy service. The National Security Act charged it with coordinating intelligence activities and correlating, evaluating and disseminating intelligence affecting national security. The statutory structure placed the agency in relation to the NSC, giving the administration a new mechanism for bringing intelligence into high-level policy formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/legacy/cia-history/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">History of CIA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">History of CIA</span></span></span>

For an alleged document describing an extraordinary secret programme reporting upwards towards the presidency, late September 1947 is therefore a much more convincing setting than an arbitrary date earlier in the summer. The bureaucratic architecture required by the story was visibly taking shape.

But the distinction between **plausible architecture** and **documented programme** remains decisive. The National Archives says that searches of Truman- and Eisenhower-era NSC indexes under terms including “MJ-12”, “majestic”, “UFO”, “flying saucers” and “extraterrestrial biological entities” produced no corresponding Truman-era MJ-12 record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span></span></span>

## The genuine 24 September meeting

The most striking coincidence occurs on the alleged authorisation date itself. Truman's official appointment calendar records an **11:30 a.m. meeting on Wednesday, 24 September 1947, with Secretary of Defense James Forrestal and Dr Vannevar Bush**. The appointment was arranged at Forrestal's suggestion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_k=&amp;page=75" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Truman Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Library Daily Appointments of Harry S. Truman &#124; Harry S. Truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman LibraryDaily Appointments of Harry S. Truman &#124; Harry S. TrumanSeptember 23, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 23, 1947</span></span></span>

This is unusually relevant because those are exactly the three central figures required by the alleged foundation story. The purported Eisenhower briefing says that Truman created MJ-12 on the recommendation of Bush and Forrestal, while the purported Truman memorandum refers back to a “recent conversation” and directs that later decisions involve Forrestal, Bush and the Director of Central Intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

The calendar therefore establishes something narrow but important: **the alleged document's principals really could have spoken face to face that morning**. Forrestal was newly installed in the defence post attributed to him by the memorandum, Bush was present, and Truman was available to make a presidential decision.

What the diary does not establish is the subject of their conversation. The entry gives the attendees and scheduling information but contains no reference to Majestic Twelve, Roswell, recovered aircraft, UFOs or an extraordinary research organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_k=&amp;page=75" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Truman Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Library Daily Appointments of Harry S. Truman &#124; Harry S. Truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman LibraryDaily Appointments of Harry S. Truman &#124; Harry S. TrumanSeptember 23, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 23, 1947</span></span></span> The evidential gap matters because a calendar match can demonstrate opportunity without demonstrating action.

This is also where provenance becomes more important than chronological cleverness. A document created later can be made more persuasive by matching publicly recoverable dates, offices and meetings. Conversely, if an independently archived 1947 record had identified an MJ-12 decision arising from the Forrestal-Bush meeting, that would be qualitatively stronger evidence. The surviving official record presently supplies the first kind of match, not the second.

## The National Security Council meets two days later

The final piece of the remarkable week came on **26 September 1947**, when Truman presided over the first meeting of the National Security Council at the [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}). The surviving official minutes identify Forrestal, Acting Secretary of State Robert Lovett, the secretaries of the Army, Navy and Air Force, National Security Resources Board chairman Arthur Hill, NSC Executive Secretary Sidney Souers and Director of Central Intelligence Roscoe Hillenkoetter among those present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the Historian...</span></span></span>

This was not merely a ceremonial gathering. The council approved its operating policies and procedures and began establishing how the new national-security machinery would function.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the Historian...</span></span></span> CIA historical documentation also notes that the first issue of its *Review of the World Situation as it Relates to the Security of the United States*, designated “CIA 1”, was prepared for this inaugural NSC meeting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov//historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d369fn0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span></span></span>

Seen purely as historical staging, the alleged order lands almost perfectly. It appears between the activation of the post-war security apparatus and its first major presidential council meeting. Several figures later named in the MJ-12 story occupied genuine positions within, or adjacent to, this emerging system.

Yet the genuine minutes of the 26 September meeting create an important control against overinterpreting that fit. They survive as an official NSC record, identify participants and decisions, and are traceable to National Archives records. The surviving minutes do not record an MJ-12 organisation or an extraterrestrial-recovery programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the Historian...</span></span></span> That absence cannot by itself disprove a compartmented secret activity, but it prevents the inaugural NSC meeting from serving as affirmative evidence for MJ-12.

## Why the timing is persuasive but not proof

The strongest case for taking the 24 September date seriously is therefore also narrower than it first appears. The alleged documents successfully inhabit the real institutional landscape of September 1947. Forrestal had just acquired the correct title. The CIA was being established under the new statute. Bush and Forrestal genuinely met Truman on the stated date. The NSC genuinely assembled two days afterwards. Those are meaningful chronological correspondences, not invented background.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.defense.gov/Multimedia/Biographies/Article-View/Article/571293/james-v-forrestal/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.defense.gov">[defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Historical Office James V. Forrestal &gt; Historical Office &gt; Article View</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Defense Historical OfficeJames V. Forrestal &gt; Historical Office &gt; Article View...</span></span></span>

But the historical record distinguishes **contextual accuracy** from **documentary authenticity**. Official archives contain abundant evidence for the surrounding reorganisation: appointments, organisational histories, presidential schedules, intelligence records and NSC minutes. The National Archives nevertheless reports negative searches for MJ-12 terminology in the relevant NSC holdings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span></span></span> The FBI's later handling of the circulated MJ-12 material points in the same direction: its Vault describes the purported Eisenhower briefing as a document that an Air Force investigation determined to be fake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=Majestic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span></span></span>

That leaves September 1947 with a double significance. For supporters of the MJ-12 papers, it supplies an unusually dense set of real-world correspondences around the alleged authorisation. For sceptical analysis, those correspondences demonstrate only that the story is **chronologically well fitted to a genuine bureaucratic transformation**.

The distinction is essential. Had the alleged authorisation placed Forrestal in the Secretary of Defense role before 17 September, referred to a functioning NSC months before its creation, or put the CIA into an impossible institutional setting, the chronology could have helped falsify it immediately. Instead, the document passes that basic plausibility test. What it does not acquire from passing it is independent proof that Truman created Majestic-12.

In that sense, **24 September 1947 may be one of the strongest features of the alleged MJ-12 narrative without being evidence sufficient to authenticate it**. The date lies exactly where a secret presidential national-security initiative could plausibly be imagined: after America's new defence and intelligence machinery began operating, during a documented Truman-Forrestal-Bush encounter, and immediately before the first NSC meeting. The authenticated record confirms the extraordinary week. It does not confirm the extraordinary organisation allegedly created within it.
