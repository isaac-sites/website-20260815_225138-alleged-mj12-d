## Introduction

Vannevar Bush is one of the more plausible names on the alleged Majestic-12 roster. In September 1947 he was already one of America’s most experienced organisers of military science, and he was moving into a newly created senior defence-research post. Yet the exact dates matter. The alleged MJ-12 briefing says the group was established on **24 September 1947** on the recommendation of Bush and Secretary of Defense James [Forrestal]({{ 'forrestal/' | relative_url }}). Bush was not formally sworn in as chairman of the new Research and Development Board until **30 September**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[wikisource.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

That six-day gap is a useful chronology check, but it is easy to overstate. Bush was already chairing the predecessor Joint Research and Development Board, and, importantly, Truman’s appointment diary records Bush and Forrestal meeting the President together on 24 September. The dates therefore expose a distinction between **plausibility** and **authentication**: Bush was exceptionally well placed to advise Truman on secret scientific matters, but his real career does not independently establish MJ-12.

## Bush's real defence-science role

Bush was not simply a prominent civilian scientist whose name happened to fit a UFO story. During the Second World War he had directed the Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD), chaired the National Defense Research Committee and occupied positions connecting civilian research directly to military requirements. His wartime responsibilities reached the Manhattan Project and other highly sensitive weapons programmes. The National Park Service describes his central role in coordinating defence research and his involvement in the wartime atomic-bomb programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://home.nps.gov/people/manhattan-project-leaders-vannevar-bush.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: home.nps.gov">[National Park Service]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">home.nps.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Park ServiceManhattan Project Leaders: Vannevar Bush (U.S. National Park Service)February 10, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2024</span></span></span>

That work continued after the war. An official US [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) history records that the Joint Research and Development Board (JRDB) was chartered on 6 June 1946 with Bush as chairman. Among other things, its committees dealt with guided missiles and the difficult problem of coordinating competing Army and Navy research programmes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Sep/24/2001329775/-1/-1/0/AFD-100924-024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[U.S. Department of War]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GENERAL HIS~O~ES Jacob Neufeld OFFICE OF AIR FOAugust 20, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 20, 2024</span></span></span>(https://media.defense.gov/2010/Sep/24/2001329775/-1/-1/0/AFD-100924-024.pdf) The National Academy of Sciences likewise records Bush as chairman of the War and Navy departments' Joint Research and Development Board in 1946–47 and then chairman of the National Military Establishment's Research and Development Board in 1947–48.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/bush-vannevar.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasonline.org">[NAS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasonline.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bush vannevar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">bush vannevar</span></span></span>

The National Security Act of 1947 reorganised this machinery. An official Defense Department history explains that the new Research and Development Board was to advise the Secretary of Defense on scientific research relevant to national security and help ensure adequate research and development on military scientific problems. Forrestal wanted continuity and asked Bush to remain at the head of the reorganised body. Bush initially hesitated, partly because of disagreements with the [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) over science policy, but Truman persuaded him to accept.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D-PURL-gpo58431/pdf/GOVPUB-D-PURL-gpo58431.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D PURL gpo58431</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE FORMATIVE YEARS 1947-1950 Problems abroad thJuly 3, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 3, 2025</span></span></span>

This makes Bush a particularly effective name in the alleged MJ-12 narrative. A hypothetical secret problem involving unfamiliar aircraft, propulsion, materials or other advanced technology would naturally seem to require someone with his mixture of scientific authority, military connections and experience with compartmented wartime research. His presence on the claimed roster is therefore historically believable in a general sense.

But that observation cuts both ways. Bush's qualifications were not secret. His wartime prominence and his post-war defence-science appointments were matters of public and institutional record. Contemporary *Chemical & Engineering News*, for example, reported in October 1947 that Truman had appointed Bush chairman of the newly created Research and Development Board.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/cen-v025n040.p2875" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubs.acs.org">[ACS Publications]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubs.acs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Publications Potomac Postscripts &#124; C&amp;EN Global Enterprise</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACS PublicationsPotomac Postscripts &#124; C&amp;EN Global EnterpriseOctober 6, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 6, 1947</span></span></span> A convincing retrospective document could therefore have selected Bush precisely because his known career made the choice look right.

## The September 24–30 chronology

The most revealing way to examine the claim is to put the relevant dates side by side.

**24 September 1947:** According to the alleged MJ-12 material, Truman established the Majestic-12 group on this date, following a recommendation by Bush and Forrestal. The purported Truman-to-Forrestal memorandum also says future decisions should follow discussions with Forrestal, Bush and the Director of Central Intelligence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

Something real and potentially important did happen that day: **Bush and Forrestal actually met Truman**. The Truman Library's presidential appointment calendar records Forrestal and Bush together with the President at 11.30 am on 24 September, with the meeting arranged at Forrestal's suggestion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_keywords=forrestal&amp;page=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span> This is one of the strongest pieces of circumstantial chronology favouring the *plausibility* of the alleged memorandum. The supposed principal actors were not merely in Washington at the same time; the official diary places them together with Truman on the very date appearing on the disputed document.

Yet the diary does not say that they discussed UFOs, recovered spacecraft or Majestic-12. A recorded meeting establishes opportunity, not subject matter. Given Forrestal's role in constructing the new National Military Establishment and Bush's pending position in its research structure, they had an obvious documented reason to discuss defence organisation.

**25 September:** Truman publicly wrote to Bush about his appointment as chairman of the new Research and Development Board. The letter is unusually useful because it explicitly describes the institutional transition. Truman told Bush that he was pleased Bush would continue in public service as chairman of the board created by the National Security Act and noted that, since the end of the war, Bush had been serving as chairman of the earlier Joint Research and Development Board.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/public-papers/192/letter-dr-vannevar-bush-upon-his-appointment-chairman-research-and" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

This means that on 24 September Bush was not an outsider waiting to enter defence work. He was the incumbent head of the predecessor research organisation and was in the process of moving into the successor board. Calling the six-day difference a simple case of “Bush had no defence-science position yet” would therefore be misleading.

**26 September:** Truman's official calendar records the first meeting of the National Security Council with the President. Forrestal, Director of Central Intelligence Roscoe [Hillenkoetter]({{ 'hillenkoetter/' | relative_url }}) and Sidney Souers were among those present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_keywords=forrestal&amp;page=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span> That gathering illustrates how rapidly the machinery created by the National Security Act was becoming operational during precisely the week in question.

**30 September:** Bush was formally sworn in as chairman of the Research and Development Board. The Truman Library preserves a photograph record identifying both the ceremony and the date, while Defense Department chronologies give his tenure as 30 September 1947 to 14 October 1948.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/photograph-records/97-1887" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

The resulting chronology is therefore more nuanced than a single apparent discrepancy suggests:

**24 September:** Bush and Forrestal meet Truman; alleged [MJ-12 creation]({{ 'mj-12-creation/' | relative_url }}) date.

**25 September:** Truman's documented appointment letter to Bush.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/public-papers/192/letter-dr-vannevar-bush-upon-his-appointment-chairman-research-and" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[trumanlibrary.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

**26 September:** the new National Security Council meets with Truman.

**30 September:** Bush is formally sworn in as chairman of the new Research and Development Board.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_keywords=forrestal&amp;page=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

The six-day gap is real, but it is a gap between the alleged MJ-12 date and Bush's **formal assumption of the new RDB chairmanship**, not between the alleged date and Bush's involvement in high-level defence science.

## Why the mismatch is still worth noticing

The chronology matters most when later descriptions implicitly give Bush's 30 September status to events dated 24 September. If Bush is described as already heading the *new* Research and Development Board when MJ-12 was supposedly established, that is technically premature. The Defense Department's own historical chronology starts his chairmanship on 30 September.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D-PURL-LPS66624/pdf/GOVPUB-D-PURL-LPS66624.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D PURL LPS66624</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOVPUB D PURL LPS66624</span></span></span>

On the other hand, the original alleged MJ-12 briefing avoids depending heavily on that mistake. Its roster simply names “Dr. Vannevar Bush”, while its account says that MJ-12 was established on Bush and Forrestal's recommendation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span> Bush could plainly have made recommendations to Truman before being sworn into the new board, particularly because he already chaired its predecessor.

There is also evidence that his formal appointment was not an automatic or long-settled formality. The Defense Department's history says Bush hesitated before accepting and was persuaded by Truman; a contemporary October 1947 report noted that Washington observers had previously wondered whether disagreements between Bush and the President might prevent the appointment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D-PURL-gpo58431/pdf/GOVPUB-D-PURL-gpo58431.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D PURL gpo58431</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE FORMATIVE YEARS 1947-1950 Problems abroad thJuly 3, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 3, 2025</span></span></span> That makes precise titles and dates more significant when evaluating a purportedly contemporary document. A document genuinely produced during the transition should reflect what officials actually knew and called their institutions at that moment, rather than smoothing the sequence into the tidier version visible in hindsight.

There is a further twist. The Bush chronology intersects with one of the separate documentary objections raised against the alleged Truman memorandum itself. Philip J. Klass argued in *Skeptical Inquirer* that Truman's signature on the disputed 24 September MJ-12 memorandum matched a signature on an authentic Truman letter to Bush dated 1 October 1947, including distinctive incidental features, suggesting photographic copying rather than two independent signatures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer SKEPTICAL</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">INQUIRER Vol. 14 No. 2/Winter 1990 $6June 27, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2006</span></span></span> MJ-12 proponents have disputed the force of this and other forensic objections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span></span></span> The point should therefore be distinguished from the six-day appointment issue: one concerns Bush's institutional chronology; the other concerns the physical or photographic history of the disputed document.

## What the date mismatch can and cannot prove

The September chronology does **not**, by itself, prove that the MJ-12 papers are forged. Bush was already running the Joint Research and Development Board, was deeply embedded in classified defence science, and demonstrably met Forrestal and Truman on 24 September. Those facts make it entirely possible, in ordinary administrative terms, that he could have participated in a sensitive presidential discussion before his 30 September swearing-in.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://media.defense.gov/2010/Sep/24/2001329775/-1/-1/0/AFD-100924-024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: media.defense.gov">[defense.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">media.defense.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">U.S. Department of War</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GENERAL HIS~O~ES Jacob Neufeld OFFICE OF AIR FOAugust 20, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 20, 2024</span></span></span>

Nor does the 24 September meeting prove the MJ-12 story. The Truman Library calendar supplies the participants and time, not an extraterrestrial agenda. The following day's presidential letter supplies a straightforward documented context: Bush's transition from the joint War-Navy research board to the Research and Development Board created under the new defence structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_keywords=forrestal&amp;page=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

That distinction is particularly important because the broader archival record does not authenticate Majestic-12. The National Archives says extensive searches of Air Force, Joint Chiefs, Truman and Eisenhower [presidential-library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}), and National Security Council records failed to locate the expected MJ-12 documentation, apart from the controversial 1954 Cutler-[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum; it also lists archival anomalies surrounding that item.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span></span></span> The FBI says that when it examined the supposed 1952 Majestic-12 briefing, an Air Force investigation determined it was fake; the Bureau's released file contains the blunt internal conclusion that the document was “BOGUS”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/unexplained-phenomenon/search" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span></span></span>

Bush's dates therefore provide a useful but limited test. **His biography fits the role extremely well; his formal new appointment comes six days too late to be used casually as his title on 24 September; and his documented meeting with Truman and Forrestal on that exact day prevents the discrepancy from becoming a simple chronological disproof.** The safest conclusion is narrower. The real Vannevar Bush explains why the claimed MJ-12 roster can feel historically convincing, while the September 1947 paper trail shows why historical plausibility must not be mistaken for evidence that the secret committee itself existed.
