## Introduction

[Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }})’s position on [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) was much more selective than the label “MJ-12 believer” suggests. He defended three papers as the original core — the 1952 Eisenhower Briefing Document (EBD), the 1947 Truman–Forrestal memorandum (TFM) and the 1954 Cutler–Twining memorandum (CTM) — and later judged the separate SOM1-01 operations manual “very likely genuine”. Yet he concluded that almost all of the much larger body of papers received by researcher Timothy Cooper were fraudulent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

That split verdict is important because Friedman did not treat apparent membership in the MJ-12 archive as evidence of authenticity. When later documents contained convincing signatures, official-looking formats or information compatible with the MJ-12 story, he still compared them against genuine archival records. In several Cooper cases, that comparison produced something much stronger than a vague suspicion of forgery: Friedman found earlier government documents whose wording and handwritten material had apparently been reused to manufacture the purported MJ-12 versions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

## The three papers Friedman kept at the core

Friedman explicitly distinguished what he called the “[original documents]({{ 'original-document/' | relative_url }})” from later material. His core consisted of the **Eisenhower Briefing Document**, dated 18 November 1952; the **Truman–Forrestal memorandum**, dated 24 September 1947; and the **Cutler–Twining memorandum**, dated 14 July 1954. The first two became public through photographs from film anonymously received by Jaime Shandera, while the [Cutler–Twining paper]({{ 'paper-test/' | relative_url }}) was found by Shandera and William Moore in Record Group 341 at the US National Archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

By 2004, after years of controversy, Friedman described those three as “almost certainly genuine”. That was his assessment, not an official authentication. The National Archives reached no such conclusion about Cutler–Twining and stresses that certifying a copy as a document in its [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}) does not authenticate either the document itself or its contents. Its investigation identified anomalies surrounding the memorandum and found no corroborating MJ-12 documentation in the relevant government holdings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Friedman nevertheless believed that details in the core documents survived challenges that should have exposed a careless modern forgery. He argued, for example, that subsequent archival research revealed information consistent with details in the EBD and CTM, including Nathan Twining's travel to New Mexico beginning on 7 July 1947. He also cited period documents to challenge critics who argued that certain classification practices or document conventions made the papers impossible for their stated dates. These arguments remained disputed, but they explain why Friedman did not abandon the core when he later identified demonstrably bad material elsewhere in the MJ-12 corpus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The distinction matters. Friedman's reasoning was document-specific: finding fraudulent later papers did not, in his view, logically establish that earlier papers from a different route were fraudulent. In a 1999 exchange concerning one Cooper document, he explicitly warned against either “guilt or genuineness by association”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/marshall.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FRAUDULENT 27-SEPTEMBER-1944 &quot;MJ-12&quot; DOCUMENT...</span></span></span>

## Why SOM1-01 and the Cooper papers became separate cases

Friedman also separated the core documents from two later streams. In 1994, Don Berliner received another anonymous roll of film containing the purported **SOM1-01 Special Operations Manual**, while Timothy Cooper accumulated numerous supposed MJ-12 records through mail, his post-office box and claimed sources. Friedman noted that there appeared to be no connection between the original trio, SOM1-01 and Cooper's material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

That separation produced a three-tier verdict rather than a simple authentic-or-fake judgement. In Friedman's 2004 summary, the original trio were “almost certainly genuine”, SOM1-01 was “very likely genuine”, while the Cooper documents were “emulations and fictional”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

SOM1-01 therefore occupied an intermediate position. It had appeared independently of Cooper and in a different physical form — photographed pages on 35 mm film — so Friedman did not allow his findings about Cooper's papers to dispose of it automatically. That did not give the manual a verified government provenance: its film was anonymously mailed, and no authenticated government original established an official chain of custody. Even sources defending SOM1-01 acknowledge that provenance is a central objection to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/source/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on specialoperationsmanual.com.</span></span></span>

Friedman's willingness to retain SOM1-01 while rejecting Cooper's papers is especially revealing. His categories were not simply chronological — “early good, late bad” — because SOM1-01 was itself a late arrival. Nor was he accepting everything that reinforced his preferred interpretation of MJ-12. He regarded the evidential histories of the documents as separable and believed SOM1-01 survived scrutiny better than the Cooper material.

## The Cooper papers failed a much harder test

The decisive feature of Friedman's rejection of the Cooper material was the discovery of what he called **“emulations”**: purported secret documents apparently constructed by retyping genuine historical records, altering selected passages to introduce MJ-12 material, and reproducing signatures or handwritten annotations from the originals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

One early warning involved a purported February 1948 memorandum from Admiral Roscoe [Hillenkoetter]({{ 'hillenkoetter/' | relative_url }}) to President Truman discussing large quantities of “MAJIC” material. Friedman thought its language sounded suspiciously like wartime correspondence about **MAGIC**, the Allied intelligence derived from intercepted Japanese communications. He subsequently found the genuine Marshall-to-Roosevelt document in a published collection, *The American MAGIC*, confirming the relationship that had made him suspicious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Another particularly strong example involved a supposed Marshall letter dated 27 September 1947. Friedman asked Larry Bland, an archivist and editor of the George C. Marshall papers, to examine it. Bland recognised it as a reworking of a genuine 1944 Marshall letter concerning the danger that political debate could expose US success in reading Japanese codes. The purported MJ-12 version retained much of the structure and wording but substituted material about “MAJIC” and New Mexico. Friedman subsequently said he “completely accepted” Bland's conclusion and regarded the document as a parody or fabrication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/marshall.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Cufon]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FRAUDULENT 27-SEPTEMBER-1944 &quot;MJ-12&quot; DOCUMENT...</span></span></span>

That example was damaging precisely because the forged document could look historically persuasive in isolation. A creator working from a genuine period document automatically inherited plausible formatting, vocabulary and institutional style. Friedman realised that apparently authentic features could therefore be artefacts of the source document rather than independent evidence that the MJ-12 paper was genuine.

## The Truman–Twining directive exposed the emulation method

The supposed 9 July 1947 presidential directive to General Nathan Twining provided an even clearer demonstration. Friedman compared it with a genuine Truman directive to General Albert Wedemeyer concerning Wedemeyer's mission to China and Korea. The alleged MJ-12 document reproduced not only language and layout but handwritten elements from the genuine document. Friedman described the placement and handwriting as matching closely enough to show how the later paper had been assembled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The substitutions also generated contextual mistakes. The real directive sensibly told Wedemeyer that after completing work in China he should go to Korea. Its supposed MJ-12 counterpart transformed this into an instruction to Twining that after finishing in New Mexico he should go to Sandia — although Sandia was itself in New Mexico. Other language suited a diplomatic fact-finding mission in politically unstable China but made little sense when transplanted into a supposed investigation of recovered extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The problem propagated into additional Cooper papers. Friedman observed that passages derived from the suspect Wedemeyer emulation appeared again in a supposed MJ-12 annual report. In other words, one manufactured document could be made to “corroborate” another because both incorporated material from the same derivative textual family.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

That finding changes how internal consistency should be interpreted. Several documents repeating the same terminology, facts or phrasing may look mutually reinforcing, but if they descend from a common fabricated source, their agreement supplies no independent confirmation at all.

## Archival checks produced external contradictions

Friedman did not rely only on textual resemblance. He tested some Cooper claims against records that could independently establish where the named officials actually were.

One Cooper document placed General Carl Spaatz with Twining in New Mexico on 7 July 1947. Friedman checked Spaatz's flight log and desk calendar as well as a contemporary newspaper account. Those records placed Spaatz fishing at Port Aransas, Texas, hundreds of miles away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

He likewise used Twining's flight information to test the chronology embedded in other purported records. A supposed Twining report implied circumstances inconsistent with the documented duration of Twining's July trip. Friedman also identified elementary technical mistakes, including one Cooper document describing deuterium as “light hydrogen”; deuterium is the heavy isotope of hydrogen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

These were stronger objections than complaints that a typeface, phrase or bureaucratic convention merely “looked wrong”. Friedman's method at its best asked whether a claim could survive comparison with an independently recoverable historical record. The Spaatz example could be checked against several records; the emulation examples could be placed beside their genuine textual ancestors.

## What Friedman's split verdict says about authentication

Friedman's treatment of the competing MJ-12 documents demonstrates a useful point even for readers who reject his positive judgement of the original trio. **Authenticity cannot reliably be assigned to the alleged MJ-12 archive as a whole.** Documents sharing the same extraordinary story can have different provenance, different physical histories and radically different evidential problems.

His Cooper investigation also shows why official-looking details deserve limited weight when provenance is weak. A forged document built from genuine historical material can possess the correct signature, period vocabulary, bureaucratic phrasing and even authentic handwritten annotations. In the Cooper examples Friedman identified, those strengths became evidence of fabrication once the underlying donor documents were found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

There is an important limitation, however. Friedman's successful exposure of Cooper material does **not** authenticate the documents he continued to defend. The same document-by-document principle cuts both ways. Showing that one alleged MJ-12 paper is an emulation establishes that paper's weakness; showing that another lacks the same identifiable emulation does not establish government authorship. The National Archives' position on Cutler–Twining illustrates the gap: custody of the paper is real, but the Archives explicitly refuses to equate custody with authentication, and its searches failed to uncover supporting MJ-12 records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives</span></span></span>

Friedman's split verdict is therefore most significant as a case study in **discriminating among competing claims**, rather than as proof of MJ-12 itself. He was a committed defender of the original documents, yet his own research supplied some of the clearest arguments against later additions to the alleged archive. By 2004 his position was unusually explicit: three original papers accepted, SOM1-01 provisionally accepted at a lower level of confidence, and the Cooper corpus overwhelmingly rejected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

That makes his verdict a caution against a common shortcut in the MJ-12 debate. Neither “some are fake, therefore all are fake” nor “some look convincing, therefore the archive is authentic” follows from the evidence. Friedman's own work on the Cooper papers showed how easily a collection can acquire impressive-looking documents that collapse once their wording, handwriting, chronology and claimed events are checked against independent historical records. The unresolved question surrounding the documents he continued to defend must therefore be judged on their own provenance and evidence — exactly the document-by-document standard that led him to reject so much of the material offered in MJ-12's name.
