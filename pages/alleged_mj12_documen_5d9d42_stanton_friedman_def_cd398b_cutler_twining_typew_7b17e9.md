## Introduction

The typewriter argument against the 14 July 1954 Cutler-Twining memorandum did **not** hold up in its strongest form. UFO sceptic Philip J. Klass argued that the memorandum's relatively large Pica type was anomalous because authentic [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) and National Security Council material associated with Robert Cutler and James S. Lay used the smaller Elite type. [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}) answered by finding authentic period documents from the same offices using Pica. Most importantly, Klass himself later acknowledged receiving more than a dozen qualifying examples and paid Friedman the $1,000 maximum under his challenge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2022/09/26195532/SUN-72-Summer-2002.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">KEPTICS UFO NEWSLETTER By Philip J. Klass · ·July 27, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2013</span></span></span>

## Why Pica looked suspicious

The dispute arose from a mundane but potentially powerful form of document testing. In the typewriter era, **Pica** generally meant larger type producing roughly ten characters per inch, whereas **Elite** was smaller, commonly around twelve characters per inch. If a purported 1954 memorandum used a type style that its supposed originating office did not use, that could provide a straightforward reason to suspect fabrication.

Klass believed he had found just such a discrepancy. In correspondence dated 16 January 1989, he told Friedman that examples he had examined from the Cutler and Lay offices showed the smaller Elite type, while the alleged Cutler-[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum used larger Pica. He therefore challenged Friedman to produce authentic White House business letters or memoranda from Cutler or Lay during the relevant period using type identical in size and style to that of the disputed memorandum. Klass offered $100 for each qualifying example, capped at $1,000. A surviving copy of that correspondence makes the terms of the challenge unusually clear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TYPEWRITER-STYLES-MJ12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>

The logic was initially attractive because it seemed testable. It did not require accepting or rejecting stories about UFOs, secrecy or witnesses. If the Cutler and Lay offices really used Elite exclusively, or nearly so, then Pica on an alleged memorandum from that bureaucratic setting would be conspicuous.

The weakness was the sample behind the generalisation. Friedman maintained that Klass had examined only a small number of relevant NSC documents before extrapolating from them. Friedman later characterised the argument as effectively treating nine Elite examples as evidence that NSC material should always have been Elite. Whether or not that description captures every nuance of Klass's original reasoning, Klass's subsequent concession establishes the essential point: **Elite was not the only type in use.**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

## The archival examples that changed the argument

Friedman's answer was to search historical records rather than debate what a White House office “normally” ought to have done. He said he already possessed examples of Pica material and then found additional documents during research at the Dwight D. Eisenhower [Presidential Library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}). His later accounts describe sending Klass numerous examples, including fourteen additional Pica memoranda associated with James Lay's NSC office.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/122965834-top-secret-majic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: goodreads.com">[Goodreads]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">goodreads.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Top Secret/Majic: Operation Majestic-12 and the United States Government&#x27;s UFO Cover-up by Stanton T. Friedman &#124; GoodreadsJuly 1...</span></span></span>

The strongest confirmation does not depend solely on Friedman's recollection. Klass himself revisited the episode in his *Skeptics UFO Newsletter* in 2002. He wrote that Friedman had promptly responded with photocopies of more than a dozen Cutler-office documents produced with larger Pica type and that he sent Friedman the promised $1,000. In other words, the critic who formulated the test accepted that Friedman had met it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2022/09/26195532/SUN-72-Summer-2002.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">KEPTICS UFO NEWSLETTER By Philip J. Klass · ·July 27, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2013</span></span></span>

That makes the typewriter episode more significant than the familiar shorthand that “Friedman won a bet”. Friedman himself objected to calling it a bet: it was a challenge proposed by Klass with specified documentary conditions and a maximum payment. The surviving correspondence supports that distinction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CUTLER TWINNING MEMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project AquariusMSI PATCH SHEETMay 7, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 7, 2026</span></span></span>

Friedman's archival work also went beyond merely establishing that *some* Pica machines existed. Notes preserved with his research compare individual characteristics of NSC documents from June and July 1954. He distinguished several apparent typewriters by such features as the shape of the numerals **4** and **5** and the alignment of the letter **E**. In those notes, he judged the Cutler-Twining memorandum most similar to what he labelled “Typewriter C”, one of several machines apparently represented in the comparative NSC material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CUTLER TWINNING MEMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project AquariusMSI PATCH SHEETMay 7, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 7, 2026</span></span></span>

That is an important refinement. The rebuttal was not simply, “Pica existed in 1954”. Nobody seriously needed that demonstrated. The relevant question was whether **Pica appeared in authentic documents generated by the offices and personnel against which the Cutler-Twining memorandum was being compared**. Friedman's examples showed that it did.

A 1987 *MUFON UFO Journal* discussion reported a preliminary assessment that the Cutler-Twining typeface was consistent with a period manual Remington-Rand machine. ## What Friedman's result actually proved

Friedman's success substantially weakened one particular forgery argument. The inference

**Cutler/Lay material used Elite → the Cutler-Twining memo uses Pica → therefore the memo is fraudulent**

could no longer stand once genuine Cutler/Lay material in Pica had been produced.

Klass's own later account effectively conceded that much. He continued to regard the Cutler-Twining memorandum and the wider MJ-12 story as fraudulent, but his 2002 description did not try to preserve the proposition that authentic Cutler-office documents were confined to Elite. Instead, he openly reported paying Friedman after receiving the Pica examples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2022/09/26195532/SUN-72-Summer-2002.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">KEPTICS UFO NEWSLETTER By Philip J. Klass · ·July 27, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2013</span></span></span>

This is a useful case study in historical document criticism because **normal practice is not the same thing as exclusive practice**. An office might favour one typewriter, format or secretary while still employing other machines. Friedman's comparative notes even suggested multiple typewriters being used on NSC material during the same narrow period, with identifiable variations among their characters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CUTLER TWINNING MEMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project AquariusMSI PATCH SHEETMay 7, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 7, 2026</span></span></span>

The episode therefore strengthened Friedman's broader defence of the core MJ-12 documents in a limited but genuine way. He had taken an apparently objective anomaly proposed by a prominent critic, looked for counterexamples in archival holdings and found enough that the critic honoured his challenge. That makes it difficult to continue presenting the mere presence of Pica type as evidence that a 1954 Cutler/Lay or NSC-related memorandum was technologically or bureaucratically impossible.

But there is an equally important logical limit. Showing that a genuine office **could have produced** a document with this type does not demonstrate that the office **did produce this particular document**. A competent forger using an appropriate period typewriter could pass exactly the same test.

## Why the memo remained problematic

The National Archives' objections demonstrate why the Pica result cannot carry the authentication argument by itself. The Cutler-Twining memorandum was reportedly discovered in Record Group 341, entry 267, but the Archives notes that the series was filed by Top Secret [register number]({{ 'register-number/' | relative_url }}) and that this document lacked one. It was found in folder T4-1846 without any other document referring to “NSC/MJ-12”. Searches by archival staff in records of the Secretary of Defense, Joint Chiefs of Staff, Headquarters US [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) and other relevant files produced no further documentation of the alleged project; inquiries to the Air Force, Joint Chiefs and National Security Council were likewise unsuccessful.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Those are provenance and corroboration problems, not typography problems. The National Archives does not treat the fact that the memorandum physically appeared in its holdings as proof that it originated with the government office named on it. Its published MJ-12 reference report continues to describe the Cutler-Twining document as posing multiple problems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

There were also reasons why some physical features of the document were less incriminating than they first appeared. Contemporary sceptical discussion acknowledged that it purported to be a carbon copy on thin onionskin paper, meaning the absence of White House letterhead and an original Cutler signature was not inherently surprising.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1988/01/22165259/p27.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry Special Report;</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry Special Report;</span></span></span> Friedman additionally argued that Cutler's absence overseas on 14 July 1954 was compatible with the unsigned document because Cutler had left instructions for James Lay and J. ## Defeating an anomaly is not authentication

The most defensible conclusion is therefore narrower than either side's strongest rhetoric.

On the **specific typewriter question**, Friedman had the better evidence. Klass proposed an empirical test based on the claim that the relevant offices used Elite rather than Pica. Archival examples demonstrated Pica use, and Klass acknowledged that Friedman had supplied enough qualifying material to receive the full $1,000. Whatever other objections might be raised against the Cutler-Twining memorandum, **its Pica typeface cannot reasonably be treated as a stand-alone proof of forgery**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2022/09/26195532/SUN-72-Summer-2002.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry S</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">KEPTICS UFO NEWSLETTER By Philip J. Klass · ·July 27, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 27, 2013</span></span></span>

On the **larger authentication question**, however, the result is much less decisive. A historically plausible typewriter is a compatibility test, not a provenance test. The memorandum still lacks independent documentary confirmation of the alleged MJ-12 project in the related government records searched by the National Archives, and its unusual placement and missing register number remain unresolved features of its archival history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That distinction captures both the strength and the limit of Stanton Friedman's defence. His archival Pica examples successfully dismantled one of the cleaner technical arguments offered against the Cutler-Twining memo. They made “wrong typewriter” a poor reason to declare the document fraudulent. They did **not** turn the typewriter into positive proof that Robert Cutler, James Lay or the National Security Council created the memorandum in 1954.

The typewriter test therefore ended in an asymmetric result: **the alleged anomaly was substantially defeated, but the document was not authenticated**. For the Cutler-Twining memorandum, that difference is crucial.
