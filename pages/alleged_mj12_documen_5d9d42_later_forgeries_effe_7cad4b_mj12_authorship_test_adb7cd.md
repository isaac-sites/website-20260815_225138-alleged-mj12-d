## Introduction

Carole Chaski’s authorship testing is one of the more consequential examinations of the later MJ-12 document corpus because it asked a narrow, testable question: **did documents attributed to named historical figures actually resemble authenticated writing by those people?** In 2007, Michael S. Heiser published a preliminary report based on computational analysis performed by Chaski, a forensic linguist specialising in authorship attribution. Seventeen disputed documents attributed to nine people were compared with authenticated writing samples. Only one placed its claimed author first in the study’s similarity ranking. More importantly, four documents already identified independently as fraudulent were included without Chaski being told which they were; her test rejected the claimed authorship of all four.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

That does not linguistically disprove every alleged MJ-12 document. Several famous papers could not be tested at all, and the exercise was explicitly described as preliminary. What it does provide is an unusual internal check on part of the later corpus: a method capable of recognising known frauds also produced widespread authorship mismatches among documents whose status was still disputed.

## How the authorship comparison was structured

The 2007 report, *The Majestic Documents: A Forensic Linguistic Report*, was written by Heiser on the basis of Chaski’s analysis. The distinction matters: this was not a Chaski-authored journal article announcing a comprehensive scientific verdict on MJ-12. Heiser described the project itself as preliminary because cost prevented the use of the full range of Chaski’s authorship-analysis capabilities. He later continued to describe the paper as his account of Chaski’s analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

The basic design nevertheless addressed a real weakness in earlier arguments over the papers. Rather than asking whether a memo *looked* like a 1940s government document, the test concentrated on its prose. Chaski’s wider work in forensic linguistics stressed empirical authorship comparison rather than intuitive reliance on conspicuous vocabulary, spelling habits or isolated stylistic quirks. The US National Institute of Justice’s summary of her earlier research likewise warns that authorship conclusions should not rest on a single linguistic attribute or common-sense impressions of [writing style]({{ 'writing-style/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nij.ojp.gov/library/publications/daubert-inspired-assessment-language-based-author-identification" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nij.ojp.gov">[National Institute of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nij.ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Institute of JusticeDaubert-Inspired Assessment of Language-Based Author Identification &#124; National Institute of JusticeJanuary 1...</span></span></span>

For the MJ-12 exercise, the questioned texts were transcribed into plain-text files. Heiser and his collaborators retained the prose, salutations, misspellings and grammatical errors but removed such non-prose features as stamps, dates, file numbers, handwritten annotations and memorandum headings. That meant the analysis was deliberately separated from questions about typewriters, security markings, signatures, paper or other physical characteristics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

Seventeen questioned documents, attributed to nine claimed authors, qualified for testing. Those names included Franklin D. Roosevelt, Harry Truman, Dwight Eisenhower, John F. Kennedy, George C. Marshall, Roscoe [Hillenkoetter]({{ 'hillenkoetter/' | relative_url }}), Nathan [Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}), Vannevar Bush and Allen Dulles. Thirty authenticated documents by those nine people formed the comparison material. Heiser says the known samples were selected with attention to such factors as genre, period, recipient and text length.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

The comparison used an **n-gram approach**, looking computationally for recurring sequences of linguistic elements and measuring their similarity. Authenticated documents by each historical figure were combined into a known-author pool. Each questioned document was then compared with the pools, and the results were ranked by similarity. Under the validation standard reported for this version of Chaski’s method, the genuine author occupied the most similar, or “top”, position in 90.7 per cent of validation tests. A second- or third-place finish was therefore not treated as partial authentication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

That procedure is particularly relevant to MJ-12 because the corpus contains documents that can imitate the *appearance* of official correspondence while making specific claims about who wrote them. Stylometry provides a line of inquiry that a convincing [letterhead]({{ 'letterhead/' | relative_url }}), period typeface or plausible bureaucratic vocabulary cannot by itself satisfy.

## Known frauds became an internal check

The strongest feature of the exercise was not simply that most questioned documents failed. Heiser had quietly placed previously exposed fraudulent MJ-12 papers in the test set and says he did not identify them to Chaski beforehand. In effect, these documents served as controls for an obvious question: would the linguistic procedure reject papers already known by independent means to carry false authorship claims?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

According to the report, it did. All four such documents failed to match their purported authors: a July 1947 Truman-to-Twining document, the Marshall-to-Humelsine paper of September 1947, a Hillenkoetter-to-Truman document and the so-called Nathan Twining “White Hot Report”. Heiser identified these as documents whose fraudulent nature [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}) had already detected separately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

This is important because those results were not produced merely by noticing obvious typographical defects in the forged papers. The test material excluded formatting and other visual clues and concentrated on prose. Nor, according to Heiser’s account, was Chaski told which items were the planted known frauds. Their rejection therefore provides a limited but meaningful check that the test was responding to something relevant to authorship rather than simply reproducing Heiser’s prior classifications.

It should not be overstated as a full blind validation experiment. Heiser selected and prepared the corpus, the sample was small, and the MJ-12-specific exercise was preliminary rather than a large independent replication. But within those [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}), the known-forgery result is stronger evidence than merely applying a classifier to disputed papers and declaring its output persuasive.

## The disputed papers showed a broader mismatch

The result that widened the significance beyond the already exposed frauds was the poor performance of the remaining questioned documents. Of all 17 tested papers, only **Twining-Q3, “Twining to General Schulgen,”** placed Nathan Twining’s authenticated writing pool in the top position. Heiser consequently reported that this was the only tested item showing a strong likelihood of authorship by the person whose name it bore under the study’s stated criterion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

The other results included striking mismatches. For example, the Roosevelt-attributed FDR-Q1 ranked the Kennedy comparison pool first, followed by Eisenhower and Twining; Roosevelt’s own pool came fourth. A Vannevar Bush-attributed document likewise ranked the Kennedy pool first while Bush’s authenticated material ranked eighth. An Allen Dulles-attributed paper ranked Kennedy first and Dulles fourth. These rankings do not mean that Kennedy literally wrote the papers. They mean that, among the comparison pools available to the test, their measured linguistic characteristics resembled the Kennedy sample more closely than those of their alleged authors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

That distinction becomes especially important because the Kennedy pool ranked first for **eight of the 17 questioned documents**. Heiser suggested that this pattern might indicate that several purportedly different historical authors were actually being imitated by one common writer. But he explicitly presented that as a suggestion rather than proof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

The cautious interpretation is therefore the stronger one. Eight papers converging on the same comparison pool is suspicious when those papers purport to come from different people, but the experiment was designed principally to test claimed authorship, not to identify an unknown forger. “Closest to Kennedy” is not equivalent to “written by Kennedy”, nor does it independently establish that all eight came from one hand. Demonstrating common anonymous authorship would require a test designed specifically for that question and preferably additional comparison material.

## What the study could not test

Some of the best-known alleged MJ-12 papers fall outside the 17-document result. Authorship attribution requires a meaningful claim about who wrote a text, together with enough authenticated writing from that person for comparison. [The Eisenhower Briefing Document]({{ 'the-eisenhower-briefing-document/' | relative_url }}) was excluded because it says it was prepared *for* Eisenhower rather than identifying Eisenhower as its author. SOM1-01, the alleged special-operations manual on extraterrestrial recovery, similarly supplies no individual author for comparison. The Einstein-Oppenheimer paper was excluded because of its overlapping authorship, while very short documents could also fail the minimum-text requirement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

Selection was not random either. Among documents that had an attributed author and sufficient prose, Heiser gave preference partly to papers making particularly consequential claims about extraterrestrial biological entities, extraterrestrial wreckage or related matters. Cost was another constraint. This makes the exercise better understood as a targeted forensic examination of a significant subset than as a statistical sample representative of the entire MJ-12 archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

There are methodological reasons for restraint as well. Chaski’s published work treated computational authorship attribution as something that should operate alongside other forensic evidence, not replace it. In her 2007 discussion of authorship identification, she specifically argued for precautions against confirmation bias and for independent convergence between linguistic and digital-forensic findings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dl.ifip.org/db/conf/ifip11-9/df2007/Chaski07.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dl.ifip.org">[IFIP Digital Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dl.ifip.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Digital Library Chapter 9</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE KEYBOARD DILEMMA AND AUTHORSHIP IDENNovember 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2025</span></span></span> The MJ-12 report itself says that only a variation of her fuller ALIAS methodology was affordable and calls the resulting tests preliminary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

Nor does a successful authorship match authenticate what a document says. A genuine letter by a historical official could contain an error, deception or unverified claim; conversely, authorship testing cannot directly determine the age of paper, provenance of a photocopy or authenticity of a security marking. Heiser explicitly drew this boundary between authorship and content.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/225277137/MJ-12-Test-Article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Preparation of the Majestic Documents for Testing The Majestic documents tested by Dr.Read more...</span></span></span>

## What the results change in the MJ-12 debate

Chaski’s tests matter most when read as **corpus evidence rather than a universal verdict**. The exercise cannot legitimately be reduced to “linguistics proved all MJ-12 documents fake”. It did not examine all of them, and prominent documents such as the Eisenhower Briefing Document and SOM1-01 were outside the method’s reach. Heiser himself left open the possibility that authorless papers might require different forms of testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

The finding is nevertheless difficult to dismiss as irrelevant to the later corpus. The tested set contained documents supposedly written by numerous senior American officials across different administrations. Yet 16 of 17 failed to place their purported writer first under the study’s criterion; all four independently known fraudulent controls were among those failures; and eight questioned papers unexpectedly clustered nearest the same Kennedy comparison pool.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://drmsh.com/michaelsheiser/MJ%20Test%20article.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: drmsh.com">[Dr. MSH]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">drmsh.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Dr. MSHMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

That pattern changes the evidential burden. A later MJ-12 document cannot gain much credibility merely because its prose sounds official, fits the developing Majestic narrative or resembles other papers in the collection. Similarity within the corpus may itself be contaminated by fabricated material. The present-day Majestic Documents repository still describes a large collection of purported leaked material and foregrounds authentication research, illustrating why distinguishing individual documents rather than treating “MJ-12” as one homogeneous source remains essential.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[Majestic Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic DocumentsDocuments &#124; Majestic DocumentsOctober 29, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2018</span></span></span>

The wider official record reinforces the need for that caution without substituting for the linguistic test. The US Government Accountability Office reported that the agencies it consulted found no evidence that submitted MJ-12 material consisted of documents originally created within the executive branch, while relevant [presidential-library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) searches found no corroborating records for the designation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/assets/154832.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material</span></span></span>

Chaski’s contribution is therefore narrower, and more useful, than a sweeping declaration about Majestic Twelve. Her analysis supplied an independent type of test to a portion of the later corpus, correctly rejected documents already exposed as fraudulent, and then found the same basic authorship problem far more widely. It cannot identify who fabricated the mismatching documents, establish that they all share one source, or adjudicate untested papers. What it does show is that, for most of the 17 documents actually examined, the claimed historical authorship did not survive the linguistic comparison.
