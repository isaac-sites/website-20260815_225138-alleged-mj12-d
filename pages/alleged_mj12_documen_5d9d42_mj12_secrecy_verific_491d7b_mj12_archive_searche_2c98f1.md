## Introduction

The National Archives did not merely look for a file explicitly labelled “MJ-12”. Archivists searched the surrounding collections in which a real presidentially connected, military and National Security Council programme would plausibly have left traces. Those searches covered [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) and Joint Chiefs of Staff records, [presidential-library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) holdings, National Security Council indexes and meeting minutes, appointment records, classified intelligence material and files associated with Robert Cutler, the Eisenhower aide named on the best-known archival MJ-12 memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_mj12_secrecy_verific_491d7b_mj12_archive_searche_2c98f1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Archive Searches illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_mj12_secrecy_verific_491d7b_mj12_archive_searche_2c98f1-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_mj12_secrecy_verific_491d7b_mj12_archive_searche_2c98f1-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

The result was strikingly sparse. The principal archival exception was a one-page memorandum dated 14 July 1954, addressed from Cutler to General Nathan [Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) and referring to an “NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project”. Beyond that disputed item, the searches produced no corroborating body of MJ-12 records. Worse for the document's evidential value, archivists found several discrepancies between the memorandum and the administrative record surrounding the event it purported to describe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Which government collections were searched?

The breadth of the search is important because MJ-12, as depicted in the alleged documents, would not have been an isolated Air Force filing. It supposedly involved senior military figures and presidential-level national-security machinery. The relevant test, therefore, was whether references appeared across the institutional records surrounding those people and organisations.

The National Archives says its staff conducted “extensive searches” among records in its [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}) from the **US Air Force and Joint Chiefs of Staff**. Its 1987 MJ-12 reference report was more specific: the Military Reference Branch searched records of the **Secretary of Defense, Joint Chiefs of Staff, Headquarters US Air Force and related files** without finding further information about MJ-12. The Archives also sent enquiries to the Air Force, Joint Chiefs and National Security Council, again without obtaining corroborating information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The search extended into the presidential record. The **Harry S. Truman and Dwight D. Eisenhower presidential libraries** searched their holdings for references to, or copies of, the alleged MJ-12 material. When the General Accounting Office, now the Government Accountability Office (GAO), revisited the question in 1995, it reported that archivists at both libraries had searched related material including **classified intelligence and National Security Council documents**. They found nothing fitting the description of the Majestic 12 material and no references to that designation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

That finding should not be confused with an absence of UFO-related material generally. The National Archives holds substantial records concerning historical UFO investigations, including the declassified Project Blue Book collection, and its current guide to presidential-library UAP holdings identifies UFO-related records in both the Truman and Eisenhower collections. The problem is narrower: within collections that demonstrably preserve material on UFOs and national-security affairs, archivists did not uncover a parallel documentary trail establishing MJ-12.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Archivists searched for more than the exact name “MJ-12”

A programme can be missed if researchers search only one code name. The National Archives therefore tested alternative terms and the administrative context surrounding the alleged documents.

For the National Security Council (NSC) records of the Truman and Eisenhower administrations, archivists searched indexes to the **Policy Paper and Meeting Minute files** using “MJ-12”, “majestic”, “unidentified flying objects”, “UFO”, “flying saucers”, “extraterrestrial biological entities” and “Aquarius”. The Archives reports that these searches were negative apart from the Cutler-to-Twining memorandum. Its more detailed reference report records a further index search for “flying discs”, likewise without a relevant listing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Archivists also checked the event supposedly sitting behind that memorandum. The [Cutler-Twining paper]({{ 'paper-test/' | relative_url }}) refers to an MJ-12 briefing associated with an already scheduled [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) meeting on **16 July 1954**. Yet a search of the NSC's official meeting-minute files found no NSC meeting on that date, while a search through the NSC minutes for July 1954 found no reference to “MJ-12” or “Majestic”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The Eisenhower Library then checked a different kind of record: the President's **appointment books**. It reported that they contained no entry for a special 16 July meeting that might have included an MJ-12 briefing. That was significant because the library noted that even Eisenhower's “off the record” meetings normally generated appointment-book entries recording their time and participants. The surviving appointment book for July 1954 provides the underlying presidential schedule against which the claim can be assessed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This is a more demanding archival test than asking whether someone can find the words “Majestic Twelve”. Researchers looked for the meeting, the administrative indexing, the presidential scheduling and the surrounding NSC documentation that the memorandum's own story would lead them to expect.

## The one archival MJ-12 memo did not lead to a paper trail

The Cutler-Twining memorandum is important precisely because it **was found in government custody**. It was located in Record Group 341, records of Headquarters US Air Force, in folder T4-1846. That fact distinguishes it from alleged MJ-12 papers whose provenance begins outside government archives. But archival custody alone does not establish that a document was genuinely created by the official whose name appears on it. The National Archives explicitly warns that when it certifies a copy, it is certifying that the reproduction matches a document in its custody; it is not authenticating the document's contents or origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The surrounding file context raised questions rather than resolving them. According to the Archives, the series containing the memorandum was filed by **[Top Secret register]({{ 'register-numbers/' | relative_url }}) number**, but the Cutler-Twining paper carried no such number. There were also no other “NSC/MJ-12” documents in its folder. Searches outward from that location into the related defence and Air Force collections did not produce the expected supporting material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Archivists also compared the paper with records produced by Cutler and the Eisenhower NSC staff. The memorandum lacked official government letterhead or a watermark. A National Archives conservation specialist identified it as a ribbon copy on dictation onionskin, while the Eisenhower Library's examination of representative Cutler documents found different patterns of paper, watermark and letterhead use. The Archives additionally reported that the “Top Secret Restricted Information” marking associated with the document was not an NSC marking used during the Eisenhower administration; the NSC's Freedom of Information office placed its introduction there in the Nixon era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

None of those features, considered separately, supplies a mathematical proof of forgery. Their importance is cumulative: the one archival object that appears to connect MJ-12 with the official record does not sit inside a surrounding sequence of documents that independently explains and corroborates it.

## Robert Cutler's own records create another mismatch

The most concrete contextual check concerned Robert Cutler himself. The memorandum is dated **14 July 1954** and bears his name as Special Assistant to the President. But the Eisenhower presidential papers document Cutler travelling outside Washington at the time.

The National Archives reports that Cutler was visiting military installations in Europe and North Africa between **3 and 15 July**. His Administration Series contains a 20 July memorandum and report to Eisenhower describing that trip. NSC Staff Papers also contain a 3 July memorandum from Cutler instructing his subordinates, James S. Lay and J. Patrick Coyne, how to handle NSC administrative business while he was absent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That does not make it physically impossible for a document bearing Cutler's name to have been prepared while he was abroad; offices could prepare communications for absent officials. But it creates a provenance question that ought to be answerable from surrounding records. If the 14 July communication represented genuine NSC business, the records of Cutler's absence and delegated responsibilities provide an obvious place to seek confirmation of who prepared, authorised or transmitted it. The National Archives instead concluded that the circumstances suggested one of Cutler's deputies would have signed such a memorandum during his absence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Here again, the evidential issue is not simply an oddity on the face of an alleged secret document. It is the failure of the ordinary records around a named official to supply the missing context.

## Why the missing cross-references matter


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

An absence in one archive is weak evidence. Records can be destroyed, misfiled, retained by another agency, remain classified or simply escape a search. The MJ-12 archival finding is more consequential because the negative results recur across **different record systems that should overlap if the alleged documents accurately describe government activity**.

</div>

The alleged trail points towards the presidency, NSC and senior military establishment. Archivists consequently looked in presidential holdings, NSC records, Air Force records, Joint Chiefs files and defence material. They searched not only a project name but related terminology, meeting minutes and scheduling records. The expected cross-references did not emerge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The GAO's 1995 review strengthened that conclusion by asking several government bodies independently about the material. The Information Security Oversight Office, Air Force and National Archives told GAO that their knowledge of Majestic 12 was limited to material submitted by **non-governmental persons** and that their own files contained no records establishing MJ-12. GAO said it found nothing contradicting their conclusion that there was no evidence the circulated MJ-12 material consisted of documents actually originating in the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

GAO also encountered a useful control case. A purported 17 November 1980 Air Force Office of Special Investigations message contained the words “MJ Twelve”. When GAO asked AFOSI to check it, the organisation found no official record copy in its files and reported that an earlier examination had concluded the message was a forgery. That episode demonstrates why merely finding government-looking material in collections assembled through public enquiries does not establish governmental provenance: the archival question is whether an official record trail independently connects the item to the agency that supposedly produced it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

## What the archive searches establish — and what they do not

The National Archives findings do **not** establish the unlimited proposition that no highly secret activity involving any of the alleged MJ-12 figures could ever have existed. Archival searches rarely justify that degree of certainty, particularly where classified national-security records are concerned.

They support a narrower and more useful conclusion. After searching the government collections in which corroboration of the alleged MJ-12 documentary story could reasonably be expected, archivists did not find a supporting network of records. The Truman and Eisenhower libraries likewise found no classified intelligence or NSC material fitting the description or referring to the MJ-12 designation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The one prominent exception, the Cutler-Twining memorandum, therefore has an unusual evidential position. Its presence in National Archives custody is real, but the Archives does not authenticate it; its immediate folder contains no accompanying MJ-12 documentation; searches of related military and NSC records failed to extend its trail; the alleged 16 July briefing is not corroborated by the NSC minutes or Eisenhower's appointment records; and Cutler's documented whereabouts complicate the memorandum's stated date and authorship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That is why the archive searches matter to the wider [secrecy problem]({{ 'secrecy-problem/' | relative_url }}). Secrecy can plausibly explain why a sensitive programme's substantive files might remain inaccessible. It is less effective as an explanation for why a document describing activity across several senior institutions fails to connect with the independent administrative records surrounding the people, meetings and offices it names. For MJ-12, the National Archives search produced not a hidden documentary system glimpsed through scattered cross-references, but essentially the opposite: **one disputed memorandum surrounded by repeated failures to find the corroborating record structure that would give it secure governmental provenance**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>
