## Introduction

The most secure fact about the original MJ-12 material is not who created it, but how it surfaced: in December 1984, television producer Jaime Shandera said he received an anonymously mailed roll of exposed 35 mm film at his home in Burbank, California. When processed, the film yielded photographs of the papers later called [the Eisenhower Briefing Document]({{ 'the-eisenhower-briefing-document/' | relative_url }}) and Truman-Forrestal memorandum. Shandera, William Moore and [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}) subsequently treated those images as the starting point for investigating the alleged Majestic-12 programme. Friedman himself dated the episode to Shandera's 1984 receipt of exposed 35 mm film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The delivery establishes a reasonably well-attested point at which the MJ-12 images entered the researchers' possession. It does **not** establish where the photographed sheets had previously been, who photographed them, whether those sheets were genuine government records, or who put the film in the post. The [Albuquerque postmark]({{ 'postmark-clues/' | relative_url }}) is therefore a clue to the mailing route, not a chain of [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}). More than four decades later, no sender has been conclusively identified.

## What arrived in Burbank in December 1984

The commonly reported date of receipt is **11 December 1984**. A detailed reconstruction by UFO researcher Kevin Randle says Shandera received a taped manila envelope with no return address, postmarked in Albuquerque, New Mexico, on 8 December. Other accounts give the Albuquerque postmark as 9 December, illustrating that even a seemingly simple detail has not been reported consistently. A 2007 MUFON symposium paper, for example, gives 11 December as the receipt date but 9 December for the Albuquerque postmark.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theufochronicles.com/2014/06/the-myth-of-mj-12-appendix-pt-1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufochronicles.com">[The UFO Chronicles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufochronicles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the myth of mj 12 appendix pt 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO ChroniclesThe Myth of MJ-12: Appendix A –Pt 1June 29, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 29, 2014</span></span></span>

Accounts also differ in how much detail they provide about the packaging. Randle describes a manila outer envelope, a heavily taped second envelope and a third envelope bearing a Marriott Hotel logo. Inside was reportedly a canister of undeveloped Kodak Tri-X black-and-white 35 mm film. His reconstruction says that, once developed, the roll contained photographs of the pages twice, with the remainder of the film blank; Moore and Shandera were reportedly present when prints were made from the negatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theufochronicles.com/2014/06/the-myth-of-mj-12-appendix-pt-1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufochronicles.com">[The UFO Chronicles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufochronicles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the myth of mj 12 appendix pt 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO ChroniclesThe Myth of MJ-12: Appendix A –Pt 1June 29, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 29, 2014</span></span></span>

Those particulars should not all be treated as equally firm. Contemporary public documentation of the receipt is sparse, and later narratives sometimes conflict. A 1990 article by sceptical investigator Philip J. Klass described a roll of 35 mm film and photocopies of the two purported documents as having arrived at Shandera's home from an unknown sender on 11 December 1984. Friedman, writing from the opposite side of the authenticity dispute, likewise identified Shandera's receipt of exposed 35 mm film at his Burbank home in 1984 as the beginning of the [original documents]({{ 'original-document/' | relative_url }})' investigation. The convergence on the basic event is stronger than the evidence for every detail of the wrapping, postmark date or contents of the package.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1990/01/22165233/p30.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry SPECIAL REPORT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry SPECIAL REPORT</span></span></span>

Once processed, the film supplied the material that became central to the original MJ-12 case. It showed the purported **Eisenhower Briefing Document**, dated 18 November 1952, and the **Truman-[Forrestal]({{ 'forrestal/' | relative_url }}) memorandum**, dated 24 September 1947. The briefing represented itself as a highly classified account prepared for President-elect Dwight D. Eisenhower, while the Truman memorandum purported to authorise Forrestal and Vannevar Bush to proceed with "Operation Majestic Twelve".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

The critical distinction is between the **film** and the supposed **1950s records depicted on it**. Shandera could possess the former without establishing the history of the latter. No original 1947 or 1952 paper sheets accompanied the delivery, and the anonymous mailing supplied no accountable witness who could explain how the alleged records had left government custody and come to be photographed.

## What the Albuquerque postmark can — and cannot — establish

The Albuquerque postmark is the parcel's most frequently cited geographical clue. Multiple independent retellings agree that the mailing bore no useful return address and had been posted through Albuquerque. That is significant because Albuquerque was already important to the UFO-research environment surrounding Moore, and Kirtland [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) Base was nearby. It has consequently encouraged theories that the sender had some connection with military or intelligence personnel in New Mexico.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Majestic_12_documents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Majestic 12 documents &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12 documents &#124; WikiDiscJuly 1, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2026</span></span></span>

But a postmark has a much narrower evidential meaning. At most, it indicates the postal system through which an envelope was processed. It does not identify the person who prepared the package, establish that the sender lived in Albuquerque, prove that the film was exposed there, or show that the photographed pages had ever been in New Mexico. Someone could have carried an already prepared package to Albuquerque specifically to mail it. The location is therefore evidence about **dispatch**, not about the provenance of the documents shown on the negatives.

Even the date requires caution. Published accounts give both 8 and 9 December for the Albuquerque cancellation. Randle cites 8 December; another symposium account says 9 December. That disagreement is small in chronological terms — both versions are compatible with delivery on 11 December — but it is methodologically important. The parcel's history has largely been reconstructed from photographs and later participant or researcher accounts rather than from a complete contemporary postal record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theufochronicles.com/2014/06/the-myth-of-mj-12-appendix-pt-1.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufochronicles.com">[The UFO Chronicles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufochronicles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the myth of mj 12 appendix pt 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO ChroniclesThe Myth of MJ-12: Appendix A –Pt 1June 29, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 29, 2014</span></span></span>

There has also been scrutiny of the surviving image of the package itself. A specialist note preserved among historical UFO files examined the postage visible on a reproduction of the envelope and identified twelve common 20-cent US postage stamps arranged in three blocks of four. The writer could extract details about the stamps from the image but not the identity of the person who used them. Such examination illustrates the limit of physical clues: packaging can potentially narrow when and how something was mailed without supplying the missing human link between the photographed papers and Shandera.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://files.bluebookfiles.org/pdfs/18010.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.bluebookfiles.org">[Bluebook Files]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.bluebookfiles.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bluebook FilesBill Moore File 5February 16, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 16, 2026</span></span></span>

That distinction matters especially because an anonymous Albuquerque mailing can support mutually incompatible stories. A believer might infer that an insider used an indirect method to leak sensitive records. A sceptic might infer that a hoaxer deliberately selected Albuquerque because of its associations with Roswell, Kirtland and Moore's contacts. A disinformation hypothesis can also be constructed around the same facts. None follows from the postmark alone.

## The delivery broke the chain of custody at its first link

For disputed historical documents, provenance means more than knowing when a copy first became visible. Ideally, investigators would be able to trace a record from the office that created it, through filing and archival custody, to the copy being examined. The Shandera delivery begins at the opposite end: researchers suddenly possessed photographs whose earlier history was unknown.

The 35 mm medium compounds that difficulty. A photograph preserves visible characteristics of a page — wording, layout, typeface, signatures and classification markings — but separates those characteristics from the physical sheet. Researchers therefore could not test the alleged 1947 and 1952 originals as pieces of paper: their paper stock, ink or toner, impressions, ageing, alterations and other physical properties were unavailable. Nor could they inspect a government folder or registry showing where the sheets had supposedly been filed.

This is why "received in 1984" and "created in 1947 or 1952" are fundamentally different claims. The mailing supplies evidence for the former. The dates printed within the photographed documents are claims made by the documents themselves and require independent authentication. The [anonymous film]({{ 'anonymous-film/' | relative_url }}) cannot bridge the roughly three-decade evidential gap.

The same problem appears in later official inquiries. When US agencies encountered purported MJ-12 material, they did not discover a government custody trail leading back to Shandera's film. The FBI says that, after copies of an "Operation Majestic-12" document reached two of its offices in 1988, an Air Force investigation determined the document to be fake; the FBI's own historical summary says the Bureau likewise concluded that the purported classified document was fraudulent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=Majestic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI — Federal Bureau of Investigation...</span></span></span>

A 1995 US General Accounting Office inquiry was equally relevant to the provenance question. In correspondence concerning Majestic-12 material, the GAO described its **origin as unknown** and sought the views of federal agencies about the authenticity of the publicly circulated records. The significance for the 1984 parcel is straightforward: subsequent government attention did not uncover an official documentary trail identifying a federal source for Shandera's film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://phatsoheff.tripod.com/ufo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: phatsoheff.tripod.com">[phatsoheff.tripod.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">phatsoheff.tripod.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Phatso Heff&#x27;s UFO page!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Phatso Heff&#x27;s UFO page!</span></span></span>

## Why Albuquerque generated suspects but not a sender

The most tempting inference from the postmark comes from Moore's pre-existing contacts. Before the film appeared, Moore had dealt with Richard Doty, an Air Force Office of Special Investigations figure stationed at Kirtland Air Force Base in the Albuquerque area. Doty's involvement in UFO-related information and disinformation has made the New Mexico origin of the package an enduring focus of speculation. Later accounts have therefore proposed variations on an intelligence operation, a private hoax involving people in the UFO field, or some mixture of the two.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Majestic_12_documents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Majestic 12 documents &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic 12 documents &#124; WikiDiscJuly 1, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1, 2026</span></span></span>

Those circumstances justify asking whether the Albuquerque location was meaningful. They do not justify identifying Doty, Moore or anyone else as the sender without additional evidence. No publicly established postal record, eyewitness testimony, fingerprint result, contemporaneous confession or comparable evidence has tied a named person to preparing and posting the December 1984 parcel.

The distinction between **opportunity** and **attribution** is particularly important here. A person might have known Shandera's address, understood Moore's research interests, had access to Albuquerque and possessed enough knowledge of government paperwork to create convincing-looking images. Demonstrating those opportunities would still fall short of proving that person exposed the film and mailed it.

The method of delivery itself is also ambiguous. Anonymous photographic film could have been chosen by a genuine source seeking to avoid handing over traceable originals. But precisely the same method would suit a fabricator: it removes the alleged source documents from physical inspection while allowing their visual content to circulate. The mailing procedure therefore cannot authenticate the story merely because secrecy would be expected of a whistle-blower. Secrecy explains both the genuine-leak hypothesis and several forms of deception.

## Why the sender remains unidentified

The mystery persists because the strongest evidence starts **after** the decisive acts have already happened. By the time Shandera had the package, someone had selected or created the pages, arranged them for photography, exposed the film, packaged it and placed it into the postal system. None of those stages came with a verified witness or record identifying the actor.

Three separate questions are sometimes collapsed into one:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Who mailed the parcel?** The evidence establishes an anonymous mailing associated with Albuquerque but no named sender.
* **Who exposed the film?** That person could have been the sender, but there is no proof that the roles were identical.
* **Who created the pages photographed on the film?** Again, the photographer need not have been their author or fabricator.

</div>

That separation makes the provenance problem harder than a simple "anonymous letter" mystery. Even discovering who posted the envelope would not automatically establish who made the MJ-12 papers or whether the photographed sheets came from a genuine government file.

There is also an important chronological limitation. Sceptical researcher Kevin Randle has argued that the envelope establishes less than is sometimes claimed because there is no independent proof that the surviving mailing envelope necessarily contained the film attributed to it. He accepts that Moore's documentation places Shandera's claimed receipt on 11 December 1984 but notes that the first securely public circulation of the briefing document came later. That is a stricter evidential position than the standard narrative, but it highlights the same underlying weakness: much of the initial chain depends on the testimony and records of the small group that first possessed the material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2014/12/mj-12-and-1985.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[kevinrandle.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

Conversely, Friedman regarded the original film delivery as genuine and spent years defending the authenticity of the core MJ-12 documents. His account is valuable because he was directly involved with Moore and Shandera, but it does not solve the anonymity problem. Even in Friedman's retrospective presentation, the story begins with Shandera receiving the film; it does not supply a verified pre-Shandera custody history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

## What the 1984 delivery actually proves

The December 1984 parcel remains historically important even without accepting the claims written on the photographed pages. It gives the original MJ-12 controversy a concrete emergence point: by Shandera's account, the images were in his possession by 11 December, after an anonymous mailing through Albuquerque. Soon afterwards they were being examined by the small research circle around Shandera, Moore and Friedman, before their eventual public release in 1987.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1990/01/22165233/p30.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry SPECIAL REPORT</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry SPECIAL REPORT</span></span></span>

What it does **not** provide is provenance extending backwards into the Truman or Eisenhower administrations. Later federal scrutiny did not supply that missing link; the FBI records instead describe the purported MJ-12 document it examined as bogus, while the GAO characterised the origin of publicly circulated MJ-12 material as unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=Majestic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI — Federal Bureau of Investigation...</span></span></span>

That leaves the most defensible conclusion narrower than either the leak or hoax narratives. An anonymous 35 mm film delivery is part of the documented history of how the alleged MJ-12 papers surfaced, but the delivery mechanism cannot establish where the photographed pages originated. Albuquerque identifies a postal waypoint, not an author. Shandera identifies the first reported recipient, not the previous custodian. And without reliable evidence linking the parcel backwards to a photographer, a source file and an accountable sender, the person behind the December 1984 mailing remains unidentified.
