## Introduction

The surviving Cutler–[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum presents a specific physical puzzle within [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}): the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) says its conservation specialist identified the sheet as a **ribbon copy prepared on dictation onionskin**, not as an ordinary carbon copy. That distinction matters because onionskin was commonly used for carbon duplicates, whereas a ribbon copy is the sheet directly typed through the typewriter ribbon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_paper_letterhead_for_a6c73e_ribbon_vs_carbon_cop_1c2ea4-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Ribbon Copy illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_paper_letterhead_for_a6c73e_ribbon_vs_carbon_cop_1c2ea4-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_paper_letterhead_for_a6c73e_ribbon_vs_carbon_cop_1c2ea4-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

If the disputed sheet were simply a carbon retained after an outgoing original had been typed on [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) stationery, its plain, lightweight paper would be comparatively easy to explain. NARA's ribbon-copy finding removes that straightforward explanation. The surviving sheet was apparently itself directly typed, yet it lacks official [letterhead]({{ 'letterhead/' | relative_url }}), while NARA reports that most National Security Council (NSC) documents sent out were prepared on White House letterhead. This does not prove fabrication, but it makes the relationship between the memo's paper, typing process and supposed administrative function substantially harder to account for.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## What ribbon typing means for the surviving sheet


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

In typewriter terminology, a ribbon copy is not simply another name for a carbon. Merriam-Webster defines it as a document made by the typewriter ribbon — effectively the original as opposed to the carbon copy. The US Patent and Trademark Office gives the mechanical distinction in: in an original or ribbon-produced sheet, the record medium is adjacent to the inked ribbon at the printing point, so the typeface's impact transfers ribbon ink directly to that sheet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/ribbon%20copy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merriam-webster.com">[Merriam-Webster]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merriam-webster.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RIBBON COPY Definition &amp; Meaning</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RIBBON COPY Definition &amp; Meaning - Merriam-Webster...</span></span></span>

</div>

A conventional carbon copy is produced differently. Carbon paper, coated with pigment, sits between sheets. The typewriter strikes the upper sheet, and pressure transfers pigment from the carbon paper to the sheet underneath. The Society of American Archivists accordingly defines a carbon copy as a copy created simultaneously with an original through pigment transferred by pressure from coated paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/carbon-copy.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dictionary.archivists.org">[dictionary.archivists.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dictionary.archivists.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SA A Dictionary: carbon copy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SA A Dictionary: carbon copy</span></span></span>

That mechanism explains why onionskin and carbon copying are so strongly associated. Onionskin is thin and strong enough to transmit the impact of typewriter keys through a multi-sheet assembly. University of Illinois preservation guidance describes carbon copies as commonly appearing on thin, semi-translucent supports such as onionskin or manifold paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psap.library.illinois.edu/collection-id-guide/officeprintcopy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psap.library.illinois.edu">[Preservation Self-Assessment Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psap.library.illinois.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on illinois.edu.</span></span></span>

But **paper type and copy type are not the same thing**. A sheet of onionskin can be used as a carbon duplicate, yet it can also be placed directly against the ribbon and typed as a ribbon copy. The latter is what NARA reported in the Cutler–Twining case. Its conservation specialist examined the actual sheet and concluded that it was a ribbon copy on “diction onionskin” — evidently a rendering of “dictation onionskin”, as reproduced in other versions of the archival correspondence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That is the crucial forensic point. The anomaly is not merely that the memo is on thin paper. It is that **thin paper normally associated with office duplication was apparently used here as the directly typed sheet**.

## Why a carbon-copy explanation would reduce the anomaly

Consider the administrative scenario that would be easiest to reconcile with NARA's other findings. A secretary types an outgoing memorandum to General Nathan Twining on official White House letterhead. Carbon paper and an onionskin sheet are placed underneath. The recipient receives the letterhead original; the office retains the thin carbon duplicate. Decades later, only that file carbon survives.

In such a scenario, the absence of printed letterhead on the surviving onionskin would have limited evidential force. The retained carbon need not necessarily reproduce stationery that was physically printed on the top sheet. Indeed, the Eisenhower [Presidential Library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }})'s comparison of Cutler material explicitly found onionskin carbon copies, including examples without a [watermark]({{ 'watermark/' | relative_url }}). NARA therefore did **not** establish a rule that every genuine Cutler-related onionskin sheet had to carry an eagle watermark.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The ribbon-copy finding changes the logic. If the Cutler–Twining sheet received its characters directly from the ribbon, it cannot be explained merely as the lower carbon sheet from a normal letterhead original. It occupied the position of the directly typed document in the typing operation. The fact that it happens to be onionskin consequently requires an additional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uspto.gov/web/patents/classification/uspc400/defs400.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uspto.gov">[USPTO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uspto.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Class Definition for Class 400</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Class Definition for Class 400 - TYPEWRITING MACHINES...</span></span></span>

This is also why the frequently encountered description of the document as “a carbon” is important rather than trivial. [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}), a leading defender of the MJ-12 documents, described the original Cutler–Twining sheet as a carbon on Fox Paper “Dictation Onion Skin”. A later publication of Friedman's arguments similarly emphasised that the sheet was genuine onionskin stock and argued that Fox Paper had supplied such material to government buyers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://luforu.org/cutler-twining-memo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luforu.org">[LUFORU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luforu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cutler-Twining memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cutler-Twining memoJuly 14, 1954...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 1954</span></span></span>

Those claims address one question — whether the paper itself could plausibly have existed in government use — but they do not resolve NARA's different finding about **how the particular sheet was typed**. Establishing that government offices possessed dictation onionskin does not establish that a directly typed memorandum to the Air Force Chief of Staff would normally have been produced on it.

There is also reason to be cautious about identifying copy method from colour or appearance alone. A researcher who inspected the Cutler–Twining sheet at NARA in 2019 reported blue text but could not determine visually whether this represented carbon transfer or a blue ribbon. More generally, questioned-document research has treated the relationship between ribbon copies, carbon copies and typewriter impressions as a technical problem in which physical features can be misinterpreted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tomwhitmoreblog.wordpress.com/2019/09/10/nara-notes-09-09-2019-the-cutler-twining-memo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tomwhitmoreblog.wordpress.com">[TOM WHITMORE BLOG]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tomwhitmoreblog.wordpress.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nara notes 09 09 2019 the cutler twining memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">nara notes 09 09 2019 the cutler twining memo</span></span></span> The strongest evidence for this particular memo is therefore not a later researcher's impression of blue typing, but NARA's recorded conservation examination.

## How direct typing sharpens the stationery problem

NARA placed three findings together: the Cutler–Twining memo lacks official government letterhead; it lacks the watermark pattern expected from the sampled Cutler bond originals; and its conservation specialist classified it as a ribbon copy on dictation onionskin. The Eisenhower Library comparison further found that most documents sent out by the NSC were prepared on White House letterhead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

These facts have different evidential weights. The absence of a particular watermark is not decisive because NARA itself says Cutler's onionskin carbon copies could have either an eagle watermark **or no watermark**. Nor does the mere presence of onionskin establish anything suspicious: preservation literature confirms that onionskin was ordinary office material for typewritten duplication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The stronger question is functional: **why does a sheet identified as the directly typed copy look physically more like copy stock than the normal outgoing stationery?**

There are possible innocent answers. A memorandum could have been prepared informally, as a working sheet, or outside the most common office routine. Government paperwork was never perfectly uniform, and NARA's statement that “most” outgoing NSC documents used White House letterhead is explicitly not a claim that all did. A ribbon copy on onionskin is therefore an anomaly to explain, not an impossibility.

Yet treating the sheet as an ordinary file carbon does not answer that anomaly, because it conflicts with the conservation finding. The distinction can be summarised simply:

* **If it were a carbon copy:** plain onionskin would fit a familiar office-copy mechanism, and the outgoing original could have existed separately on White House letterhead.
* **If it is a ribbon copy:** the surviving onionskin was itself directly typed, so the missing letterhead belongs to the directly produced document rather than being an artefact of carbon duplication.
* **What NARA found:** the second description — ribbon copy on dictation onionskin — while also reporting that most outgoing NSC documents were produced on White House letterhead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That combination, rather than onionskin alone, is the branch-specific forensic discrepancy.

## The carbon-versus-ribbon dispute does not authenticate the memo by itself

The physical evidence should not be pushed further than it can support. A ribbon copy does not necessarily mean “the recipient's original”, still less “authentic original”. The term describes the **typing mechanism**: the sheet received its typing directly through the ribbon rather than through an intervening carbon-transfer sheet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/ribbon%20copy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merriam-webster.com">[Merriam-Webster]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merriam-webster.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">RIBBON COPY Definition &amp; Meaning</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RIBBON COPY Definition &amp; Meaning - Merriam-Webster...</span></span></span> A document could be directly typed for several administrative reasons, and a later fabrication could obviously also be directly typed.

Conversely, calling the Cutler–Twining memo a carbon cannot make the stationery discrepancy disappear unless there is persuasive evidence that the surviving sheet actually was produced by carbon transfer. NARA's published account says otherwise. The later observation that its lettering appears blue is ambiguous: blue carbon transfer existed, but so did coloured typewriter media, and visual colour alone does not override a conservation examination. University of Illinois guidance notes that carbon-copy text itself can occur in black, blue, violet or red, illustrating why colour is not a sufficient discriminator.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://psap.library.illinois.edu/collection-id-guide/officeprintcopy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psap.library.illinois.edu">[Preservation Self-Assessment Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psap.library.illinois.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on illinois.edu.</span></span></span>

Friedman's defence adds a useful qualification. He argued that the Fox Paper dictation-onionskin stock was appropriate to the period and had been bought by the US government.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span> If correct, that weakens any simplistic argument that the *paper stock itself* was unavailable to government typists in 1954. It does not, however, establish the provenance of the words typed onto that sheet, nor does it explain why this directly typed document lacks the White House letterhead that NARA found on most outgoing NSC material.

The defensible forensic conclusion is therefore narrower than either side's strongest rhetoric. NARA's ribbon-copy finding does not prove that the Cutler–Twining memorandum was planted or forged. What it does is remove one of the easiest mundane explanations for its unusual stationery. A plain onionskin **carbon retained from a letterhead original** would fit a familiar mid-century filing practice. A plain onionskin **ribbon copy**, which is what NARA says survives, requires a different explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why the distinction matters

The [Cutler–Twining paper]({{ 'paper-test/' | relative_url }}) controversy is sometimes reduced to “wrong paper” versus “government used that paper too”. Neither formulation captures the actual issue. The evidential value lies in the interaction of three features: the sheet is onionskin, it lacks official letterhead, and NARA says it was directly ribbon typed.

Onionskin by itself is unremarkable. Missing letterhead on a carbon file copy would also be relatively unsurprising. But once the surviving sheet is identified as the ribbon copy, those two otherwise weak observations reinforce one another: the directly typed document was produced on plain lightweight stock even though the archival comparison found White House letterhead to be the usual medium for NSC documents sent out of the office.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That is why the ribbon-versus-carbon distinction deserves to be treated as a mechanism rather than a semantic dispute. It changes the hypothetical production history of the sheet. The carbon explanation supplies a missing letterhead original almost automatically; the ribbon finding does not. The result is not a stand-alone verdict on the alleged MJ-12 document, but a concrete physical discrepancy that an authenticity argument has to explain rather than simply label away.
