## Introduction

The Eisenhower [Presidential Library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }})’s Cutler comparison was strong enough to establish a meaningful baseline, but not strong enough to establish an exceptionless rule about Robert Cutler’s stationery. That distinction is central to the paper-and-[letterhead]({{ 'letterhead/' | relative_url }}) dispute surrounding the alleged MJ-12 Cutler–Twining memorandum.



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_paper_letterhead_for_a6c73e_cutler_sample_streng_4ba4b5-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Cutler Sample illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_paper_letterhead_for_a6c73e_cutler_sample_streng_4ba4b5-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_paper_letterhead_for_a6c73e_cutler_sample_streng_4ba4b5-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

The National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) says the Library examined a **“representative sample”** of Cutler documents. In that sample, Cutler’s National Security Council (NSC) bond-paper documents carried an eagle [watermark]({{ 'watermark/' | relative_url }}); onionskin carbon copies could have an eagle watermark or none; and most correspondence sent out by the NSC used [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) letterhead. The disputed memorandum was different: NARA’s conservator identified the surviving sheet as a ribbon-typed copy on dictation onionskin, without official letterhead or a watermark.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That makes the comparison genuinely probative. It does not, however, justify the stronger claim that Cutler **never** used unwatermarked paper, or that every conceivable memorandum to General Nathan [Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) had to follow one stationery formula. The surviving archival description does not publish the sample size, selection method or item-by-item inventory. The safest conclusion is therefore statistical and comparative: the questioned memo departs from the documented normal pattern, but the sample alone cannot prove that such a departure was impossible.

## What categories of Cutler material were compared

NARA’s current MJ-12 reference report is unusually useful because it does not collapse every sheet of paper into one category. It distinguishes among **bond-paper documents, onionskin carbon copies, outgoing NSC correspondence and carbon copies from a period when Cutler was outside the NSC staff**. Those distinctions matter because different copies of the same office communication could legitimately be made on different stocks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

According to NARA, the Eisenhower Library reported three relevant patterns. First, all Cutler documents in the sampled material that were created while he served on the NSC staff had an eagle watermark **when they were on bond paper**. Second, onionskin carbon copies were less uniform: they could have an eagle watermark or no watermark. Third, most documents sent out by the NSC were produced on White House letterhead. NARA separately notes that during Cutler’s brief absence from the NSC, his carbon copies used “prestige onionskin”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That wording is more careful than some retellings of the controversy. It does **not** say that every surviving Cutler sheet had an eagle watermark. On the contrary, the official account expressly acknowledges unwatermarked onionskin carbons. Nor does it say that every outgoing NSC document used White House letterhead: it says **most** did. Both qualifications are important when assessing how much weight to put on the [Cutler–Twining memo]({{ 'cutler-memo/' | relative_url }})’s physical anomalies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

There is also considerably more Cutler material at the Eisenhower Library than the phrase “representative sample” might suggest to a casual reader. The Library’s finding aid for Robert Cutler’s personal papers describes roughly **13 linear feet and approximately 16,000 pages**, spanning 1895–1974. Those personal papers are not all relevant to his White House stationery practices, but they show the breadth of the archival record available for comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/cutler-robert-papers.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cutler robert papers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential LibraryDWIGHT DNovember 19, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 19, 2020</span></span></span>

More directly relevant is the separate White House Office collection for the Special Assistant for National Security Affairs — covering Cutler, Dillon Anderson and Gordon Gray — which the Library describes as **49 feet** of records. It contains NSC administrative, briefing-note, policy, subject and status-of-projects files, along with chronological, presidential, name and subject subseries. The Library also holds a much larger NSC Staff collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/research/finding-aids/white-house-office-office-special-assistant-national-security-affairs-robert" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential LibraryWHITE HOUSE OFFICE, OFFICE OF THE SPECIAL ASSISTANT FOR NATIONAL SECURITY AFFAIRS (Robert Cutler, Dillon A...</span></span></span>

So the comparison did not arise in an archival vacuum. The Eisenhower Library possesses substantial bodies of material from the office and period against which an anomalous Cutler memorandum could be judged.

## What “representative sample” actually establishes

A representative archival sample is best understood as evidence about **normal practice**, not a census proving universal practice.

If archivists inspect material spread across the relevant office records and repeatedly encounter the same combination of paper, watermark and letterhead practices, that pattern is useful authentication evidence. A questioned document that fits those patterns gains some contextual plausibility; one that does not deserves closer scrutiny. This is especially valuable with mid-century office records because paper type, copy status and stationery can reveal how a document was produced and where it sat in an administrative workflow.

The Cutler comparison therefore supports a fairly specific proposition: **the surviving Cutler–Twining sheet is physically atypical relative to the Library’s sampled Cutler material and to the usual form of outgoing NSC correspondence**. NARA reports that the sheet lacks official government letterhead and a watermark, while its conservator identified it as a [ribbon copy]({{ 'ribbon-copy/' | relative_url }}) on dictation onionskin. At the same time, most outgoing NSC documents were on White House letterhead, and the sampled bond-paper Cutler documents bore an eagle watermark.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The word **ribbon** makes this more significant than a simple observation that an archival copy lacks fancy stationery. A ribbon copy is the sheet directly struck through the typewriter ribbon. A carbon copy, by contrast, could naturally survive on thin onionskin even if an outgoing original had been typed on another stock. NARA explicitly distinguishes the questioned document’s ribbon-copy status from the onionskin **carbon** copies found in Cutler material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That distinction also explains why evidence of genuine unwatermarked Cutler carbons does not completely neutralise the comparison. Such examples demonstrate that **“no watermark equals forgery” is an invalid rule**. They do not demonstrate that a directly typed, unletterheaded ribbon copy on dictation onionskin was normal for an outgoing Cutler memorandum of this kind.

At the same time, the public NARA account leaves important methodological questions unanswered. It calls the Library examination a “representative sample” but does not state how many items were sampled, which boxes supplied them, what dates were represented, how many examples fell into each stationery category, or whether documents addressed specifically to senior military recipients such as Twining were deliberately included.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Those omissions prevent a numerical estimate such as “99 per cent of Cutler memoranda used this paper”. The evidence supports a pattern, not a measured frequency.

## The archival record itself contains variation

The strongest reason not to turn the Library comparison into an absolute rule is that Cutler’s paperwork was demonstrably heterogeneous.

NARA’s present wording acknowledges this directly: onionskin carbon copies in the representative sample could have an eagle watermark **or no watermark at all**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span> That qualification matters because earlier reproductions of correspondence about the case circulated stronger wording suggesting that all relevant Cutler material had an eagle watermark. One online reproduction of a July 1987 National Archives letter, for example, describes the Eisenhower Library as having examined its Cutler collection and states more broadly that Cutler-created documents carried the eagle watermark.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://raids.org/files/ufo2/mj12doc6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raids.org">[raids.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raids.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATIONAL ARCHIVES Washington, DC 20408 Date: July 22, 1987 Reply to attn of: Military RefeJuly 22, 1987...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 1987</span></span></span> The current NARA reference report is more carefully qualified and should be preferred when characterising the agency’s position.

MJ-12 defender Stanton Friedman made precisely this issue part of his rebuttal. In a later article published in a collection of his writings, he said the Library had initially asserted that Cutler’s onionskin copies all carried the eagle watermark but corrected that position after he pointed to Cutler-file examples on onionskin with other or no watermarks. That is an advocate’s account rather than an independent archival report, so it should not simply replace NARA’s evidence. But the important part of the dispute is consistent with NARA’s present wording: genuine Cutler onionskin copies were **not universally watermarked**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span>

Contemporary pro-MJ-12 arguments went further, questioning whether the Eisenhower Library comparison contained enough closely analogous Cutler-to-Twining material. Friedman and William Moore cited two other Cutler-to-Twining memoranda they said they had located at the Library of Congress and argued that such material showed the danger of treating holdings at one repository as the complete universe of Cutler paperwork.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archive.jfkrfkmurdersolved.com/viewtopic.php?p=41017" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archive.jfkrfkmurdersolved.com">[archive.jfkrfkmurdersolved.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archive.jfkrfkmurdersolved.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E REMOVAL OF JFK # 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE REMOVAL OF JFK # 1 - # 41: - JFK RFK MLK Assasination Forum - ArchiveJanuary 2, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2, 2017</span></span></span> This is a legitimate methodological caution even if one does not accept the advocates’ conclusions about MJ-12: presidential-library holdings can be extensive without containing every copy of every document an official produced.

Material later assembled from MJ-12 researcher Bill Moore’s files also records searches through Eisenhower Library holdings in which onionskin carbons without watermarks were encountered, alongside a Cutler-to-Twining document carrying an eagle watermark and a White House stationery memorandum displaying a larger typeface. Because these are researcher notes hosted by the Mutual UFO Network rather than an official Library catalogue, they are weaker evidence than NARA’s own findings. They are nevertheless useful in showing how granular the dispute became and why sweeping claims about one invariant Cutler format proved difficult to sustain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/EISENHOWER-LIBRARY.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">EISENHOWER LIBRARY</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project AquariusMSI PATCH SHEETJune 24, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 24, 2026</span></span></span>

## Where claims of universal office practice go too far

Three increasingly strong claims are sometimes treated as though they were interchangeable, but the archival evidence supports them very differently.

**“The questioned memo is unusual.”** This is well supported. Its combination of unletterheaded dictation onionskin, no watermark and ribbon-copy production differs from the normal patterns reported from the Eisenhower Library comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

**“Cutler normally used different stationery for documents of this kind.”** This is also reasonably supported, particularly because NARA says most material sent out by the NSC used White House letterhead and because the Library found consistent eagle-watermarked bond paper among the sampled Cutler NSC documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

**“Cutler could never have produced a genuine document in the questioned format.”** The sample does not establish that. NARA itself records variation among onionskin carbons, uses the qualified term “most” for outgoing White House-letterhead correspondence and describes the examination as a representative sample rather than an exhaustive item-by-item census.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This is the main critique risk when using the Cutler sample in an authenticity argument. Moving from “we did not observe this form in a representative sample” to “this form was never used” changes an empirical observation into a universal proposition. Unless the archive has been exhaustively surveyed under defined criteria, that inference is too strong.

The converse error is equally important. Finding one authentic unwatermarked Cutler carbon does not make every unwatermarked Cutler-looking document normal. A meaningful comparison has to match **document function, copy status, date, office and intended recipient**, not simply ask whether some sheet somewhere in Cutler’s files lacked a watermark. The questioned Cutler–Twining sheet was identified by NARA as a ribbon copy; examples known to be carbon copies answer only part of that comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## How strong was the Cutler stationery comparison?

Taken on its proper terms, the Eisenhower Library sample is **strong baseline evidence but weak proof of an absolute prohibition**.

Its strength comes from the institutional setting and breadth of the comparison. The Library holds extensive Cutler personal papers and a much larger body of White House national-security records encompassing his office.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/cutler-robert-papers.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cutler robert papers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential LibraryDWIGHT DNovember 19, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 19, 2020</span></span></span> NARA did not merely eyeball a photocopy: its conservation specialist examined the physical sheet, identified its copy type and paper, while the Eisenhower Library compared relevant Cutler material and reported the stationery patterns it found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Its limitation is methodological transparency. The published reference report does not supply the underlying sample list or sample size. Readers therefore cannot independently calculate how common each paper type was, determine precisely how the sample was stratified, or know how many closely analogous Cutler-to-senior-official memoranda were included. Evidence subsequently emphasised by Friedman also confirms a point now incorporated into NARA’s own account: Cutler’s onionskin copies did not follow one universal watermark rule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span>

For [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}), that leaves a narrower but firmer conclusion. **The Cutler–Twining memo fails to match the ordinary stationery pattern documented by the Eisenhower Library sample, and its ribbon-copy status makes the mismatch more interesting than an ordinary unwatermarked file carbon would be. But the sample cannot, by itself, prove that no genuine exception could ever have existed.**

That is why the Cutler sample works best as one component of a cumulative forensic assessment. Used carefully, it shows that the questioned memo is physically anomalous. Used as an exceptionless “Cutler always did X” rule, it asks more of the archival evidence than the published record can support.
