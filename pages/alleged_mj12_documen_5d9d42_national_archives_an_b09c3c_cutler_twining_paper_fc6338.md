## Introduction

The physical paper of the Cutler-[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum is compatible with the broad world of 1950s office supplies, but that is much less decisive than it may sound. The National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) had the original sheet examined by a conservation specialist, who identified it as a ribbon-typed copy on “dictation onionskin”. Onionskin was unquestionably a recognised class of writing paper in US government use, and independent documentation indicates that the particular “Dictation Onionskin” stock associated with the disputed memo was being manufactured by 1953.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_national_archives_an_b09c3c_cutler_twining_paper_fc6338-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Paper Test illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_national_archives_an_b09c3c_cutler_twining_paper_fc6338-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_national_archives_an_b09c3c_cutler_twining_paper_fc6338-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

The difficulty is narrower and more important: the sheet does not closely reproduce the normal stationery pattern that the Eisenhower [Presidential Library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) found among Robert Cutler's authenticated National Security Council (NSC) papers. NARA therefore did not treat period-compatible paper as proof that Cutler's office produced the memorandum. The physical evidence can make a crude anachronism less likely; it cannot establish authorship, official transmission or authorisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

## What NARA found on the physical sheet

NARA's finding came from examination of the actual archival document, not merely the photocopies through which most people encountered the alleged MJ-12 papers. Its conservation specialist determined that the Cutler-Twining sheet was a **[ribbon copy]({{ 'ribbon-copy/' | relative_url }}) prepared on “dictation onionskin”**. In other words, the typing on the surviving sheet was produced directly by a typewriter ribbon rather than being an ordinary carbon impression made underneath another typed sheet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because “onionskin” describes a lightweight, thin writing paper commonly suited to making copies and conserving filing or mailing space; it is not, by itself, the name of some exotic classified-document medium. Federal specifications confirm that onionskin writing paper was already an established US government commodity well before 1954: a federal specification for onionskin paper, UU-P-641a, was recorded in 1946. Thus, the mere presence of onionskin is entirely compatible with mid-century American administrative practice.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB C13 930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Supplement to national directory of commodity specifications...</span></span></span>

There is also evidence bearing specifically on the commercial paper identified by MJ-12 researchers. In a December 1987 letter preserved in William Moore's research files, Fox River Paper Company representative Thomas J. Lison said that the company first manufactured its watermarked **“Dictation Onionskin” in January 1953**. He described it as a registered Fox River [watermark]({{ 'watermark/' | relative_url }}) used for bid business or situations in which the firm's normal mill-watermarked paper could not be supplied through a Fox River merchant. The company's records showed the grade still available in 1974 and gone from its dandy-roll list by 1977.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>

That company letter is useful but needs careful interpretation. Lison said Fox River sold paper both directly and through merchants to the US government on a bid basis; he did **not** document a particular government order for the batch represented by the Cutler-Twining sheet. His wording establishes that this kind of paper existed from the correct period and could have entered government supply channels. It does not demonstrate that the [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}), NSC or Cutler's office purchased the particular sheet in the archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>

This produces the strongest limited conclusion available from the paper itself: **the stock is not obviously too modern for a memorandum dated July 1954**. If the Fox River identification is accepted, production beginning in January 1953 leaves ample chronological room for such paper to have existed when the memorandum was supposedly created. That removes one possible form of anachronism, but nothing more.

## How Cutler's known papers compare

The more revealing test is not “Did onionskin exist in 1954?” but “Does this sheet resemble the papers actually produced by Cutler's office?”

NARA asked the Eisenhower Presidential Library to examine a representative sample of its Robert Cutler material. According to NARA's published MJ-12 reference report, the authenticated documents in that sample created while Cutler served on the NSC staff used **eagle-watermarked bond paper**. The onionskin carbon copies examined had either an eagle watermark or no watermark. NARA further reported that most material sent out by the NSC was prepared on **White House [letterhead]({{ 'letterhead/' | relative_url }})**, while carbon copies from the brief interval when Cutler was away from the NSC used “prestige onionskin”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

That comparison makes the Cutler-Twining sheet atypical rather than physically impossible. NARA specifically noted that the disputed document did not bear official government letterhead or an official government watermark. Its physical form therefore failed to provide the sort of stationery match that could positively connect it with Cutler's normal NSC output.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The distinction between an **outgoing original** and a **retained carbon** sharpens the problem. The Archives described the disputed sheet as a ribbon copy, while its comparison with Cutler's papers showed onionskin appearing among carbon copies. Most outgoing NSC documents, meanwhile, were on White House letterhead. A ribbon-typed onionskin sheet without that letterhead therefore does not fit neatly into either of those documented patterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The comparison should not be overstated into a rule that every authentic Cutler-era sheet had to look identical. NARA itself says that the onionskin carbons in the representative sample could have an eagle watermark **or no watermark at all**. MJ-12 advocate Stanton Friedman later argued that he had found additional Cutler-file onionskins bearing different watermarks or none, challenging stronger versions of the claim that Cutler invariably used one distinctive paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

That rebuttal weakens a simplistic argument — “wrong watermark, therefore fake” — but it does not turn the Cutler-Twining paper into a positive match. The archival comparison is probabilistic. Genuine offices use different batches of stationery; secretaries improvise; copies and originals can be made differently; supplies change. The significant point is that the disputed sheet lacks a stationery feature that independently identifies it as a product of Cutler's office.

The Eisenhower Library's holdings also show why comparison with a documentary population is more useful than judging one sheet in isolation. Its Cutler papers include a National Security Council series containing correspondence, reports, organisational material and other Eisenhower-administration records, while the larger White House Office holdings preserve extensive NSC administrative, chronological, subject and policy files. There is therefore a substantial body of contemporary paperwork against which an alleged Cutler memorandum can be compared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/cutler-robert-papers.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cutler robert papers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential LibraryDWIGHT DNovember 19, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 19, 2020</span></span></span>

## Does “Dictation Onionskin” strengthen the case?

It strengthens one very specific proposition: **someone could have obtained this type of paper in the 1950s**.

The Fox River correspondence is the strongest evidence for that limited point. According to the company's surviving response to Moore, the watermark originated in January 1953. A July 1954 sheet carrying it would therefore be chronologically plausible. The company also acknowledged selling paper into US government procurement channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>

For proponents such as Friedman, this was significant. His argument was essentially that a casual modern hoaxer would have needed to reproduce not just old-looking text but an appropriate piece of period paper. Friedman also challenged the initial impression that Cutler's onionskin papers followed a single watermark pattern, citing examples from archival files that he said used other or unwatermarked onionskin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span>

There are nevertheless two large evidential jumps between those observations and authenticity.

First, **period manufacture is not document manufacture**. A sheet produced in or after 1953 could have remained unused for years or decades. Fox River's own letter explicitly acknowledged that purchasers might retain stocks after a watermark ceased production. Establishing the earliest possible date of the paper is therefore not equivalent to establishing when somebody typed on it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>

Second, **government availability is not NSC provenance**. Fox River's representative said that the company sold to the federal government, but the correspondence located in this research does not identify a purchase order, merchant invoice, White House stationery record or NSC supply record linking “Dictation Onionskin” to Cutler's office in July 1954. The commercial evidence makes government use possible, not demonstrated.

That is why the paper question cannot be reduced to either “NARA found the wrong paper” or “Fox River proved it was government paper”. Neither formulation accurately represents the evidence.

## What period-correct paper can and cannot prove

Paper analysis works particularly well when it discovers a hard chronological contradiction. If a supposedly 1954 document were made on a paper stock first manufactured years later, that would be powerful evidence against the claimed date. Here, the surviving Fox River information points in the opposite direction: “Dictation Onionskin” existed by 1953, so the paper does **not** supply that kind of disqualifying anachronism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>

But passing an earliest-date test is a low evidential bar. It establishes compatibility, not provenance. A useful way to separate the claims is:

* **Could onionskin have been used by the US government in the period?** Yes. Federal specifications show government recognition of onionskin writing paper before the Eisenhower administration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB C13 930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Supplement to national directory of commodity specifications...</span></span></span>
* **Could Fox River “Dictation Onionskin” have existed on 14 July 1954?** Yes. Fox River's later company records placed its introduction in January 1953.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>
* **Could such paper have been sold into government channels?** Yes. Fox River said it conducted US government bid business.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/CUTLER-TWINNING-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius MSI PATCH SHEET</span></span></span>
* **Does the sheet closely match the normal outgoing format documented for Cutler's NSC office?** Not on NARA's comparison. Most outgoing NSC material examined was on White House letterhead, whereas this sheet lacks official letterhead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>
* **Does the paper establish that Cutler, his staff or the NSC produced it?** No. Neither NARA's examination nor the Fox River correspondence supplies that chain of provenance.

The last distinction is the decisive one. A forger, later inserter or unauthorised contemporary writer could in principle use genuine period stationery. Conversely, an authentic government document could conceivably appear on atypical paper. Physical compatibility therefore has to be combined with administrative provenance rather than substituted for it.

That is especially important in the Cutler-Twining case because NARA expressly refuses to make the inference sometimes attached to the sheet's archival location. The Archives says that when it certifies a reproduction, it certifies that the copy corresponds to a document in its custody; **it does not authenticate the document or the information contained in it**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

## The paper test leaves a narrow verdict

On the specific question of whether the Cutler-Twining paper fits 1950s government use, the fairest answer is **partly, but not distinctively**.

The paper passes the broad chronological test. Onionskin was a recognised government writing-paper category before 1954, and the available Fox River company record dates “Dictation Onionskin” to January 1953. Nothing in that evidence requires the physical sheet itself to be a post-1954 invention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-C13-930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400/pdf/GOVPUB-C13-930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB C13 930bb69aa45e1424e3213e95f8413400</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Supplement to national directory of commodity specifications...</span></span></span>

It performs less convincingly when compared with the actual documentary habits of Cutler's NSC office. NARA found no official government letterhead or watermark on the disputed memorandum, while the Eisenhower Library's representative sample showed eagle-watermarked bond for Cutler's NSC documents, eagle-watermarked or unwatermarked onionskin for carbon copies, and White House letterhead for most outgoing NSC documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

Those differences are anomalies, not a laboratory demonstration of forgery. Friedman's identification of greater variation among Cutler onionskins is a legitimate reason not to treat stationery practice as absolutely uniform.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span> But the reverse inference is equally unwarranted: showing that the paper **could** have existed in a government office in 1954 does not show that it **did** come from Cutler's office.

For [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}), that is the enduring significance of the paper test. The Cutler-Twining sheet possesses a physically period-compatible feature, but not a uniquely governmental or Cutler-specific one. Its paper can support a plausible date range; it cannot supply the missing authorship and authorisation that would be needed to turn an old sheet in government custody into an authenticated 1954 National Security Council memorandum.
