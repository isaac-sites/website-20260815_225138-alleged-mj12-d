## Introduction

The presidential-library searches produced a clear archival result: neither the Harry S. Truman Library nor the Dwight D. Eisenhower Library found records matching the alleged Majestic 12 (MJ-12) papers, or an independent documentary trail showing that such a presidential programme existed. The General Accounting Office (GAO) reported in 1995 that archivists had repeatedly searched relevant holdings, including classified intelligence and National Security Council (NSC) material, after years of public enquiries, without finding records fitting the MJ-12 description or references corroborating it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

That finding matters because the disputed papers place MJ-12 unusually close to the presidency: they purport to involve Harry Truman's creation of the organisation and a later briefing for President-elect Dwight Eisenhower. Presidential-era archives were therefore an obvious place to look for corroborating records. The searches did not establish that every conceivable secret record had survived or been examined. They did, however, fail to produce the supporting archival footprint that would help tie the circulating MJ-12 papers to an identifiable executive-branch record system.

## What the Truman and Eisenhower libraries searched

GAO's 28 July 1995 letter on Majestic 12 records that it specifically asked archivists at the Harry S. Truman and Dwight D. Eisenhower presidential libraries for their views on the disputed material. This was not the first time either institution had confronted the subject: according to GAO, the archivists said they had received public enquiries about MJ-12 for years and had searched their holdings for related records. Those searches included classified intelligence material and National Security Council documentation. GAO summarised the result as finding nothing that fitted the description of Majestic 12 and no references to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The National Archives and Records Administration (NARA) gives additional detail about the wider archival effort. It says that the Truman and Eisenhower libraries searched their holdings for both references to MJ-12 and copies of the purported documents. NARA also searched the [NSC records]({{ 'nsc-records/' | relative_url }}) for the Truman and Eisenhower administrations, which are in National Archives [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

This distinction between looking for *copies* and looking for *references* is important. A highly [restricted]({{ 'restricted/' | relative_url }}) original might plausibly be absent from a particular collection. But a search for references also tests for a wider administrative trail: whether the purported project appears in indexes, meeting documentation or related presidential and national-security files even when the supposed foundational papers themselves cannot be located. Across these searches, no corroborating MJ-12 trail emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The searches are particularly relevant to the claims made by the circulating documents themselves. The alleged material places Truman and Eisenhower at critical points in MJ-12's supposed history, so presidential records are not merely peripheral sources. If the papers accurately described presidential authorisation and briefings, the Truman- and Eisenhower-era collections were among the places where independent traces might reasonably have appeared.

## Which records and indexes were checked

The archival work went beyond searching only for the exact phrase “Majestic 12”. NARA says the indexes to the NSC Policy Paper and Meeting Minute files were searched under multiple terms: “MJ-12”, “majestic”, “unidentified flying objects”, “UFO”, “flying saucers”, “extraterrestrial biological entities” and “Aquarius”. Those index searches were negative except for one anomalous document: the July 1954 Cutler–[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum referring to an “NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

That exception did not lead archivists to a larger file series or supporting chain of records. The Cutler–Twining memorandum was found in Air Force Record Group 341, in a series filed by [Top Secret register]({{ 'register-numbers/' | relative_url }}) number, yet the document itself lacked such a number. There were no other “NSC/MJ-12” documents in its folder. Searches of records belonging to the Secretary of Defense, Joint Chiefs of Staff, Headquarters US Air Force and related holdings found no further information, while enquiries to the Air Force, Joint Chiefs and NSC likewise produced nothing further.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The NSC meeting record supplied another check. NARA searched the official NSC meeting-minute files and found no NSC meeting on 16 July 1954, the date on which the Cutler–Twining paper appeared to envisage a briefing. A search of all NSC meeting minutes for July 1954 found no mention of either “MJ-12” or “Majestic”. The NSC record indexes similarly contained no listing under MJ-12, Majestic or several UFO-related terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The Eisenhower Library could test the proposed meeting against a different kind of presidential record: Eisenhower's appointment books. In a 16 July 1987 letter later incorporated into NARA's MJ-12 reference report, the library said those books contained no entry for a special meeting on 16 July 1954 that could correspond to an MJ-12 briefing. This was significant because, according to the library, even Eisenhower's “off the record” meetings normally generated appointment-book entries recording their time and participants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The Eisenhower holdings supplied further contextual checks on the Cutler memorandum. NARA reports that Robert Cutler was travelling through Europe and North Africa on presidential business from 3 to 15 July 1954, including on 14 July, when the disputed memorandum was supposedly issued. The Administration Series in Eisenhower's presidential papers contains Cutler's report following that trip. NSC Staff Papers also contain his 3 July instructions assigning administrative responsibilities to subordinates during his absence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

Archivists also compared features of the disputed memorandum with period records. The NSC's Freedom of Information Office advised that the marking “Top Secret Restricted Information” did not come into NSC use until the Nixon administration, while the Eisenhower Library confirmed that this marking was not used during Eisenhower's presidency. A representative sample of genuine Cutler documents held by the library also provided a comparison for paper, [watermark]({{ 'watermark/' | relative_url }}) and office-document practices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

They did not do so.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

## Why the negative result carries weight

The strongest point is not simply that archivists failed to locate a folder labelled “Majestic 12”. It is that the alleged programme failed to appear across several overlapping record systems associated with the two presidencies in which the disputed documents place its creation and early operation.

For the Truman period, GAO's report says searches of relevant presidential-library material, including classified intelligence and NSC records, produced no matching MJ-12 records or references. For the Eisenhower period, that same general absence could be tested against more specific documentary claims: an alleged July 1954 briefing, Cutler's supposed role in arranging it and the classification and administrative conventions visible on the disputed memorandum. The contemporary records NARA describes did not supply the expected corroboration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The Cutler–Twining memorandum is therefore an instructive exception rather than a straightforward reversal of the negative result. It is a physical document in National Archives custody that contains “MJ-12”, but NARA explicitly distinguishes custody from authentication: certifying a reproduction establishes that it is a true copy of an item held by the Archives, not that the Archives authenticates the document's contents or provenance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

Moreover, the memorandum did not unlock an archival network around itself. It lacked the Top Secret register number expected in its record series; no companion MJ-12 records were present in the folder; relevant NSC indexes and July 1954 minutes were negative; the proposed 16 July meeting did not appear in Eisenhower's appointment books; and searches elsewhere did not produce supporting documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

That is the evidential significance of the library searches within GAO's wider inquiry. A single unexplained item containing the term “MJ-12” existed in archival custody, but investigators could not connect it to the wider presidential and NSC documentary structure that would independently establish the alleged organisation described by the circulating papers.

## What the negative results do and do not prove

The library findings provide substantial evidence against a straightforward claim that the circulated MJ-12 papers are ordinary surviving records of a Truman- and Eisenhower-era executive-branch programme. GAO's broader conclusion was correspondingly careful: it found no evidence that the Majestic 12 written material constituted actual documents originally created within the executive branch. GAO also reported that its work uncovered nothing contradicting the conclusions reached by the agencies it consulted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The searches do **not**, however, logically prove that no secret activity using that name could ever have existed. Negative archival evidence has limits. Records can be destroyed, misfiled, retained outside expected collections, withheld under classification rules or created in ways that leave unusually sparse documentation. A sufficiently compartmented activity might therefore be harder to trace than an ordinary presidential or NSC programme.

But that limitation should not be turned into positive evidence for MJ-12. The historical question is comparative: does the surviving archive supply independent evidence that makes the disputed papers more credible as executive-branch records? In the Truman and Eisenhower collections, the answer reported by GAO was no. Where archivists could test specific details — indexes, meeting records, appointment books, classification terminology and Cutler's whereabouts — the checks either produced nothing or created additional difficulties for the alleged documentary trail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

There is also an important distinction between authentic historical material *about UFOs* and authentic records of *MJ-12*. Modern NARA guides list genuine UFO- and unidentified anomalous phenomena-related holdings in presidential-library collections, including Truman- and Eisenhower-era material. The existence of such records demonstrates that relevant presidential collections do preserve material concerning UFO questions; it does not turn those records into evidence for the particular MJ-12 organisation described in the disputed papers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/topics/uaps/presidential-libraries" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesRecords Related to Unidentified Flying Objects (UFOs) and Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAPs) at the National Archiv...</span></span></span>

The presidential-library evidence is therefore best read as a [provenance test]({{ 'provenance-test/' | relative_url }}) with a negative outcome. Investigators looked where records associated with Truman, Eisenhower, classified intelligence and the National Security Council could reasonably have supplied corroboration. They found no matching presidential MJ-12 record trail. The one conspicuous archival “MJ-12” memorandum remained isolated and surrounded by documentary inconsistencies rather than supported by the surrounding record. That does not prove an absolute historical negative, but it leaves the alleged MJ-12 papers without the independent presidential-archive corroboration that would be expected to strengthen their claimed executive-branch origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>
