## Introduction

The alleged MJ-12 chronology does **not** place President Harry S. Truman’s supposed creation of Majestic-12 immediately after the Roswell announcement. Roswell Army Air Field publicly announced possession of a “flying disc” on **8 July 1947**; the disputed MJ-12 papers date Truman’s supposed authorisation to **24 September 1947**. That is a gap of **78 days — eleven weeks and one day**. Contemporary reporting confirms the July date, while the alleged Eisenhower briefing document supplies the September date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/renogazette8jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947 - 1947 Press, flying disc found in Roswell, The Reno GazetteJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

That interval matters because it changes the mechanism implied by the MJ-12 story. Rather than depicting a presidential committee created within days of Roswell, the documents imply an intervening period in which information could supposedly have moved through military and technical channels before reaching an institutional decision. There really was wider official investigation of “flying discs” during those weeks. What has not been found is reliable evidence showing that this activity constituted an eleven-week Roswell-to-MJ-12 investigation culminating in Truman’s alleged order. Federal archival searches have instead found no executive-branch record establishing Majestic-12 as an authentic organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/news/stories/2010/april/ufos_040610" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FB I — The FBI and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The FBI and UFOsApril 6, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 6, 2010</span></span></span>

## The July 8 Roswell announcement

The useful starting point is **8 July**, rather than treating “Roswell” as a vaguely dated event. Contemporary Associated Press reporting stated that the Army [Air Forces]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) at Roswell had announced possession of a flying disc recovered from a ranch. The statement said the object had first been inspected at Roswell Army Air Field and then sent to “higher headquarters”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/renogazette8jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947 - 1947 Press, flying disc found in Roswell, The Reno GazetteJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

An authentic FBI teletype from the same day shows how quickly the matter entered official communications outside Roswell. According to the later Government Accountability Office (GAO) examination, the FBI’s Dallas office was told by an Eighth Air Force official that the recovered object resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector and was being sent to Wright Field for examination. The FBI records the Bureau now makes available likewise date this communication to 8 July.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

That contemporary trail is significant for the eleven-week question. It establishes that there was immediate military handling and communication concerning the Roswell debris, but it does **not** establish an immediate presidential organisation. Indeed, GAO's later search found only two government records originating in 1947 that specifically mentioned the Roswell event: the July history of the 509th Bomb Group/Roswell Army Air Field and the 8 July FBI teletype. Other government records examined by GAO, including material that had once been classified, did not supply the [missing chain]({{ 'missing-chain/' | relative_url }}) from Roswell to an MJ-12 organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

The Air Force's later investigation offered a conventional explanation for the recovered material, identifying it with the classified balloon-borne Project MOGUL programme rather than an extraterrestrial vehicle. Whatever conclusion a reader reaches about that explanation, it reinforces an important distinction for this particular timeline: **the documented Roswell episode and the alleged September MJ-12 decision are separate historical propositions**. Evidence that officials dealt with something near Roswell in July is not, by itself, evidence that Truman created MJ-12 eleven weeks later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/The-DAF-Legacy-Vault/DAF-History-Publications/igphoto/2003789023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DAF History The Roswell Report: Fact versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF History The Roswell Report: Fact versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert</span></span></span>

## What the MJ-12 narrative puts in the intervening weeks

The disputed Eisenhower briefing document retrospectively connects the July recovery with the September organisation. It describes Majestic-12 as a presidential research and intelligence operation and says Truman established its governing group by a special classified executive order on **24 September 1947**, following recommendations from Vannevar Bush and James [Forrestal]({{ 'forrestal/' | relative_url }}). Its attached purported Truman memorandum authorises Forrestal to proceed with an already discussed “undertaking” and assigns it the name “Operation Majestic Twelve”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

Read purely as a narrative, that wording makes the eleven-week interval look less like dead time than a supposed **pre-authorisation phase**. The alleged memorandum does not read as though Forrestal is being introduced to an entirely new subject on 24 September: it refers to a “recent conversation” and to “your undertaking”. If authentic, those phrases would imply that some planning had preceded the presidential instruction. They do not say when that planning began, how Roswell information supposedly reached Forrestal or Bush, or what happened during the 78 days.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

There is genuine evidence that the US military was investigating the broader flying-disc phenomenon during this period. The FBI says that on **30 July 1947** it instructed field offices that, at the request of Army Air Forces Intelligence, the Bureau had agreed to cooperate in investigations of flying discs. That is useful evidence of a real investigative response during the Roswell-to-September interval — but one concerned with the wider wave of reports, not documentary evidence of MJ-12.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/news/stories/2010/april/ufos_040610" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.fbi.gov">[archives.fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FB I — The FBI and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The FBI and UFOsApril 6, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 6, 2010</span></span></span>

The interval culminated in another genuine document that can make the alleged MJ-12 chronology appear superficially persuasive. On **23 September**, one day before the alleged Truman authorisation, Lieutenant General Nathan [Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) of Air Materiel Command transmitted his formal opinion concerning “flying discs”. The surviving material records the command's assessment that the reported phenomenon was “something real and not visionary or fictitious” and warranted further investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/PURSUE-RELEASE-01/serial/9304cfef-6b9e-4bab-bf5b-7f20fa3a036b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bluebook.one.</span></span></span>

The proximity is striking: Roswell on 8 July, broader investigations during the summer, Twining's memorandum on 23 September, and the alleged MJ-12 order on 24 September. But chronology alone cannot turn those separate events into a documented causal chain. Twining's genuine memorandum concerns flying-disc reports generally; the evidence cited for it does not establish that he was reporting an extraterrestrial Roswell recovery or preparing the ground for Truman to create MJ-12. The distinction between **events that genuinely occurred during the eleven weeks** and **the interpretation later imposed on them by the MJ-12 narrative** is therefore essential.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bluebook.one/case/PURSUE-RELEASE-01/serial/9304cfef-6b9e-4bab-bf5b-7f20fa3a036b" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bluebook.one">[BlueBook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bluebook.one</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on bluebook.one.</span></span></span>

## Why September 24 looks unusually well placed

The strongest coincidence comes at the end of the gap. Truman's authentic appointment calendar shows that at **11:30 a.m. on 24 September 1947** he met jointly with Secretary of Defense James Forrestal and Vannevar Bush. The appointment was arranged at Forrestal's suggestion. Those are precisely the two advisers whom the alleged MJ-12 briefing later names as recommending the group's establishment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_keywords=forrestal&amp;page=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

That makes 24 September a much more interesting alleged date than an arbitrary day eleven weeks after Roswell. It supplies a genuine presidential meeting involving the central figures in the purported authorisation story. The supposed Truman memorandum's reference to a recent conversation also sits neatly beside that historical fact. Yet the authentic appointment entry gives no indication that Roswell, flying discs or Majestic-12 were discussed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_k=&amp;page=75" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

The wider calendar also shows how dangerous it is to treat a plausible meeting as proof of its alleged subject. Truman had a crowded programme that day, including Cabinet business and other appointments. The following morning his schedule recorded Bush's appointment as chairman of the Research and Development Board, placing Bush and Forrestal squarely within the genuine reorganisation of US defence and scientific administration then under way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/calendar/search?body_value_op=word&amp;comment_k=&amp;page=75" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Harry S. Truman Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

In other words, the end of the eleven-week interval contains a **real historical convergence of Truman, Forrestal and Bush**, but no authenticated record connecting their meeting to Roswell. That is exactly the sort of distinction on which the MJ-12 timeline turns: historical circumstances can make an alleged document look contextually convincing without authenticating the extraordinary claim contained in it.

## Why the eleven-week interval changes the timeline

Calling MJ-12 a body supposedly formed “immediately after Roswell” compresses the chronology too far. From 8 July to 24 September, the alleged sequence allows more than two and a half months for whatever investigation, technical assessment, consultation or bureaucratic planning the story requires. If one provisionally accepts the narrative told by the disputed documents, MJ-12 therefore looks less like an emergency committee assembled at the moment debris arrived and more like the supposed institutional outcome of a longer investigative process.

That interpretation also creates an evidential problem. A longer process should potentially leave more opportunities for collateral documentation: routing records, memoranda, briefings, correspondence, scientific assessments, presidential files or references in the records of the officials supposedly involved. The disputed Truman memorandum does not fill those 78 days; it merely appears at their end and alludes to earlier discussion. The alleged 1952 briefing then retrospectively supplies the claim that Truman's September action followed the July recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online library...</span></span></span>

Later government searches have not recovered the documentary bridge that would substantiate that sequence. The National Archives says it conducted extensive searches of Air Force and Joint Chiefs of Staff holdings for the MJ-12 material, while the Truman and Eisenhower presidential libraries also searched their collections for copies or references. GAO reported that federal archivists found nothing fitting the description of Majestic-12 and no evidence that the submitted material originated as genuine executive-branch documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

Some Roswell Army Air Field records from the relevant era were destroyed, and GAO noted deficiencies in the surviving record-disposition documentation. That [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) claims based purely on archival silence. It does not, however, supply affirmative evidence for MJ-12: GAO's extensive search still produced only the two known 1947 records specifically concerning Roswell, neither of which documents an eleven-week progression towards a presidential extraterrestrial-recovery committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

There is an additional credibility problem specific to using the alleged MJ-12 papers as the missing link. When copies of the supposed Eisenhower briefing reached the FBI in 1988, an Air Force investigation concluded that the document was fake; the FBI now describes its own conclusion in similarly unequivocal terms. GAO subsequently reported that the agencies and archives it consulted possessed no evidence that the MJ-12 material constituted authentic executive-branch documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=Majestic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span></span></span>

## The gap is real; the alleged chain is not established

The **eleven-week gap itself is straightforward chronology**. Roswell's public “flying disc” announcement occurred on 8 July 1947. The disputed MJ-12 documents place Truman's supposed authorisation on 24 September. Between those dates, the historical record does show genuine official interest in flying-disc reports, including FBI cooperation with Army Air Forces Intelligence and, at the end of the period, Twining's 23 September assessment. On 24 September, Truman genuinely met Forrestal and Bush.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archives.fbi.gov/archives/news/stories/2010/april/ufos_040610" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">FB I — The FBI and UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The FBI and UFOsApril 6, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 6, 2010</span></span></span>

What the surviving evidence does **not** demonstrate is that those events formed successive stages of a Roswell investigation culminating in MJ-12. That connective mechanism comes principally from the disputed documents themselves. The authenticated records establish interesting pieces around the alleged chronology; they do not establish the alleged organisation linking them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The most accurate description, therefore, is not that Truman supposedly created MJ-12 “immediately after Roswell”. **The allegation places its creation 78 days later.** That longer interval makes the story more bureaucratically elaborate and gives its chronology some intriguing intersections with genuine 1947 events. At the same time, it makes the absence of an authenticated documentary trail through those intervening eleven weeks more important, because the alleged September order is presented as the culmination of a process whose crucial Roswell-to-[White-House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) steps have never been independently established.
