## Introduction

The roughly 3.6 per cent size difference between Harry Truman's signature on the alleged 24 September 1947 Truman–[Forrestal]({{ 'forrestal/' | relative_url }}) MJ-12 memorandum and his signature on an authentic 1 October 1947 letter to Vannevar Bush does **not** by itself defeat the copying hypothesis. The key reason is mechanical: photocopying can change scale, and repeated copying can accumulate small distortions. Modern forensic research confirms that photocopiers can alter both the size and shape of handwriting, particularly across successive generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journal.asqde.org/articles/10.69525/jasqde.237" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journal.asqde.org">[JASQDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journal.asqde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2017</span></span></span>

That does not mean Philip J. Klass's original numerical explanation — roughly three photocopying stages at about 1.2 per cent enlargement each — should be treated as a universal law of photocopiers. It was a proposed reconstruction of this particular discrepancy. The stronger point is narrower: once reproduction-induced scaling is allowed for, a few per cent difference in overall dimensions is not evidence that two otherwise closely corresponding signature images must have independent handwritten origins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

## How large was the reported difference?

Klass's 1990 account put the central discrepancy plainly. He reported that the Truman signature on the alleged MJ-12 memorandum was approximately **3.6 per cent longer** than the signature on the genuine Truman–Bush letter. He initially attributed that difference to optical distortion arising during the several copying operations that, in his reconstruction, would have been required to manufacture the disputed document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

The difference was real enough to become an important part of the defence against Klass's copying argument. William L. Moore wrote to Klass in September 1989 insisting that the signatures were not identical. Moore supplied measurements of individual features: for example, he gave the cross-stroke of the capital "T" as 48.8 mm on his enlarged measurement of the Truman–Forrestal specimen versus 47.1 mm on the Truman–Bush specimen, while his measurement from the top of the "r" to the top of the final "n" was 29.7 mm versus 28.6 mm. He also argued that parts of the signatures differed in line weight and positioning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

Those measurements established an important qualification: an unadjusted overlay of the two available reproductions did not simply produce a perfect one-to-one match at their existing sizes. [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}) similarly wrote that he could not make the September 24 and October 1 signatures match perfectly on his light table.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

But that observation does not settle whether one image was derived from the other. Both sides were working with reproductions rather than two pristine original ink signatures. Moore himself stressed that his Truman–Forrestal specimen was a print from the original negative while the Truman–Bush comparison was a first-generation copy; he believed Klass's copies were still further removed from the originals. In other words, the very evidence offered to show a dimensional mismatch also documented a reproduction chain capable of introducing dimensional differences.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

## Why photocopying can alter image scale

A photocopier is not necessarily a geometrically perfect camera producing an exact 100.000 per cent replica of every mark on the platen. Klass reported being told by the document examiner he consulted that contemporary copiers commonly omitted a narrow outer border and compensated through a small enlargement. Klass gave a figure of roughly **1.2 per cent per copying operation**. On that model, about three copying stages could account for a signature approximately 3.6 per cent larger than its proposed source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

The arithmetic should be understood as approximate. Three exact 1.2 per cent enlargements compound to about 3.64 per cent rather than exactly 3.6 per cent:

1.012 × 1.012 × 1.012 ≈ 1.0364.

That happens to sit remarkably close to the reported discrepancy. It made Klass's reconstruction mechanically plausible, but it did **not** prove that precisely three copier generations occurred, nor that every copier involved enlarged images by precisely 1.2 per cent.

Later questioned-document research provides stronger support for the underlying mechanism than for that exact historical reconstruction. Robert Gervais's 2017 peer-reviewed study for the *Journal of the American Society of Questioned Document Examiners* tested handwriting through as many as twenty successive copying generations on different analogue and digital machines. The study found changes in both **size and shape**, with the magnitude and direction depending upon the machine and even the position of material on the platen. Some areas could enlarge while others reduced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journal.asqde.org/articles/10.69525/jasqde.237" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journal.asqde.org">[JASQDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journal.asqde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2017</span></span></span>

This distinction matters for the MJ-12 dispute. Photocopy distortion need not behave as a perfectly uniform "zoom". Horizontal and vertical magnification can differ, and local distortions can accumulate. Material in the Moore correspondence likewise recognised that copying devices could have differing horizontal and vertical magnifications, so measurements of differently angled parts of a signature need not all yield precisely the same scale ratio.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

Successive copying also changes line quality. A 2021 study of 2,550 genuine signature samples followed through five photocopying generations found progressive effects including line widening, merging, breaks and loss or alteration of fine stroke characteristics; distortion became more troublesome in later generations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journal.asqde.org/articles/279?_rsc=f6n96" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journal.asqde.org">[JASQDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journal.asqde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">December 1, 2021...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 1, 2021</span></span></span> This is relevant because the MJ-12 argument concerns not simply the dimensions of a clean digital image but reproductions whose history included photography and copying.

The important forensic lesson is therefore not that "all photocopiers enlarge by 1.2 per cent". They plainly do not. It is that **small dimensional differences cannot safely be interpreted as proof of independent handwriting unless the reproduction history has been controlled**. Gervais specifically warns that in suspected cut-and-paste or traced-signature cases, photocopies of unknown generation can make a straightforward superimposition look less convincing even where the images are related.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journal.asqde.org/articles/10.69525/jasqde.237" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journal.asqde.org">[JASQDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journal.asqde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2017</span></span></span>

## What happened when the signatures were rescaled?

This is where the 3.6 per cent objection loses much of its force. According to Klass, the professional document examiner he consulted made transparencies of the two signatures and enlarged the authentic October 1 Truman–Bush signature by approximately **3.6 per cent** before superimposing it on the disputed MJ-12 signature. Klass reported that the examiner then characterised the MJ-12 specimen as a "classic signature transplant" — in other words, a signature image reproduced from another document rather than a separately written signature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

The reported comparison went beyond overall length. Klass had already drawn attention to a small extraneous mark associated with the "H" in "Harry", which appeared in corresponding positions in both specimens. His examiner also focused on the top of the "T" in "Truman". In the genuine Bush letter, that stroke intersected part of the typed closing "Sincerely yours". According to Klass's account, the corresponding portion in the disputed signature appeared slightly thinned, consistent with somebody removing the surrounding typed words with correction material while retaining the signature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

That feature is more significant than the raw 3.6 per cent measurement because it concerns the proposed **history of the image**. A copied signature can become larger or smaller during reproduction while preserving distinctive internal relationships between its strokes. If material adjoining the source signature has also apparently been removed at exactly the place where it intersected the handwriting, the hypothesis becomes not merely "these signatures look alike" but "one reproduced image may have been prepared from the other".

The contemporary debate nevertheless contained genuine disagreement over how convincing the match was. Moore maintained that the later portions of "Truman", especially the "man" and final "n", showed discrepancies and argued that multiple photocopying normally degraded fine lines. He also disputed Klass's interpretation of the small mark near the "H", citing other Truman signatures with superficially similar marks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

Bruce Maccabee's analysis likewise became part of the dispute over whether dimensional ratios established non-identity. A subsequent critical commentary argued that comparing individual segments without adequately correcting for potentially different horizontal and vertical magnification factors was methodologically unreliable. The disagreement therefore shifted from the simple observation that the signatures differed in size to the harder question of whether those differences exceeded what their separate reproduction histories could produce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

By May–June 1990, the *International UFO Reporter* was publishing sharply divided assessments of the broader MJ-12 controversy, but one contributor explicitly accepted Klass's conclusion that the Truman–Forrestal signature was a photocopy forgery derived from a genuine document. That contemporary response shows that the copying interpretation was not confined to Klass's own publication, although it remained disputed by MJ-12 defenders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/IUR%20issues/IUR%20Vol.%2015%20No.%203%20May-June%201990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies I nternational UF O Reporter</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesI nternational UF O ReporterNovember 2, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2, 2025</span></span></span>

## Does the signature size difference weaken the case?

It weakens only the **simplest** version of the copying claim: that two photocopies placed together at their existing dimensions should overlay perfectly without adjustment. Moore and Friedman were correct that the specimens they examined did not do that.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

It does much less damage to the more realistic claim that the Truman–Forrestal signature image descended from the Truman–Bush signature through a reproduction and paste-up process. There are three reasons.

First, the disputed difference is small — about 3.6 per cent — rather than a wholesale change in proportions. Second, both the historical correspondence and modern forensic experiments establish that photocopying can introduce dimensional distortion, and repeated generations can compound it. Third, after compensating for the reported scale difference, Klass's examiner said the signatures could be superimposed closely enough to identify what he considered evidence of transplantation at the "T".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

The modern research also suggests caution about making the historical reconstruction too neat. It would be an overstatement to say that a 3.6 per cent difference **proves** three successive enlargements of 1.2 per cent. Copier distortions vary by machine, generation, orientation and location on the platen. Gervais's experiments expressly found both enlargement and reduction, as well as non-linear distortion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journal.asqde.org/articles/10.69525/jasqde.237" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journal.asqde.org">[JASQDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journal.asqde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">June 1, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1, 2017</span></span></span>

The defensible conclusion is therefore narrower but stronger. **The 3.6 per cent size difference is not a reliable objection to common image ancestry.** It is compatible with a signature that passed through several reproductive stages, and the fact that the two images become much more closely comparable after scale correction is precisely what would be expected if copying altered the dimensions of an otherwise related image.

Within the Truman–Forrestal signature dispute, the decisive question is consequently not whether the two available reproductions have exactly the same millimetre measurements. It is whether their distinctive stroke structure and apparent editing artefacts remain correlated once plausible reproduction distortion is taken into account. On that narrower question, the small size mismatch does not rescue the alleged MJ-12 memorandum from the signature-transplant objection; it is a feature that photocopy mechanics can plausibly explain.
