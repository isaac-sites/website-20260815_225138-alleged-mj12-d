## Introduction

The four dead occupants are one of the sharpest dividing lines between the Roswell story documented in July 1947 and the much more elaborate account presented by the alleged MJ-12 Eisenhower briefing. The briefing says aerial reconnaissance found four small, human-like beings roughly two miles from the main wreckage, all dead and badly decomposed, after which a special scientific team removed them for study.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

Nothing comparable appears in the surviving contemporary Roswell material identified by later archival searches. The July 1947 newspaper coverage describes debris, its recovery by military personnel and the Army's rapidly changing explanation; an authentic FBI teletype describes a balloon and radar reflector. Neither mentions bodies, biological remains, a separate occupant site or a scientific recovery team. The National Archives says the subsequent government search found no record that even hinted at recovered alien bodies or extraterrestrial material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ARoswell_Daily_Record._July_8%2C_1947._RAAF_Captures_Flying_Saucer_On_Ranch_in_Roswell_Region._Full_front_page.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Roswell Daily Record</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Roswell Daily Record. July 8, 1947. RAAF Captures Flying Saucer On Ranch in Roswell Region. Full front page.jpg - W...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

That absence does not prove that every relevant 1947 record survived. Some Roswell Army Air Field records from the period were destroyed. But it creates a substantial evidential problem for the MJ-12 account: its most extraordinary Roswell detail appears without contemporary corroboration and resembles a body-recovery tradition that entered the published Roswell story decades later.

## What the briefing claims about the occupants

The alleged briefing does not make a vague reference to rumours of casualties. It supplies a remarkably specific sequence. A secret recovery operation supposedly began on 7 July 1947 after a rancher reported the wreckage. During the operation, aerial reconnaissance allegedly discovered that four small, human-like occupants had apparently left or been ejected from the craft before it exploded. Their bodies were said to have landed about two miles east of the principal wreckage site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

The document adds forensic-sounding details. All four beings were supposedly dead, with decomposition attributed to predators and exposure during approximately a week in the open. A special scientific team then allegedly assumed responsibility for removing them for examination. The wreckage itself was dispersed to several locations, while civilian and military witnesses were supposedly debriefed and the weather-balloon explanation supplied to reporters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

These details matter because they transform Roswell from a disputed debris recovery into a claimed biological event. If authentic, the briefing would imply an operation involving several activities beyond anything needed merely to collect wreckage: aerial searching, discovery of a second location, handling of four corpses, specialist scientific personnel, transport and preservation of biological material, and presumably some subsequent chain of [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}).

Yet the provenance of the briefing itself gives no independent contemporary support to that narrative. The papers attributed to MJ-12 surfaced decades after Roswell. An account of their provenance records that television producer Jaime Shandera received the Eisenhower briefing on undeveloped 35 mm film in December 1984. The FBI later described the purported Operation Majestic-12 material it received as a claimed briefing concerning an extraterrestrial recovery and says an [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) investigation determined the document to be fake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/documents/document-sources/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[Majestic Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic Documents Document Sources &#124; Majestic Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic DocumentsDocument Sources &#124; Majestic DocumentsOctober 29, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2018</span></span></span>

The National Archives likewise reports extensive searches for MJ-12 documentation in Air Force, Joint Chiefs of Staff, [presidential-library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) and National Security Council holdings without finding a supporting documentary trail. Its investigation of the related Cutler-[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum found numerous archival anomalies and no further records establishing an MJ-12 organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The four bodies therefore cannot be treated as a contemporary Roswell record simply because the briefing bears a 1952 date. The evidential question is when the information can actually be demonstrated to have existed. For this particular document, the physical trail leads to the 1980s, not securely back to 1947 or 1952.

## What the July 1947 record does — and does not — contain

The contrast is clearest when the MJ-12 description is placed alongside reporting produced while the Roswell episode was actually unfolding.

On 8 July 1947, the *Roswell Daily Record* reported the announcement from the intelligence office of the 509th Bombardment Group that the field had obtained a “flying saucer”. Its account described a rancher contacting the sheriff, Major Jesse Marcel becoming involved and the recovered object being sent onwards to higher headquarters. The article says no details of the disc were revealed. It says nothing about dead occupants, aerial discovery of bodies or a second recovery site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ARoswell_Daily_Record._July_8%2C_1947._RAAF_Captures_Flying_Saucer_On_Ranch_in_Roswell_Region._Full_front_page.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Roswell Daily Record</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Roswell Daily Record. The following day's reporting became considerably more concrete. Rancher W. W. “Mac” Brazel described scattered rubber and lightweight material — tinfoil, paper, tape and sticks — spread across an area roughly 200 yards in diameter. He estimated that the collected material weighed about five pounds and reported no engine-like metal or propellers. Whatever one makes of Brazel's insistence that the material did not resemble weather balloons he had previously found, his account is fundamentally a debris-field story. It contains no occupants and no indication that he had encountered a vehicle containing bodies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/roswelldailyrecord9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: The Roswell 1947 incident in the Press, The Roswell Daily Record, July 9, 1947July 9, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 1947</span></span></span>

An independent government document from the same news cycle also survives. The General Accounting Office — now the Government Accountability Office (GAO) — authenticated an FBI teletype dated 8 July 1947. It recorded information from Eighth Air Force headquarters describing the recovered object as a hexagonal-shaped disc suspended from a large balloon by cable. Again, the message discusses an object and its transfer for examination, not casualties or biological material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

The point is not that every contemporary source agrees about what the debris was. They plainly do not: the Army's initial “flying disc” announcement, Brazel's doubts about an ordinary weather balloon and the military's balloon explanation created the contradiction that later made Roswell so durable. What the surviving sources *do* share is equally important for this narrower question. The dispute in July 1947 concerns **debris and its identification**. The four-body narrative is absent.

That distinction remained significant when federal researchers revisited the records decades later. The National Archives says the Air Force's systematic search and interviews found no records that “indicated or even hinted” at recovered alien bodies or extraterrestrial material. It also notes that Project Blue Book's holdings contain no documentation discussing the 1947 Roswell incident itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

## The second recovery site changes the evidential burden

The phrase “four bodies” can obscure an even more consequential part of the MJ-12 claim: they supposedly were **not at the main wreckage site**. The briefing places them about two miles east of it, where aerial reconnaissance allegedly discovered them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span>

That effectively adds another event to Roswell. There would have to be a debris recovery and a separate biological recovery. The second location would potentially involve different witnesses, search personnel, transport arrangements and physical traces. The briefing's own wording implies an organised reconnaissance effort rather than four bodies accidentally encountered while debris was being collected.

This is difficult to reconcile with the shape of the contemporary reporting. Brazel's 9 July interview goes into surprisingly mundane physical detail: the approximate diameter of the debris field, bundles of rubber and other material, its estimated weight, tape, paper, sticks, eyelets and the absence of an engine or propellers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/roswelldailyrecord9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: The Roswell 1947 incident in the Press, The Roswell Daily Record, July 9, 1947July 9, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 1947</span></span></span> Yet neither that account nor the previous day's report supplies any contemporary clue that military personnel had simultaneously discovered four corpses only a short distance from another Roswell-related wreckage location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ARoswell_Daily_Record._July_8%2C_1947._RAAF_Captures_Flying_Saucer_On_Ranch_in_Roswell_Region._Full_front_page.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Roswell Daily Record</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Roswell Daily Record. July 8, 1947. RAAF Captures Flying Saucer On Ranch in Roswell Region. Full front page.jpg - W...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Nor does the FBI's 8 July communication betray an escalation from unusual wreckage to a biological-security event. It records a balloon-like device being sent for examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span> That silence is especially relevant because the MJ-12 briefing presents the bodies not as an uncertain rumour but as a central discovery requiring a specialist scientific team.

None of these documents can establish that no secret second operation could possibly have occurred. Secret programmes can generate deliberately incomplete public accounts. The more defensible conclusion is narrower: **the alleged second site has no support in the identified contemporary records cited for the Roswell episode**. Its existence therefore depends on much later evidence rather than on documentation demonstrably produced during the incident.

## The record gap is real, but it does not supply the missing bodies

There is an important complication. GAO's 1994–95 investigation found that some Roswell Army Air Field records covering the relevant period had been destroyed. Administrative records covering March 1945 to December 1949 and outgoing messages covering October 1946 to December 1949 were gone. The disposal documentation did not properly state the authority under which the destruction occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

That finding is sometimes tempting to treat as evidence of concealment, but GAO reported additional context: the responsible archivist said many Air Force organisational records from that period had been destroyed without proper citations to disposal authority, and GAO's examination of other records-control forms supported that explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

For the four-body claim, this distinction is essential. Missing records create uncertainty about what the complete Roswell administrative file once contained. They do **not** provide affirmative evidence that the destroyed material concerned alien bodies. The surviving evidence still includes contemporary press reports, the FBI teletype and other records examined by investigators, none of which supplies the missing biological-recovery story. GAO itself characterised its task as a search for government records concerning Roswell; it did not report finding documentary evidence of extraterrestrial occupants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

A sound evidence assessment therefore has to hold two propositions together. The surviving official record is incomplete, so claims that “every possible 1947 document survives” would be unjustified. But the incompleteness cannot legitimately be reversed into proof that the missing documents contained the very extraordinary evidence under dispute.

## The body story appears in the later Roswell tradition

The chronology becomes more revealing when the 1947 sources are compared with the revival of Roswell decades later. The incident attracted relatively little sustained attention until UFO researchers returned to it in the late 1970s. Charles Berlitz and William L. Moore's *The Roswell Incident*, published in 1980, helped establish the modern crash-retrieval story; even its table of contents advertised a chapter dealing with a crashed UFO and the dead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Roswell_Incident.html?id=2zVGAAAAYAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books The Roswell Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Books The Roswell Incident</span></span></span>

One influential strand involved the posthumous story attributed to civil engineer Barney Barnett, who was said to have encountered an unusual crashed object and small bodies alongside an archaeological party. Later Roswell narratives accumulated additional alleged body witnesses and alternative crash locations. A retrospective account in *Wired*, drawing on the history of the case, notes how the story expanded from the original debris episode into multiple crash sites and recovered occupants as researchers pursued testimony decades after 1947.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/roswell-aliens-fermi-paradox" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Here&#x27;s the Proof There&#x27;s No Government Alien Conspiracy Around Roswell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Here&#x27;s the Proof There&#x27;s No Government Alien Conspiracy Around Roswell</span></span></span>

That chronology does not by itself prove that whoever created the MJ-12 briefing borrowed from *The Roswell Incident* or another particular source. There is no secure documentary trail establishing exactly where its author obtained the four-body detail. But it does change the evidential significance of the claim.

By the time the alleged Eisenhower briefing surfaced on film in 1984, a published Roswell tradition involving recovered bodies already existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/documents/document-sources/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[Majestic Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic Documents Document Sources &#124; Majestic Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic DocumentsDocument Sources &#124; Majestic DocumentsOctober 29, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2018</span></span></span> The document therefore did not reveal the general idea of dead Roswell occupants before that idea was publicly available. For authentication purposes, that is crucial: information already circulating in books and UFO research cannot function as a strong independent prediction of facts subsequently confirmed from contemporary archives.

There is also a revealing difference between the developing folklore and the briefing's precision. Later Roswell accounts have disagreed about locations, witnesses, numbers and circumstances. The alleged briefing resolves that messy tradition into a clean official-sounding narrative: four beings, two miles from the wreckage, roughly a week of exposure, aerial reconnaissance and a special scientific team.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span> Precision can make a document sound authoritative, but historical precision is valuable only when it can be traced to independent evidence.

## What later official explanations can and cannot establish

The US Air Force eventually addressed the body stories directly. Its 1997 *Roswell Report: Case Closed* argued that at least some later memories of strange bodies in the New Mexico desert could have resulted from anthropomorphic test dummies dropped from high-altitude balloons, along with memories of military accidents, compressed retrospectively into the 1947 Roswell story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo92195" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D301 PURL gpo92195</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GOVPUB D301 PURL gpo92195</span></span></span>

That explanation has an obvious limitation: the dummy drops occurred years after 1947. It therefore cannot mean that test dummies literally lay beside the Roswell debris in July 1947. The Air Force's argument instead concerns the **formation of later witness memories** — that events from different years could have become combined after decades had passed. This distinction matters because presenting the dummy programme as a direct 1947 explanation would overstate what the official report claims.

For the MJ-12 briefing, however, the more fundamental problem comes earlier than the debate over dummies. The briefing needs independent reason to believe that four bodies were recovered in 1947 in the first place. The surviving July documentation supplies none. Later testimony may be evaluated on its individual merits, and the Air Force's retrospective explanations may themselves be debated, but neither changes the absence of a contemporary four-body record.

## Where the four bodies leave the MJ-12 claim

The four occupants are therefore not merely another colourful detail in the alleged MJ-12 papers. They provide a useful test of how the briefing relates to the historical record.

The briefing gives the bodies an exact number, approximate location, physical condition and recovery procedure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span> Contemporary Roswell reporting instead documents a debris recovery and a dispute about what the material represented. Brazel's detailed account discusses rubber, foil, paper, tape and sticks; the FBI teletype describes a balloon-and-reflector configuration; neither provides a body story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/roswelldailyrecord9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: The Roswell 1947 incident in the Press, The Roswell Daily Record, July 9, 1947July 9, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 1947</span></span></span> Later archival searches likewise found no records pointing to recovered extraterrestrial occupants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

There is a genuine archival caveat because some Roswell Army Air Field records from the period were destroyed. But an evidential gap is not evidence of what once occupied that gap.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://irp.fas.org/gao/nsi95187.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: irp.fas.org">[Intelligence Resource Program]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">irp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Intelligence Resource Program GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Intelligence Resource ProgramGAO - Government Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico...</span></span></span>

Most importantly for assessing the alleged MJ-12 briefing, stories of dead Roswell occupants were already in public circulation before the document surfaced in the mid-1980s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Roswell_Incident.html?id=2zVGAAAAYAAJ" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books The Roswell Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Books The Roswell Incident</span></span></span> The four-body passage consequently does not behave like a securely authenticated secret record that disclosed an otherwise unknown 1947 fact later confirmed from independent contemporary evidence. It behaves as an extraordinary addition to Roswell for which the surviving contemporary record supplies no corroborating trail.

That does not resolve every argument about what happened near Roswell. It does establish the narrower point that matters here: **the four dead humanoids and their separate recovery site belong to the later Roswell narrative, not to the surviving documentary record produced during the events of July 1947.**

    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
