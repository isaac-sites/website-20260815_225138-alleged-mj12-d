## Introduction

The National Archives and Records Administration’s failed searches for MJ-12 matter because archivists did not simply look for another copy of the disputed Cutler-[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum. They looked for the independent administrative trail that a real “NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project” would reasonably be expected to leave across the institutions named or implied by the document: the National Security Council (NSC), Department of Defense, Joint Chiefs of Staff, [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) headquarters and the Truman and Eisenhower presidential collections. Those searches produced no corroborating MJ-12 record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That is stronger evidence than saying that nobody has yet found a second copy. Meeting minutes, indexes, presidential schedules, classified records and related agency files provide separate opportunities for a programme to leave traces. In MJ-12's case, those routes repeatedly led nowhere. The result does not logically prove that no secret activity of any kind could have existed. It does, however, substantially weaken the narrower historical claim that the surviving MJ-12 papers describe a genuine interagency project operating under that designation and through the official machinery they invoke.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Which archives and agencies did NARA check?

NARA's reference report describes a search considerably wider than the Air Force folder containing the Cutler-Twining sheet. National Archives researchers examined records of the **Secretary of Defense, Joint Chiefs of Staff and Headquarters US Air Force**, along with other related files, without finding further information about the supposed project. NARA also made enquiries to the Air Force, Joint Chiefs and National Security Council; those enquiries likewise produced no additional MJ-12 information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The presidential record was another independent test. The Truman and Eisenhower libraries searched their holdings for references to, or copies of, the MJ-12 material. The importance of these collections is easy to underestimate. Eisenhower-era national-security holdings include NSC policy papers, briefing papers, formal minutes, agendas, records of actions, correspondence, memoranda and reports. In other words, these are precisely the sorts of records in which the administrative consequences of a presidential-level national-security project might surface even if its central operational files remained classified or compartmented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/iwg/declassified-records/presidential-libraries/eisenhower.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesHISTORICAL MATERIALS IN THE DWIGHT D. EISENHOWERDecember 7, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 2010</span></span></span>

The searches were not limited to the exact string “MJ-12”, either. NARA says the NSC Policy Paper and Meeting Minute indexes were searched under terms including **MJ-12, majestic, unidentified flying objects, UFO, flying saucers, extraterrestrial biological entities and Aquarius**. The results were negative apart from the already-disputed July 1954 Cutler-Twining memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction is crucial. A search only for “MJ-12” would be vulnerable to the objection that an ultra-secret programme might have used another name. Searching related subject terms does not eliminate that possibility, but it makes the archival test less dependent upon one alleged code name.

The later General Accounting Office — now the Government Accountability Office (GAO) — review provides a useful independent check on NARA's conclusion. In 1995, responding to a congressional request, GAO reported that the Information Security Oversight Office, Air Force and National Archives found no records in their files relating to Majestic 12. Archivists at both the Truman and Eisenhower libraries told GAO that searches including classified intelligence and NSC material had likewise produced nothing fitting the description of the MJ-12 papers or referring to that designation. GAO said it found nothing contradicting the agencies' conclusion that there was no evidence the circulated MJ-12 material consisted of genuine executive-branch documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

That later review matters because it turns the problem from one unsuccessful NARA search into a repeated pattern. The supposed project is invoked in documents as a high-level government operation, yet searches in several of the institutional settings where its administrative footprint might reasonably survive failed to uncover one.

## What the NSC record is missing

The National Security Council records provide the most revealing negative evidence because the Cutler-Twining memorandum does not merely contain the letters “MJ-12”. Its subject is an “NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project”, and it tells General Nathan Twining that a previously arranged briefing would be held on **16 July 1954**. That creates specific propositions that can be checked against contemporary [NSC records]({{ 'nsc-records/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

NARA searched the official NSC Meeting Minute files and found **no NSC meeting on 16 July 1954**. A search of all NSC meeting minutes for July 1954 found no reference to either “MJ-12” or “Majestic”. The branch responsible for NSC records also searched its indexes without finding listings for MJ-12, Majestic or several UFO-related terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This is particularly notable because a genuine NSC meeting took place one day earlier. The State Department's published *Foreign Relations of the United States* record contains the memorandum of discussion for the **206th meeting of the National Security Council on 15 July 1954**. It identifies Eisenhower as presiding and records an extensive roster that included senior defence, intelligence, Joint Chiefs and NSC officials. The existence of this detailed record illustrates that NSC business in precisely this period generated a substantial documentary structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v01p2/d304" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the HistorianJuly 15, 1954...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 1954</span></span></span>

The absence is therefore more specific than “NARA found nothing about UFOs”. The archival record successfully preserves and identifies ordinary high-level NSC activity surrounding the alleged MJ-12 briefing date, while failing to supply the parallel administrative evidence that would connect the [Cutler-Twining memo]({{ 'cutler-memo/' | relative_url }}) to an actual NSC/MJ-12 project.

The presidential schedule supplies another check. The Eisenhower Library told NARA that the President's appointment books contained no entry for a special meeting on 16 July that might correspond to an MJ-12 briefing. The library considered that significant because even Eisenhower's off-the-record meetings normally generated entries giving a time and participants. NARA's current Eisenhower Library resources confirm that presidential appointment books survive for July 1954 as part of the documentary record of Eisenhower's meetings and activities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

None of this establishes that the memorandum necessarily contemplated a full NSC meeting or that Eisenhower personally had to attend. Its wording refers to a briefing, so the absence of a presidential appointment cannot by itself disprove the event. The more important point is cumulative: **no meeting-minute reference, no useful index entry and no corresponding presidential schedule entry joins the memorandum to the surrounding NSC record**.

There is an additional archival mismatch. The Eisenhower Library reported that the NSC Declassification Office had no record of declassification action involving the Cutler-Twining memorandum or other papers from the alleged project. That matters because the disputed sheet presents itself as material belonging to a classified national-security system, yet NARA could not reconstruct the expected documentary history by following the NSC's own records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why independent traces matter more than another MJ-12 paper


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The strongest way to test a disputed historical document is often not to scrutinise the document indefinitely, but to ask whether facts implied by it appear independently elsewhere.

</div>

A genuine interagency project can produce records that never state its entire secret purpose. There may be meeting arrangements, routing slips, registers, budget references, security instructions, correspondence between participating offices, appointment entries, records of actions, declassification decisions or later references to earlier decisions. Some can disappear while others survive because copies are dispersed among different offices.

MJ-12 is therefore unusual not simply because its documentation is sparse, but because the surviving claim is institutionally ambitious while its independently verifiable footprint is exceptionally weak. The Cutler-Twining memorandum invokes the NSC and addresses the Air Force Chief of Staff; the wider MJ-12 story invokes presidential authority, defence and intelligence officials. Yet the searches reported by NARA crossed several of those institutional boundaries without locating corroboration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

GAO's 1995 findings sharpen the distinction between **government possession of MJ-12 material** and **government creation of MJ-12 material**. Agencies certainly possessed copies by then, because members of the public had submitted them and requested authentication. But GAO found that several agencies' knowledge of MJ-12 was limited to material supplied by non-governmental people. That reverses a potentially misleading inference: finding an MJ-12 paper in a later government FOIA or investigative file does not establish that the government originated the paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The same problem appeared when GAO investigated a separate 17 November 1980 message containing the words “MJ Twelve”. The message appeared to originate with the Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI), but AFOSI could find no official record copy in its files and reported that an earlier authenticity examination had concluded it was a forgery. The episode illustrates why archival corroboration matters: official-looking formatting or terminology becomes much stronger evidence only when it connects to the originating agency's own record system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The FBI reached a similar conclusion concerning the better-known “Operation Majestic-12” material it received in 1988. Its present archival description says that an Air Force investigation determined the document to be fake. That is separate from NARA's searches, but it reinforces the central archival problem: attempts to trace purported MJ-12 documents back into the government systems from which they supposedly emerged did not establish an authentic official lineage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/unexplained-phenomenon//search" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI — Federal Bureau of Investigation...</span></span></span>

## How much weight should archival silence carry?

A missing documentary trail is evidence, but it needs to be interpreted carefully. “NARA found no corroborating record” and “NARA proved that no secret programme could possibly have existed” are not equivalent claims.

Classified records can remain withheld, be destroyed under authorised schedules, be misfiled, use unexpected terminology or reside in collections that researchers did not search. Highly compartmented programmes deliberately restrict distribution. Historical archives are never perfect mirrors of everything a government once did.

For MJ-12, however, the secrecy objection has limits. It can explain why a central operational file might be absent, but it becomes progressively less economical as an explanation for **multiple independent absences**. To preserve the Cutler-Twining memorandum as evidence of a genuine NSC/MJ-12 project, one must account not only for missing core project files but also for the lack of useful references in NSC indexes and July 1954 minutes, the absence of supporting material in related defence and Air Force searches, the presidential libraries' failure to find corroboration and the lack of a matching entry in Eisenhower's appointment record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Nor were archivists searching an administrative vacuum. The Eisenhower collections contain extensive national-security documentation, and the official record preserves detailed NSC proceedings from the very week in question. That does not make survival universal, but it makes the contrast informative: the normal machinery is visible while the alleged MJ-12 machinery is not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/files/iwg/declassified-records/presidential-libraries/eisenhower.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesHISTORICAL MATERIALS IN THE DWIGHT D. EISENHOWERDecember 7, 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 2010</span></span></span>

The fairest evidential judgement is therefore asymmetric. The failed searches **cannot establish universal non-existence**: they do not prove that no highly secret US government activity concerning UFO reports, unusual technology or any other subject could have operated outside the searched trail. They provide much stronger evidence against the more testable proposition that there was an authentic project called **MJ-12 or Majestic** operating in the manner suggested by the disputed documents.

That narrower conclusion is also close to the wording used by GAO. Its finding was not a philosophical assertion that secret programmes cannot escape archives. It was that there was **no evidence that the circulated Majestic 12 material constituted genuine documents originating in the executive branch**, and that its own work uncovered nothing contradicting the agencies' negative assessments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

## The missing trail changes the burden of proof

The Cutler-Twining sheet remains an archival object: NARA acknowledges that it exists in its custody. But the searches around it change what that fact can reasonably demonstrate.

If the sheet had been accompanied by an NSC index entry, corresponding meeting or briefing records, references in Twining's official files, related Joint Chiefs correspondence and a recognisable declassification history, its discovery in an Air Force record group would form one element of a mutually supporting documentary chain. Instead, the sheet is effectively the point from which the corroborating trail was sought — and repeatedly failed to materialise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

That is why the NARA searches are more damaging to the MJ-12 case than the simple phrase “absence of evidence” suggests. The issue is not merely that archives are missing something. It is that **a document making specific claims about identifiable institutions and a particular 1954 briefing failed to generate confirmation when those institutions' surviving records were checked**.

At the same time, the negative searches should not be exaggerated into proof of how the disputed paper entered NARA's holdings. They do not identify a forger, demonstrate who placed the Cutler-Twining memorandum in the Air Force files or establish whether its presence resulted from deliberate insertion, accidental filing or some other route. NARA's own position is appropriately narrower: certifying a reproduction means that it is a true copy of a document in the Archives' custody; certification does not authenticate either the document or the information it contains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

For [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}), that distinction is the central takeaway from the failed searches. The archive can confirm **where one disputed sheet was found**. The broader record has not confirmed **the organisation that the sheet says existed**. After searches across the NSC, defence establishment, Air Force and presidential collections — followed by GAO's later cross-agency review — the supporting paper trail that would turn “MJ-12” from words on a contested document into a documented government project remains missing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>
