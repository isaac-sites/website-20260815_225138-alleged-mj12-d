## Introduction

The US General Accounting Office (GAO, now the Government Accountability Office) did **not find evidence that the circulated Majestic 12 material was originally created as official executive-branch records**. In a two-page letter dated 28 July 1995 to Representative Steven H. Schiff of New Mexico, GAO reported that the relevant federal bodies knew MJ-12 chiefly from material supplied by people outside government and had found no corresponding records in their files. GAO added that its own work uncovered nothing contradicting those agencies’ assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

That finding is particularly important to the authenticity case surrounding the alleged 1952 Eisenhower briefing. The question GAO addressed was narrower than whether every claim associated with MJ-12 could be disproved: investigators asked whether the purported secret papers could be tied to the documentary systems of the US government. On that question, the answer was negative. [Presidential-library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) searches also failed to find corroborating MJ-12 records, while a separate 1980 message mentioning “MJ Twelve” was judged a forgery by the [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) Office of Special Investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

## Why the GAO examined the MJ-12 material

The MJ-12 inquiry formed a related part of Representative Schiff's larger effort to determine what federal records existed concerning the 1947 Roswell incident. The principal GAO Roswell investigation examined record-keeping requirements and searched for surviving government documentation; its findings were published on 28 July 1995 as report NSIAD-95-187. On the same date, GAO issued Schiff its separate comments on the publicly circulated Majestic 12 material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. GAO was not conducting a general investigation of UFO beliefs or attempting to determine through scientific evidence whether extraterrestrial craft existed. Its MJ-12 work concerned **documentary authenticity and government provenance**. The circulated material claimed to represent highly classified government records dealing with UFO recovery procedures and the Roswell crash. If genuine, papers of that kind would ostensibly have originated somewhere within the executive branch and passed through government systems for classified information and records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

By the time GAO examined the question, federal agencies had already been receiving MJ-12 enquiries from members of the public since the late 1980s. GAO therefore had more to work with than a simple absence of a document from one archive: it could ask the agencies responsible for classified-information oversight, Air Force security matters and federal archival [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}) what they actually knew about the supposedly governmental papers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/alt.ufo.reports/c/1g8hHH9pHEo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: groups.google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">groups.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Groups CONSPIr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google GroupsCONSPIr...</span></span></span>

The organisations identified by GAO included the Information Security Oversight Office (ISOO), which oversaw executive-branch national-security classification programmes; the Office of the Secretary of the Air Force's security and investigative apparatus; and the National Archives. GAO also sought the views of archivists at the Harry S. Truman and Dwight D. Eisenhower presidential libraries. This was a particularly relevant combination for material supposedly produced under Truman and prepared for Eisenhower.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/alt.ufo.reports/c/1g8hHH9pHEo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: groups.google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">groups.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Groups CONSPIr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google GroupsCONSPIr...</span></span></span>

## What federal agencies told investigators

The most consequential result was strikingly consistent. ISOO, the Air Force and the National Archives told investigators that their knowledge of Majestic 12 was limited to written material that **nongovernmental people had submitted to them**. They reported finding no records in their own files relating to MJ-12. GAO summarised their common conclusion as finding no evidence that the circulated material constituted documents originally created within the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

That finding goes directly to the provenance problem surrounding [the Eisenhower Briefing Document]({{ 'the-eisenhower-briefing-document/' | relative_url }}). A paper can contain plausible names, dates, classification markings and descriptions of genuine institutions without thereby becoming a government record. What would strengthen its status considerably is an independent official trail: an originating-office copy, registry entry, related correspondence, filing reference, declassification history or corroborating material preserved elsewhere in government. The agencies queried by GAO could not supply that kind of institutional link for the circulated MJ-12 material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The classification issue was equally significant. According to GAO, ISOO and the Air Force advised that the MJ-12 material should **not be treated as though it had actually been classified by an executive-branch agency or government official**. GAO then stated that its work had found nothing contradicting those conclusions. That is stronger than merely saying that GAO itself happened not to locate the original briefing: the offices responsible for the relevant government functions did not recognise the circulated papers as authenticated classified records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/alt.ufo.reports/c/1g8hHH9pHEo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: groups.google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">groups.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Groups CONSPIr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google GroupsCONSPIr...</span></span></span>

The presidential libraries supplied another independent check. Archivists at both the Truman and Eisenhower libraries told GAO that they had received public questions about MJ-12 over the years. Their searches included classified intelligence and National Security Council material, yet they found neither records fitting the description of the circulated Majestic 12 papers nor references to that particular designation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/alt.ufo.reports/c/1g8hHH9pHEo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: groups.google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">groups.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Groups CONSPIr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google GroupsCONSPIr...</span></span></span>

The broader National Archives record reinforces why that absence mattered. The Archives says that searches were conducted among Air Force and Joint Chiefs of Staff holdings and through National Security Council indexes from the Truman and Eisenhower administrations. Search terms included “MJ-12”, “majestic”, UFO-related terms and “Aquarius”. Those searches did not produce an official documentary series establishing the purported organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

## The 1980 “MJ Twelve” message failed a direct check

GAO encountered one item that initially looked more promising because it appeared to carry a recognisable government origin. Among material that members of the public had supplied to ISOO in connection with previous Freedom of Information Act requests was a message dated 17 November 1980. It appeared to originate from the Operations Division of the Air Force Office of Special Investigations (AFOSI) and contained the words “[MJ Twelve]({{ 'mj-twelve/' | relative_url }})”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/alt.ufo.reports/c/1g8hHH9pHEo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: groups.google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">groups.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Groups CONSPIr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google GroupsCONSPIr...</span></span></span>

Rather than accepting the document's appearance as proof, GAO asked AFOSI to authenticate it. The response provided a useful illustration of the difference between a paper that *looks* governmental and one that can be established through government records. In a letter dated 28 February 1995, the commander of AFOSI's Investigative Operations Center told GAO that a search of AFOSI files had failed to find an official record copy of the message.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sunrisepage.com/roswell/files/government/gao/1995_GAO_Report_on_the_Roswell_Incident_%28Original%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sunrisepage.com">[sunrisepage.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sunrisepage.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">United States General Accounting Office</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 1, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2025</span></span></span>

There was also a prior authenticity determination. AFOSI reported that it had previously examined the message in connection with an earlier Freedom of Information Act request and had concluded that it was **a forgery**. GAO accordingly could not use the apparent 1980 “MJ Twelve” reference as independent government corroboration for the older MJ-12 papers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

The episode is important because it illustrates the method underlying the GAO conclusion. The test was not simply whether a document used an official-looking format or terminology. Investigators checked whether the supposed originating organisation possessed a corresponding official record and asked that organisation about authenticity. In this instance, the answer was negative on both counts.

## The archival exception did not establish an MJ-12 programme

There is one complication that can make the federal record seem less straightforward at first sight. The National Archives possesses a one-page memorandum dated 14 July 1954, nominally from presidential adviser Robert Cutler to General Nathan [Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}), whose subject line refers to an “NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project”. Unlike the anonymously circulated Eisenhower briefing, this document was actually found among federal archival holdings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

But archival custody is not the same thing as authentication of a document's origin or contents. The National Archives explicitly explains that when it certifies a reproduction, it is attesting that the copy reproduces a document in its custody; it is **not authenticating the document or the information in it**. Its MJ-12 reference report identifies numerous anomalies surrounding the Cutler-Twining memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

Among them, the memorandum lacks the [Top Secret register]({{ 'register-numbers/' | relative_url }}) number expected for the series in which it was found; no other document in its folder concerns “NSC/MJ-12”; searches of related Defence Department, Joint Chiefs, Air Force and National Security Council records produced no supporting material; and the classification marking on the memorandum was reported to be one not used by the NSC until the Nixon administration. The Archives also found no NSC meeting on the briefing date indicated by the memo and no MJ-12 or Majestic reference in the July 1954 NSC meeting minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

There was an additional chronological difficulty. The Eisenhower Library's records showed that Cutler was visiting military installations in Europe and North Africa from 3 to 15 July 1954, meaning he was overseas on 14 July, the date on which he supposedly issued the memorandum. Contemporary instructions in his files delegated NSC administrative matters to subordinates during his absence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

The [Cutler-Twining paper]({{ 'paper-test/' | relative_url }}) therefore does not overturn GAO's finding about the circulated MJ-12 material. Its physical presence in an archival record group establishes where that particular sheet was found, but the Archives' own investigation documents substantial reasons not to treat it as straightforward proof that a genuine MJ-12 organisation existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

## What the GAO conclusion does and does not establish


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The strongest way to state the 1995 finding is also the narrowest: **GAO found no evidence that the publicly circulated Majestic 12 material constituted genuine documents originally created within the executive branch.** Relevant agencies reported no corresponding records, presidential archivists found no corroborating MJ-12 documentation, and GAO found nothing that contradicted those institutional conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>

</div>

For the Eisenhower Briefing Document authenticity case, that is a serious evidential problem. The briefing purports to be an extraordinarily sensitive presidential document. Yet the 1995 congressional investigation did not uncover the institutional traces needed to connect the circulated material to the government whose classification system, officials and authority it invokes.

The conclusion should not, however, be enlarged beyond what GAO actually established. An unsuccessful records search cannot logically demonstrate that no secret programme could ever have existed, nor did GAO claim to prove such a universal negative. Records can be missing, misfiled, destroyed or withheld. Indeed, the broader Roswell review separately found that some Roswell Army Air Field administrative records had been destroyed, with the available disposition documentation failing to establish who destroyed them or precisely when and under what authority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gao.justia.com/department-of-defense/1995/7/government-records-nsiad-95-187/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.justia.com">[gao.justia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.justia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GAO Reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Justia GAO Reports - NSIAD-95-187 - Government Records - Department of DefenseJuly 28, 1995...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 28, 1995</span></span></span>

But that general possibility does not supply positive evidence for MJ-12. For authentication purposes, the burden runs in the other direction: a purported government document becomes more credible when independent records connect it to the agency, office, classification process and historical chain of custody it claims. The 1995 GAO inquiry found no such corroborating trail for the circulated Majestic 12 papers.

That distinction is the enduring value of the GAO review. It did not settle every conceivable question surrounding UFO secrecy. It addressed a more concrete and answerable one: **could investigators establish that the famous MJ-12 material was genuinely produced as US executive-branch documentation?** On the evidence available to GAO and the federal institutions it consulted, they could not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span>
