## Introduction

Yes, a mechanical signing device could in principle explain why two Harry Truman signatures look extraordinarily alike. Truman lived at a time when signature-reproducing machines were entering federal use, and MJ-12 defender Robert Wood has offered a more specific possibility: a pantograph-like apparatus in which Truman moved a master pen while several linked pens produced simultaneous copies. Such a system could generate signatures far closer to one another than two ordinary handwritten signatures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/autopen-inventor-origins-presidents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">autopen inventor origins presidents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Did Presidents Start Using the Autopen? &#124; HISTORYDecember 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2025</span></span></span>

The crucial evidential gap is surprisingly simple: no source located here documents the two relevant 1947 documents — the alleged Forrestal memorandum and Truman's genuine 1 October letter to Vannevar Bush — being produced together by such a device. Wood himself described the evidence that Truman's pantograph reached Washington as “murky”. The signing-machine hypothesis therefore supplies a plausible mechanism for duplication, not a documented history of these particular signatures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span></span></span>

## Truman did use mechanical signature technology

There are really two related technologies in this discussion, and distinguishing them prevents confusion. A **pantograph or polygraph-style device** mechanically links pens so that a person's movements at one pen are duplicated by another pen, potentially at the same time. A later **autopen** instead stores a signature pattern and can reproduce it with a pen without the signer physically tracing every copy. Modern accounts date commercially successful autopen technology to the early 1940s and describe its rapid spread through the US government.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/autopen-inventor-origins-presidents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">autopen inventor origins presidents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Did Presidents Start Using the Autopen? &#124; HISTORYDecember 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2025</span></span></span>

There is credible historical evidence connecting the Truman era with automated signing. Contemporary histories of the technology place widespread federal autopen use in or around the Truman administration, although accounts differ over precisely when and how Truman himself used it. A 1989 *Los Angeles Times* history said use went back to the Truman administration and noted that the Defense Department was an early large-scale user. More recent histories likewise identify Truman as an early presidential user, while sometimes qualifying the claim as reported or believed rather than fully documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-10-01-vw-1151-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latimes.com">[Los Angeles Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Los Angeles Times Autopen: A Powerful Sign of the Times in Washington</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Los Angeles TimesAutopen: A Powerful Sign of the Times in Washington - Los Angeles TimesOctober 1, 1989...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1, 1989</span></span></span>

That distinction is important because the specific MJ-12 defence advanced by Wood is not simply “Truman had an autopen”. Wood argued that Truman had used a **master-and-slave pantograph**, describing a device with one master pen and five slave pens. In his 2000 MUFON symposium paper, he reproduced a photograph that he said showed Truman using such equipment while signing court papers in 1929, when Truman was a Missouri county judge. Wood further reported that an archivist had told him the apparatus was later taken to Washington — but immediately acknowledged that the evidence for this point was uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span></span></span>

There is therefore a solid historical core beneath the defence: mechanically reproduced signatures were technologically possible in Truman's period, such devices existed within government, and Truman has historical associations with automated or multiple-pen signing. What is not established is the much narrower proposition required by the MJ-12 argument: that a particular multi-pen mechanism produced both signatures at issue in September and October 1947.

## How a multiple-pen device could create the match

The attraction of the pantograph explanation is that it addresses a genuine difficulty for anyone defending the Truman–Forrestal memorandum. The disputed signature and Truman's genuine signature on the 1 October 1947 Bush letter are not merely examples of the same man's broadly similar handwriting. Critics reported that, after correcting for reproduction differences, they overlay extremely closely. Robert Wood himself acknowledged that when the two were superimposed they essentially matched.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/docs/SUN/SUN58.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for Inquiry SUN #58</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">S KEPTICS UFO NEWSLETTER By Philip J. KlDecember 6, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 6, 2024</span></span></span>

Ordinary handwriting would make such correspondence suspicious because even the same person does not normally execute every stroke identically on separate occasions. A mechanically linked set of pens changes that expectation. If Truman moved one master pen while several slave pens followed its movements, several documents could receive signatures derived from the **same physical hand movement**. Near-identity would then cease to be automatic evidence that one signature image had been photographically copied from the other.

This is a materially stronger argument than simply saying that Truman happened to sign his name consistently. It proposes an actual mechanism capable of generating extremely close contemporaneous signatures.

There is also an important distinction between a pantograph and a later template-based autopen. A stored-pattern autopen can reproduce essentially the same signature repeatedly on unrelated occasions. A master-and-slave pantograph instead produces multiple physical signatures simultaneously from a single act of writing. Under Wood's version of the hypothesis, the Bush and Forrestal signatures would therefore most naturally be understood as surviving products of the same signing session rather than two independently handwritten signatures a week apart.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span></span></span>

That creates the hypothesis's central historical problem.

## The dates make the mechanism harder to apply

The alleged Truman–Forrestal memorandum is dated **24 September 1947**. The genuine Truman letter to Vannevar Bush carrying the comparison signature is dated **1 October 1947**. If their signatures were simultaneously generated by linked pens, the documents would have had to be physically present for signing at the same time despite bearing dates a week apart.

Wood recognised this issue. His proposed answer was that the date on the questioned Truman–Forrestal memorandum had been inserted separately: he pointed to the date's alignment as evidence that the sheet had been removed and later returned to the typewriter. A forensic assessment cited by the Woods likewise described the numerals in “September 24, 1947” as sitting above the line and suggested that the paper had been removed between typing the text and adding the date. That assessment said the signature “may be a reproduction or produced by a pantograph” rather than deciding between those possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">December 9, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 9, 2025</span></span></span>

This makes a mechanically duplicated signature **possible as a reconstruction**, but it adds another unsupported step. The scenario now requires not merely that Truman possessed the right machine, but that the alleged Forrestal memorandum remained unsigned until around the time of the Bush letter, that both were placed into the same multiple-pen signing operation, and that the earlier date on the Forrestal page was separately inserted or otherwise does not represent its actual signing sequence.

None of those steps is impossible. The problem is that the surviving evidence does not independently document them.

The contrast with an ordinary important presidential signature from the same year is instructive. When Truman was away from Washington and needed to sign the Greek-Turkish aid legislation in 1947, contemporary reporting says the bill was physically flown to him in Kansas City for his signature. That does not prove that Truman never used automated signatures that year, particularly for routine correspondence, but it warns against assuming that the mere availability of signing technology tells us how any particular significant document was executed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.deseret.com/2011/5/28/20194659/making-history-with-nod-from-obama-and-stroke-of-an-autopen/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: deseret.com">[Deseret News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">deseret.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">News Making history, with nod from Obama and stroke of an autopen – Deseret News</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Deseret NewsMaking history, with nod from Obama and stroke of an autopen – Deseret NewsMay 27, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 27, 2011</span></span></span>

## The stray mark is the harder test

The most significant challenge to the mechanical-device explanation is not simply the outline of Truman's name. Critics have emphasised an anomalous small mark associated with the “H” in “Harry” that appears in both the disputed signature and the genuine Bush signature. Philip J. Klass's published material described the marks as matching, and the *International UFO Reporter* discussion of the controversy likewise highlighted the anomalous stroke as part of the correspondence between the signatures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TRUMAN FORRESTAL MEMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">TRUMAN FORRESTAL MEMO</span></span></span>

That detail matters because different mechanisms predict different things.

A template-based autopen can naturally repeat peculiarities contained in its stored master signature. A simultaneous multi-pen pantograph can also transmit the same unusual **intentional or hand-generated motion** to several slave pens. So the existence of an odd stroke is not, by itself, logically incompatible with mechanical signing.

But an accidental artefact produced only on one physical copy is different. A slave pen catching the paper, an ink irregularity or a stray scratch unique to one sheet would not automatically appear on another sheet simply because both pens were mechanically linked. To make the matching anomaly support a pantograph explanation, it would need to result from the common movement transmitted through the apparatus rather than from a paper-specific accident.

This is where access to originals would be especially valuable. The publicly circulated MJ-12 material is reproduced imagery, not two securely preserved original ink signatures that can be examined side by side for pressure, ink flow, microscopic stroke sequence and other physical characteristics. Wood's own presentation worked from a photographic print of the questioned document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span></span></span>

That limitation cuts both ways. It prevents a definitive microscopic demonstration of exactly how the disputed signature was made, but it also prevents the signing-machine defence from being positively verified through physical traces of independent pen execution.

## Why the 1947 connection remains unproven

The signing-device hypothesis ultimately has three different evidential levels that should not be collapsed into one.

**First, the technology existed.** Mechanical signature reproduction was well established by the 1940s, and automated signing spread rapidly through the federal government. That part of the defence is historically credible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/autopen-inventor-origins-presidents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">autopen inventor origins presidents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Did Presidents Start Using the Autopen? &#124; HISTORYDecember 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2025</span></span></span>

**Second, Truman had relevant associations with mechanical signing.** Sources connect the Truman administration with autopen use, and Wood presented evidence that Truman had earlier used a multi-pen pantograph. It is therefore unreasonable to dismiss mechanical duplication merely because it sounds technologically anachronistic. It was not.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-10-01-vw-1151-story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: latimes.com">[Los Angeles Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">latimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Los Angeles Times Autopen: A Powerful Sign of the Times in Washington</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Los Angeles TimesAutopen: A Powerful Sign of the Times in Washington - Los Angeles TimesOctober 1, 1989...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1, 1989</span></span></span>

**Third, however, the required connection to these two documents has not been demonstrated.** No contemporaneous [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) record located in the evidence reviewed here says that the 24 September Forrestal memorandum and 1 October Bush letter were signed in a common mechanical operation. Wood's own account concedes uncertainty over whether Truman's earlier pantograph was even brought to Washington. Nor did Wood report finding the especially valuable corroborating example his hypothesis predicts: another contemporaneous Truman signature sufficiently close to these two to demonstrate that the same mechanism was producing a family of near-identical signatures. Instead, he said researchers were searching archives for just such an example.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span></span></span>

That missing comparison is important. If multiple genuine Truman documents from the same period carried signatures with the same geometry and characteristic strokes, the claim of routine mechanical reproduction would become much stronger. Conversely, if the Bush letter remains uniquely matched by the disputed MJ-12 signature, photographic transplantation remains the simpler explanation for the pair.

## The signing machine is possible, but not demonstrated

The multiple-pen argument therefore succeeds at one limited but worthwhile task: it shows why “these signatures match closely” cannot, in complete isolation, be treated as a mathematical proof of forgery. Truman-era technology was capable of producing mechanically related signatures, and there is evidence that Truman had experience with such devices.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/autopen-inventor-origins-presidents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[HISTORY]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">autopen inventor origins presidents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Did Presidents Start Using the Autopen? &#124; HISTORYDecember 18, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 18, 2025</span></span></span>

It does considerably less well as an explanation of **this particular match**. To account for the Truman–Forrestal and Truman–Bush signatures through a simultaneous multi-pen device, the hypothesis needs a signing session linking documents dated a week apart, an explanation for the dating sequence, and evidence that Truman's relevant apparatus was being used for those papers in Washington in 1947. The published defence provides possibilities for those steps, but not contemporaneous documentation of them. Its principal proponent explicitly acknowledged that part of the historical trail was murky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[majesticdocuments.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MUFONMUFO N Symposium Proceedings VALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTIC</span></span></span>

The fairest conclusion is consequently narrower than either extreme. Truman's history with mechanical signing makes the multiple-pen explanation technically and historically plausible enough to consider. It does **not** supply evidence that the alleged 24 September 1947 MJ-12 memorandum was genuinely signed by that method. Until a securely authenticated contemporaneous document, White House record or additional matching signature establishes the missing link, the device remains an alternative mechanism rather than an authentication of the Truman–Forrestal memorandum.
