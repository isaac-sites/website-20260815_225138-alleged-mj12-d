## Introduction

The Truman signature became [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }})’s hardest authentication problem because the strongest forensic evidence did not merely show that President Harry Truman’s signature *looked* suspicious. A professional questioned-document examiner retained by MJ-12 proponents, James A. Black, concluded that the signature on the alleged 24 September 1947 Truman-[Forrestal]({{ 'forrestal/' | relative_url }}) memorandum was probably a **reproduction rather than an original ink signature**. Friedman accepted that finding. His defence therefore had to change: instead of arguing simply that Truman had personally signed the surviving memorandum, he proposed that an authentic signature could have been deliberately reproduced when a later briefing copy was assembled.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Friedman had to explain why an authentic secret document would contain what looked like a mechanically transferred presidential signature — precisely the technique a document fabricator could also have used.

## The signature match that alarmed critics

The disputed memorandum purportedly records Truman authorising Secretary of Defense James Forrestal to proceed with an undertaking thereafter called “Operation Majestic Twelve”. It was especially important because it supplied the alleged presidential authority behind MJ-12 and appeared as an attachment to [the Eisenhower Briefing Document]({{ 'the-eisenhower-briefing-document/' | relative_url }}) released publicly in 1987.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2022/09/26195517/SUN-69-Fall-2001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

The problem emerged through comparison with an unquestionably genuine Truman communication to Vannevar Bush dated **1 October 1947**, only a week after the supposed Forrestal memorandum. Friedman himself had located the Bush document while researching Truman-era records. Critics argued that its Truman signature corresponded extraordinarily closely with the one on the MJ-12 memorandum. Philip J. Klass eventually treated the match as a “smoking gun”: in his interpretation, the genuine Bush signature had supplied a convenient donor image that could be reproduced on a fabricated memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

This was more troublesome than an ordinary handwriting resemblance. Presidents sign thousands of documents, so broadly similar signatures prove little. What critics alleged was correspondence in individual features sufficiently close to suggest a *single reproduced image*. Klass particularly emphasised scratch-like details that he regarded as accidental characteristics carried from the genuine signature into the disputed one. His argument was straightforward: if two supposedly separate acts of signing preserve the same incidental marks, reproduction becomes more plausible than two independent signatures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2022/09/26195517/SUN-69-Fall-2001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span>

William Moore, Friedman’s fellow MJ-12 researcher, disputed the claim that the signatures were literally identical. In a September 1989 response to Klass, Moore supplied measurements showing differences in the lengths and proportions of several strokes. He also argued that the supposedly incriminating mark around the “H” had analogues in other authentic Truman signatures. Significantly, however, Moore stopped short of claiming that these observations authenticated the memorandum: he explicitly said his point was only that calling the two signatures “identical” was erroneous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

That was an important limitation. Demonstrating differences between two reproduced images can defeat a simplistic claim of perfect one-to-one identity, but it does not establish that both came from independent acts of signing. Photographic enlargement, reduction, copying and generational degradation can change dimensions and line quality. The real authentication question therefore became not “are the images mathematically identical?” but “does the disputed image contain evidence that it was made by pen on this document, or evidence that it was reproduced?”

## Friedman's briefing-copy explanation

James Black’s examination forced Friedman to confront that second question directly. Black was not simply an enthusiast commenting on photographs. His professional background was in questioned-document examination, including handwriting, signatures, typewriters, photocopying, alterations, paper and ink, and he had qualified as an expert witness in state and federal proceedings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thedocumentexpert.com/qual.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thedocumentexpert.com">[The Document Expert]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thedocumentexpert.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on thedocumentexpert.com.</span></span></span>

Black actually helped Friedman’s side on one major technical issue. He disagreed with the claim, associated with examiner Peter Tytell, that the Truman-Forrestal memorandum had been typed with a machine unavailable in 1947. Black concluded that the visible typeface was consistent with an **Underwood Standard**, comparing it with an exemplar produced in May 1940. Friedman repeatedly cited that finding against the typewriter-anachronism argument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

But Black’s finding about the signature cut in the opposite direction. As Friedman later reported it, Black considered the disputed Truman signature “most likely a reproduction”, basing that judgement on the homogeneous appearance of the ink line and the absence of expected feathering at line endings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Friedman did not deny this conclusion. Instead, he argued that reproduction did not necessarily mean fraudulent reproduction.

His proposed scenario depended on treating the material as a **later briefing package**, not necessarily the physical memorandum that Forrestal originally received in September 1947. Forrestal’s original, Friedman reasoned, could have carried Truman’s genuine signature. Other officials mentioned in the memorandum — Vannevar Bush and Director of Central Intelligence Roscoe [Hillenkoetter]({{ 'hillenkoetter/' | relative_url }}) — might have held copies, perhaps unsigned. When material was later assembled for the purported 1952 Eisenhower briefing, an intelligence office could have wanted a visually complete historical exhibit and added a reproduced Truman signature to a copy. Friedman suggested that the CIA would certainly have possessed the technical ability to lift such a signature from another document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

That explanation was ingenious because it converted the damaging observation into a question about **document function**. A reproduced signature would be fatal if the surviving sheet claimed to be Truman’s signed original. It would not necessarily be fatal if the sheet were a retrospective reproduction prepared only to show a future president what an earlier directive had said.

Friedman also pointed to the peculiar construction of the memorandum as evidence that it might have passed through several stages of preparation. The numerical portion of its date appears offset and, in his interpretation, was typed on a different machine. He cited former Truman [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) aide George Elsey for the more general point that presidential documents were commonly prepared by staff and sometimes dated later when the appropriate date became known.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

There was also a genuine historical coincidence that Friedman considered significant. The Truman Library told him that Truman’s only recorded meeting with Vannevar Bush between May and the end of December 1947 occurred on **24 September 1947**, the very date appearing on the disputed memorandum, and that Forrestal accompanied Bush. The library found no record of what was discussed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

That coincidence strengthened Friedman’s broader argument that the document contained [period details]({{ 'period-details/' | relative_url }}) worth explaining. It did **not**, however, solve the signature problem. A historically appropriate date and an inexplicably reproduced signature are logically separate pieces of evidence. The former can make a fabrication seem less careless; it cannot establish that the reproduction was made inside government rather than by whoever created the disputed document.

## What James Black added to the dispute

Black’s contribution made the case unusually difficult for Friedman because it split the forensic evidence in two directions. The same examiner whom Friedman could cite to rebut the alleged typewriter anachronism also supplied professional support for the proposition that the presidential signature was reproduced. That made it difficult to dismiss the signature criticism as merely another hostile claim from Klass or other sceptics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

This distinction also helps separate several claims that are sometimes blurred together:

* **The typeface question:** Black thought the memorandum could have been typed with a period-appropriate Underwood Standard. That weakened one specific anachronism argument.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>
* **The exact-match question:** Moore disputed Klass’s assertion that the Truman-Bush and Truman-Forrestal images were literally identical and documented measurable differences between them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>
* **The reproduction question:** Black nevertheless concluded that the disputed signature itself was probably reproduced rather than directly written onto the document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>
* **The authenticity question:** neither an appropriate typewriter nor differences between two signature images establishes that the underlying Truman-Forrestal text is genuine. Conversely, identifying a reproduced signature establishes a serious anomaly but does not, by itself, identify who reproduced it or when.

The weakness in Friedman’s response was therefore evidential rather than merely technical. His briefing-copy scenario is *possible*, but the surviving material does not independently document the proposed chain: an original signed Truman memorandum, an unsigned distribution copy, and then a government-produced briefing reproduction onto which Truman’s signature was restored. Friedman framed the issue himself in terms of where an original signed copy might once have existed and why a later preparer might have wanted a signature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Critics could offer a much shorter mechanism: obtain an authentic Truman signature, place its reproduction on a fabricated memorandum, then reproduce the whole page. Klass argued that the close Truman-Bush correspondence was evidence for just such a construction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cdn.centerforinquiry.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2022/09/26195517/SUN-69-Fall-2001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cdn.centerforinquiry.org">[Center for Inquiry]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cdn.centerforinquiry.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for Inquiry</span></span></span> The difficulty for authentication is that Friedman's explanation requires additional undocumented stages of legitimate government copying, whereas the forgery explanation treats the reproduced signature as part of the fabrication itself.

A related pro-authenticity attempt illustrates the same problem. Robert Wood later proposed that Truman’s known use of a multi-pen signing device might help explain very similar signatures, while acknowledging that the evidence for its relevant use in Washington was “murky”. Wood also described the Truman signature as one of the authentication issues that remained unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/pdf/rmwood_mufon2000.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[Majestic Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rmwood mufon2000</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic DocumentsMUFONMUFON Symposium ProceedingsVALIDATING THE NEW MAJESTICMarch 26, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 26, 2026</span></span></span> This did not provide the missing provenance for the disputed memorandum.

## Why this anomaly was harder than Friedman's other rebuttals

Friedman was often at his most effective when an MJ-12 criticism took the form of an absolute historical claim that archival research could falsify. If someone said a particular security marking did not exist in the relevant era, finding period examples could weaken that objection. Friedman used precisely this method when discussing “TOP SECRET RESTRICTED”, citing the later discovery by the General Accounting Office of the marking on documents from the 1948–56 period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The Truman signature problem was structurally different. Black’s reproduction finding could not be answered by locating another government memorandum with a similar font or another Truman signature with a similar flourish. Once reproduction was conceded, the decisive question became **provenance**: who made the reproduction, from what source, for what administrative purpose, and at what stage?

No surviving chain of [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}) answers those questions. The Truman Library told Friedman in 1987 that it could not locate a copy of the purported 18 November 1952 briefing document and that its rules did not permit it to authenticate the submitted material or handwriting. Its records could confirm the remarkable Truman-Bush-Forrestal meeting on 24 September 1947, but not the disputed memorandum itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

That is why the signature remained such a difficult point within Friedman’s defence of the core MJ-12 documents. His research produced legitimate reasons not to accept every early debunking claim at face value: Moore demonstrated that “identical” was too simple a description of the two surviving signature images; Black supported a 1940-era typewriter identification; and Truman’s appointment records supplied an intriguing match to the memorandum’s date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/pcg-bill-moore-files-collection/TRUMAN-FORRESTAL-MEMO.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius</span></span></span>

Yet Black’s central signature finding survived those rebuttals. Friedman could explain **how an authentic government briefing copy might conceivably acquire a reproduced Truman signature**, but he could not independently demonstrate that this was what happened. The signature therefore moved the argument away from anomalies Friedman could attack with archival counterexamples and towards the fundamental weakness of the MJ-12 material: without an authenticated original or a documented government chain of custody, a feature compatible with legitimate copying can also be compatible with fabrication.

For that reason, the Truman signature was not simply one more disputed detail. It created an authentication problem in which Friedman’s defence depended on reconstructing an undocumented legitimate copying process to explain exactly the forensic feature that critics regarded as evidence of manufacture.
