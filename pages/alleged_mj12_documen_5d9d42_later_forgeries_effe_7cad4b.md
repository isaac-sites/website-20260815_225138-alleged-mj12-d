## Introduction

Later MJ-12 forgeries weaken the case for the wider document collection, but they do not logically prove that every earlier MJ-12 paper is false. That distinction is important because [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) did not emerge as one authenticated archive. Different papers appeared at different times, through different intermediaries and with very different chains of [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}). A forgery discovered in a batch received in the 1990s therefore cannot, by itself, determine the status of documents first circulated in the 1980s.

## Why later papers are treated separately

The later MJ-12 material has a different provenance from the best-known early documents. Jaime Shandera received [the Eisenhower Briefing Document]({{ 'the-eisenhower-briefing-document/' | relative_url }}) and associated material on anonymously mailed 35 mm film in 1984. By contrast, researcher Timothy Cooper began receiving a much larger body of purported MJ-12 material in the early 1990s, much of it as photocopies delivered through the post or attributed to sources whose identities could not be independently verified. The Majestic Documents project itself describes Cooper as receiving papers from an anonymous correspondent using the name “[Cantwheel]({{ 'cantwheel/' | relative_url }})”, alongside documents obtained through other informal channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/documents/document-sources/timothy-s-cooper/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[Majestic Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic Documents Timothy S. Cooper &#124; Majestic Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic DocumentsTimothy S. Cooper &#124; Majestic DocumentsNovember 14, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 14, 2018</span></span></span>

Friedman therefore resisted treating “MJ-12 documents” as a single evidential unit. In his 2004 review he separated the Eisenhower Briefing Document, Truman-Forrestal memorandum and Cutler-Twining memorandum from the later SOM1-01 manual and the mass of Cooper material, saying there appeared to be no established connection between their sources. His position was that fraudulent later documents should be rejected individually rather than used automatically to condemn earlier ones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

That document-by-document approach is logically sound. If two papers arrive from entirely independent sources, proving that one is fabricated does not prove that the other is fabricated. This is especially relevant to MJ-12 because no single archival accession or official file series ties the disputed corpus together. The National Archives found no broader documentary trail for MJ-12 in relevant [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}), Joint Chiefs, defence and National Security Council records, while the Government Accountability Office reported that agencies had no evidence that the MJ-12 material supplied to them actually originated within the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The separation, however, cuts both ways. If advocates want later documents judged independently from exposed forgeries, they must also resist using one apparently impressive MJ-12 paper to authenticate others merely because they repeat the same names, terminology or narrative. A heterogeneous corpus cannot coherently be treated as independent when a document fails but mutually reinforcing when one appears favourable.

## What a proven forgery establishes

The most damaging later examples are not documents that merely contain questionable formatting. They are papers for which investigators found identifiable genuine documents that appear to have served as templates.

Friedman described discovering several such “phony emulations” among material associated with Cooper. One purported 1948 communication from Admiral Roscoe Hillenkoetter to President Truman struck him as suspicious because its discussion of large volumes of intelligence “product” and machines resembled wartime reporting about MAGIC, the Allied exploitation of Japanese encrypted communications. Friedman then found the genuine historical material on which he concluded the supposed MJ-12 paper had been modelled. He similarly reported finding authentic documents whose wording and structure matched other purported Majestic papers after UFO-related terms, names or dates had been substituted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

A particularly clear example concerned a purported Marshall document linked to the later MJ-12 material. Material preserved by the Computer UFO Network includes correspondence in which Friedman accepted historian Larry Bland's conclusion that the document was a parody or reworking of genuine material. Friedman explicitly argued that this demonstrated the fraudulence of that particular document, while rejecting “guilt … by association” as a basis for dismissing unrelated papers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/marshall.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Computer UFO Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Computer UFO NetworkFRAUDULENT 27-SEPTEMBER-1944 &quot;MJ-12&quot; DOCUMENT...</span></span></span>

This matters because it establishes more than the existence of errors. It demonstrates a feasible forgery technique. A fabricator did not need to invent convincing 1940s bureaucratic prose from scratch. An authentic letter could supply the period vocabulary, sentence structure, names, formatting and handwritten elements. Selected passages could then be retyped or reproduced, with references altered to introduce MJ-12. Friedman described essentially that process: genuine documents could be reworked using an old typewriter and copied handwritten portions to produce a historically plausible-looking derivative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

That lesson changes the evidential value of “[period accuracy]({{ 'period-accuracy/' | relative_url }})”. Before such examples were exposed, obscure bureaucratic details or convincing language might appear difficult for a hoaxer to reproduce. Once direct historical templates are known to have been used, those features become weaker authentication criteria. Accurate names, authentic-looking signatures and correct period terminology may show that the maker had good source material, not that the resulting document came from the alleged government office.

The risk is particularly serious where only photocopies survive. Michael Heiser's 2007 review of forensic linguistic testing noted that Friedman had already identified several fraudulent MJ-12 documents that consisted of authentic source material modified towards a Majestic narrative. Heiser observed that the presence of such demonstrated fabrications inevitably raises suspicion about a collection in which many items cannot be examined as physical originals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.priory-of-sion.com/biblios/links/heiser.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: priory-of-sion.com">[Priory of Sion]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">priory-of-sion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Priory of Sion Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Priory of SionMicrosoft Word - MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

## The linguistic tests widened the credibility problem

Later research also asked whether suspect MJ-12 papers merely contained isolated fabrication techniques or showed broader authorship anomalies. In 2007 Heiser reported a computational authorship study carried out by forensic linguist Carole Chaski on seventeen purported Majestic documents bearing the names of identifiable authors.

The exercise included several documents that Friedman had already classified as fraudulent, without telling Chaski which ones they were. According to the report, her method rejected those known fraudulent controls as being inconsistent with their claimed authors. More broadly, only one of the tested, author-attributed documents not already established as fraudulent emerged as a strong linguistic match for its purported writer. In eight cases, the closest match was instead a comparison pool of Kennedy-era prose. Heiser stressed that this pattern could suggest common authorship but did not prove it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.priory-of-sion.com/biblios/links/heiser.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: priory-of-sion.com">[Priory of Sion]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">priory-of-sion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Priory of Sion Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Priory of SionMicrosoft Word - MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

Chaski's underlying field was not devised for UFO research. Her National Institute of Justice-supported work examined whether grammar, syntax, punctuation and related linguistic characteristics could be used for empirical authorship attribution, with an emphasis on testing methods rather than relying on subjective impressions of writing style.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/linguistic-authentication-and-reliability" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ojp.gov">[Office of Justice Programs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ojp.gov.</span></span></span>

The MJ-12 study nevertheless has limits that are important to the later-forgery debate. Heiser described it as preliminary; only a portion of the corpus was tested, and documents without a named author could not be directly compared with authenticated writings by their supposed creator. The results therefore should not be converted into a claim that linguistic analysis has “proved the entire MJ-12 archive fake”. Heiser himself called for further testing and acknowledged that untested documents remained unresolved by that method.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.priory-of-sion.com/biblios/links/heiser.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: priory-of-sion.com">[Priory of Sion]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">priory-of-sion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Priory of Sion Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Priory of SionMicrosoft Word - MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

Its stronger relevance is narrower: a test capable of recognising several previously exposed fraudulent documents also found serious authorship problems elsewhere in the later corpus. That makes the demonstrated forgeries harder to dismiss as a few isolated bad papers that have no implications for their neighbours.

## Why the damage spreads beyond the forged pages

Although forgery is not contagious in a strictly logical sense, evidence is evaluated cumulatively. Later fabrications affect the wider MJ-12 debate through several mechanisms.

First, they alter the prior credibility of anonymous “leaks”. Once documents carrying Majestic terminology are known to have been deliberately created and circulated, the mere arrival of another anonymous paper no longer has neutral evidential significance. A new document needs positive provenance rather than simply an absence of obvious mistakes.

Second, the forgeries weaken arguments based on cross-document corroboration. Suppose document A uses an unusual MJ-12 designation and document B independently uses the same designation. That agreement would be interesting if A and B were securely independent. It means much less if the corpus contains documents manufactured by copying or adapting existing MJ-12 material, or if their transmission histories overlap.

Third, demonstrated fabrication exposes how apparently impressive historical detail can be manufactured. A hoaxer with access to published memoirs, archival reproductions and genuine official correspondence can reproduce the bureaucratic texture of an era remarkably well. The later MJ-12 forgeries therefore raise the standard for what counts as authentication: correct stationery, period vocabulary or an authentic signature image cannot substitute for a demonstrable chain of custody.

Finally, the existence of multiple bad documents creates a selection problem. If researchers accept impressive-looking papers provisionally, discard those later exposed as false, and continue citing the survivors as corroborative evidence, the surviving collection can appear stronger than it really is. Documents that have not yet been disproved are not thereby authenticated.

This concern is reinforced by the official record. The National Archives' search for corroborating MJ-12 documentation found no supporting trail for the famous Cutler-Twining memorandum, while also identifying classification and [filing anomalies]({{ 'filing-anomalies/' | relative_url }}) in that document. The GAO likewise found no evidence that the submitted MJ-12 material represented genuine executive-branch records and recorded an Air Force determination that a separate message containing “MJ Twelve” was a forgery. Those findings concern different pieces of evidence, but they show why positive provenance is particularly important in a corpus already known to contain fabrications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## What later forgeries cannot establish about the originals

The strongest sceptical conclusion still has a boundary. A forged Cooper document from the 1990s cannot retrospectively demonstrate who created the Eisenhower Briefing Document mailed in 1984, nor can it establish that the Cutler-Twining memorandum was planted in the National Archives by the same person. Those propositions require evidence connecting the sources, makers or transmission histories.

Friedman's response to the later fakes therefore contains a legitimate logical point even if one rejects his wider MJ-12 conclusions. In correspondence about one exposed document, he accepted the finding of fraud while arguing that neither authenticity nor fraud could properly be assigned to other papers merely “by association”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cufon.org/cufon/marshall.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufon.org">[Computer UFO Network]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Computer UFO NetworkFRAUDULENT 27-SEPTEMBER-1944 &quot;MJ-12&quot; DOCUMENT...</span></span></span>

The same restraint is appropriate with the 2007 linguistic findings. Heiser concluded that the results cast serious doubt on using the tested Majestic documents as reliable evidence, but he also stated that documents lacking attributed authors remained outside the direct scope of that test. The report thus supports heightened suspicion and document-by-document testing, not blanket proof about every item ever labelled MJ-12.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.priory-of-sion.com/biblios/links/heiser.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: priory-of-sion.com">[Priory of Sion]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">priory-of-sion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Priory of Sion Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Priory of SionMicrosoft Word - MJ Test article.doc...</span></span></span>

What the later forgeries do remove is any reasonable presumption that the wider corpus should be trusted until disproved. The burden runs in the opposite direction. A disputed MJ-12 document needs independent reasons for acceptance: traceable archival provenance, a verifiable physical original, period-correct material evidence, corroborating records produced independently of the MJ-12 controversy, or some comparable authentication route.

That is why later forgeries occupy an important but sometimes overstated place in the MJ-12 debate. They are not a logical shortcut proving that every original paper is fraudulent. They are evidence that sophisticated fabrication occurred inside the wider MJ-12 document tradition, that genuine historical records were capable of being repurposed to manufacture convincing “Majestic” papers, and that similarities within the corpus cannot safely authenticate one document with another. The original MJ-12 papers therefore still stand or fall on their own evidence — but they do so under a substantially darker credibility shadow than they would if the later forgeries had never appeared.

      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
