## Introduction

The alleged Cutler–Twining memorandum points to a very specific event: an “MJ-12 SSP briefing” that, it says, President Dwight D. Eisenhower had ordered moved into an already scheduled [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) meeting on Friday, 16 July 1954. Yet Eisenhower’s surviving appointment books contain no such briefing, no meeting with General Nathan Twining that day, and no unexplained presidential appointment that obviously corresponds to it. The Eisenhower [Presidential Library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) regarded this absence as significant because the appointment books routinely recorded even meetings explicitly designated “off the record”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The gap becomes more striking when the actual pages for 14–19 July are examined. They record sensitive national-security meetings, off-the-record conversations and [Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }})’s presence on adjacent days. On 16 July itself, however, Eisenhower’s documented schedule moves from a Cabinet meeting and several appointments to lunch and golf away from the White House.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span> That does not prove that no clandestine briefing could possibly have occurred, but it leaves the disputed memorandum without the scheduling corroboration its wording leads researchers to expect.

## What the Cutler–Twining memorandum appears to anticipate

The disputed memorandum is dated 14 July 1954 and addressed to General Nathan F. Twining, then Chief of Staff of the US [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}). Its subject is given as “NSC/MJ-12 Special Studies Project”. The crucial passage says that the President had decided the “MJ-12 SSP briefing” should occur **during** an already scheduled White House meeting on 16 July rather than afterwards, and instructs Twining to alter his plans accordingly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://luforu.org/cutler-twining-memo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luforu.org">[LUFORU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luforu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cutler-Twining memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cutler-Twining memoJuly 14, 1954...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 1954</span></span></span>

That wording matters because it describes more than a vague intention to brief someone sometime in July. It supplies a date, a location, a presidential decision and an intended participant. Whatever the underlying “already scheduled White House meeting” was supposed to be, the memorandum implies that Twining needed to be at the White House on 16 July and that a briefing significant enough to involve presidential scheduling had been fitted into the day.

One point requires care. The memorandum does **not** actually call the underlying event an ordinary National Security Council meeting. Some later descriptions have treated it that way, but proponents of the document have correctly observed that its wording says only “White House meeting”. The National Archives nevertheless checked the [NSC records]({{ 'nsc-records/' | relative_url }}) and found no NSC meeting on 16 July and no mention of MJ-12 or Majestic anywhere in the NSC meeting minutes for July 1954.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span> This closes off one obvious interpretation of the memorandum without establishing what other White House meeting, if any, its author intended.

The date also sits immediately after a real, well-documented NSC meeting. Eisenhower presided over the 206th meeting of the National Security Council on Thursday, 15 July. The surviving memorandum of discussion names the President, Vice-President Richard Nixon, senior State and Defence representatives, CIA Director Allen Dulles, Joint Chiefs Chairman Admiral Arthur Radford and NSC Executive Secretary James Lay among those present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov//historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v13p2/d1057fn8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the HistorianJuly 15, 1954...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 1954</span></span></span> Eisenhower’s appointment book independently records that NSC session.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span> The contrast is therefore not between the alleged briefing and a week for which White House records have disappeared: major national-security business on the preceding day is extensively documented.

## What Eisenhower’s appointment books actually record

The Eisenhower Presidential Library describes the presidential appointment books as a collection of nearly 23,000 pages covering January 1953 to January 1961. The chronological books normally give dates, times, places and the people or organisations who saw Eisenhower. Importantly for the MJ-12 question, the Library says that some meetings are specifically recorded as “Off the Record”; the associated card index likewise marks such meetings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/research/presidential-appointment-books" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eisenhowerlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>

This was not merely a theoretical feature of the filing system. The July 1954 pages repeatedly demonstrate it in practice. On 14 July, for example, Eisenhower had an off-the-record breakfast with publisher Roy Howard, an off-the-record meeting involving Arthur Flemming and Gerald Morgan, another with Admiral William Leahy, and an off-the-record meeting with NSC Executive Secretary James S. Lay and Deputy Executive Secretary S. Everett Gleason, who briefed the President on NSC matters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

That last entry has become part of the defence of the Cutler–Twining memorandum. [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}), a prominent advocate of the MJ-12 documents, argued that because Cutler was overseas, Lay could have handled a short administrative memorandum on his behalf. Friedman specifically pointed to Lay’s off-the-record meeting with Eisenhower on 14 July as a possible opportunity for the presidential instruction described in the disputed memo to have originated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://luforu.org/cutler-twining-memo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luforu.org">[LUFORU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luforu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cutler-Twining memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cutler-Twining memoJuly 14, 1954...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 1954</span></span></span>

But the same evidence also illustrates the problem with explaining away the 16 July gap by secrecy alone. The allegedly useful 14 July meeting between Eisenhower and Lay is known precisely because the appointment book **did record it**, despite labelling it off the record. Contemporary historical research confirms that this was normal enough for genuinely sensitive business: the US State Department’s published *Foreign Relations of the United States* series, for example, uses Eisenhower’s appointment records to document off-the-record White House national-security meetings in March 1954 involving Secretary of State John Foster Dulles, Defence Secretary Charles Wilson, CIA Director Allen Dulles and Joint Chiefs Chairman Radford.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov//historicaldocuments/frus1952-54v13p1/d622" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the Historian...</span></span></span>

Twining himself is likewise visible in the records surrounding the disputed date. On 15 July, the appointment book places General Nathan Twining at Eisenhower’s luncheon for Libyan Prime Minister Mustafa Ben Halim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span> On Monday, 19 July, it records Twining again, this time with Admiral Radford during Radford’s usual Monday appointment with the President.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span> His absence from the presidential schedule on Friday the 16th therefore cannot readily be attributed to the appointment books simply omitting Twining as a matter of course.

## What happened at the White House on 16 July?

The surviving appointment record gives a surprisingly detailed picture of Eisenhower’s 16 July. It begins with an off-the-record breakfast with Senator William Knowland. Eisenhower then received members of the Federal Farm Credit Board before a 9 a.m. Cabinet meeting. The appointment book lists the Cabinet participants individually, including Vice-President Nixon, Secretary of State Dulles and Deputy Secretary of Defense Robert Anderson. Twining is not among them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

The Cabinet meeting itself was a documented governmental event rather than an empty calendar label. The Eisenhower Library’s Cabinet-series finding aid identifies the subjects considered on 16 July, including telephone-call monitoring, foreign economic-policy organisation, the St Lawrence Seaway, employment security, manganese purchases and Mexican migratory labour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/finding-aids/pdf/eisenhower-dwight-papers-as-president/cabinet-series.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Eisenhower Presidential Library Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential LibraryMicrosoft Word - CABINET.docJune 12, 2008...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 12, 2008</span></span></span> After the Cabinet meeting, Eisenhower met Budget Director Rowland Hughes and Deputy Director Percival Brundage off the record to discuss the 1956 budget — again showing that the appointment book did not automatically suppress meetings because they were not for public disclosure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

At 11 a.m. Eisenhower signed legislation authorising a congressional gold medal for Irving Berlin. The book then records an off-the-record meeting involving Secretary of the Navy Charles Thomas and Sherman Adams, followed at 12.15 p.m. by a group of members of Congress. Shortly after 1 p.m., Eisenhower left the White House for Congressional Country Club, where he had lunch and played golf. The appointment book records his golfing companions and his later return to the White House.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

There is consequently no obvious slot labelled as the “already scheduled White House meeting” into which the alleged MJ-12 briefing was inserted. More importantly, there is no entry placing Twining with Eisenhower at any point that day. In its 1987 response on the MJ-12 controversy, the Eisenhower Library therefore concluded that the appointment books contained no special 16 July meeting that might have included an MJ-12 briefing and stressed that even off-the-record presidential meetings normally carried times and participant information. The National Archives incorporated that finding into its MJ-12 reference report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Could the briefing simply have been too secret to record?

Absolute exclusion is impossible. An appointment book is an administrative record, not an electronic tracking system, and no responsible historical argument can prove from a blank entry that Eisenhower could not have spoken privately to somebody for a few minutes. A clandestine meeting could theoretically have been omitted, misrecorded or arranged outside the normal process.

There is also a narrower argument made by defenders of the Cutler–Twining document: because it refers to an “already scheduled White House meeting”, not explicitly to an appointment **with Eisenhower**, perhaps the underlying meeting occurred elsewhere within the White House organisation and therefore would not necessarily appear in the President’s personal appointment book. Friedman and later defenders have emphasised this distinction and have also pointed to Lay’s 14 July access to Eisenhower as a possible mechanism by which instructions could have been transmitted while Cutler was abroad.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span>

That qualification prevents the appointment book from functioning as a stand-alone disproof. But it does not turn the missing entry into neutral evidence. The disputed memorandum itself attributes the scheduling decision to “The President”, orders Twining to change his plans and places the briefing at the White House on a particular day. Against that claim stand unusually detailed presidential records which capture off-the-record encounters, name senior national-security participants and record Twining both the day before and the following Monday — yet show neither him nor an MJ-12-like presidential briefing on the 16th.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

There is a second independent scheduling problem. The National Archives searched the official NSC meeting-minute files and found no NSC meeting on 16 July. Its search of every NSC meeting minute for July 1954 found neither “MJ-12” nor “Majestic”, while searches of NSC indexes under those names and several UFO-related terms also produced no relevant listing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span> Thus, interpreting “White House meeting” broadly avoids one objection but does not supply the missing event with affirmative documentary support.

## Why the missing meeting matters to the MJ-12 claim


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The appointment-book discrepancy is strongest when treated as a problem of **corroboration**, rather than as proof by absence. If the Cutler–Twining memorandum were authentic, its precise scheduling language creates several opportunities for independent records to agree with it: Eisenhower’s appointments might show Twining or the underlying White House meeting; Twining’s official schedule might reflect the altered plans; NSC records might contain a corresponding session or administrative trace; or related White House files might identify the meeting into which the briefing was supposedly moved.

</div>

The presidential records do something more useful than simply remain silent. They show that the record-keeping system was operating around the disputed event. On 14 July it captured Lay briefing Eisenhower off the record; on 15 July it captured the regular NSC meeting and Twining’s White House luncheon attendance; on 16 July it documented another off-the-record breakfast, the Cabinet meeting, an off-the-record budget discussion, other appointments and Eisenhower’s departure for golf; and on 19 July it again recorded Twining meeting the President alongside Radford.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span> This continuity makes the missing 16 July encounter harder to dismiss as merely a general defect in the surviving calendar.

It is also significant that the National Archives did not rely on the appointment book alone. Its MJ-12 reference report records unsuccessful searches in related records of the Secretary of Defense, Joint Chiefs of Staff, Headquarters US Air Force and National Security Council, as well as the negative NSC minutes and index searches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span> The appointment-book gap therefore fits a wider pattern: the alleged memorandum describes an administratively specific event, but researchers have not found the independent governmental paperwork that would securely anchor that event in July 1954.

The fairest conclusion is consequently narrower than “the calendar proves MJ-12 was impossible”, but stronger than “a secret meeting would not be written down anyway”. Eisenhower’s records demonstrate that his White House staff did record off-the-record and sensitive meetings, often with times and named participants. They record Twining around the relevant date but not with Eisenhower on 16 July. They document a full presidential schedule that day without identifying the special briefing anticipated by the disputed memorandum. Combined with the absence of a corresponding NSC meeting or other corroborating official record, that meeting gap remains a concrete and unresolved weakness in the Cutler–Twining memorandum’s claim to describe a real White House event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/research/presidential-appointment-books" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on eisenhowerlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>
