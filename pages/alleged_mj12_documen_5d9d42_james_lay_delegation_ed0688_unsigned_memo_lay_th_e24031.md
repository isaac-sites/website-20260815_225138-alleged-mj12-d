## Introduction

The Cutler–[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum’s missing signature is genuinely relevant to the James Lay explanation, but it does not settle the authorship question. The disputed 14 July 1954 memorandum ends with Robert Cutler’s typed name and title yet carries neither Cutler’s signature nor an “/s/” notation indicating that an original had been signed. That absence helps [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }})’s argument in one limited respect: because Cutler was overseas, the surviving document does not falsely depict him as personally signing it in Washington. Friedman therefore proposed that James S. Lay Jr., Executive Secretary of the National Security Council (NSC), could have prepared the short notice while handling Cutler’s business.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_james_lay_delegation_ed0688_unsigned_memo_lay_th_e24031-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Unsigned Memo illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_james_lay_delegation_ed0688_unsigned_memo_lay_th_e24031-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_james_lay_delegation_ed0688_unsigned_memo_lay_th_e24031-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

The difficulty is that the same blank signature space creates the opposite evidential problem. National Archives investigators knew that Cutler had explicitly left administrative responsibilities with Lay and J. Patrick Coyne, yet reasoned that an authentic memorandum prepared under that delegation should have identified one of them as the acting official.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024</span></span></span> The unsigned carbon therefore removes one conspicuous contradiction without supplying positive evidence that Lay wrote it.

## Why Friedman treated the missing signature as significant

Friedman’s argument begins with a useful distinction between the name typed at the bottom of a memorandum and evidence that the named official personally signed it. The Cutler–Twining document displays “ROBERT CUTLER” and his [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) title, but the surviving sheet has no handwritten signature and, according to Friedman’s description, no “/s/” beside the typed name. He contrasted that with two other Cutler-to-Twining memoranda available for comparison: one bore a signature, while another reportedly used “/s/” to indicate that the original had been signed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

That distinction mattered once researchers established that Cutler was not in Washington. The National Archives and Eisenhower Library found that he was visiting military installations in Europe and North Africa from 3 to 15 July 1954. They also located Cutler’s 3 July instructions concerning how Lay and Coyne should handle NSC administrative business during his absence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024</span></span></span>

For Friedman, the combination was suggestive. A document carrying an actual Cutler signature dated 14 July would have created an obvious difficulty. An “/s/” notation representing an original Cutler signature would have raised much the same question. Instead, the disputed carbon has neither. Friedman argued that this was exactly what might be expected if someone else prepared a brief administrative communication in Cutler’s name while Cutler was away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

He pushed the point further by making it an argument about the knowledge available to a hypothetical forger. Friedman maintained that the Cutler–Twining memorandum was found before sceptical researcher Robert Todd uncovered the evidence establishing Cutler’s absence. On that chronology, Friedman asked why a forger who supposedly did not know that Cutler was abroad would have refrained both from forging a signature and from adding the easier “/s/” notation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

This is probably the strongest use that can reasonably be made of the missing signature in support of the Lay theory. It shows that the physical form of the surviving memorandum does not require Cutler to have personally signed it on 14 July. It also makes the simplest version of the objection — “Cutler was abroad, therefore he could not possibly have issued this memorandum” — too categorical. His absence establishes a problem requiring explanation; it does not, by itself, establish who typed or authorised an unsigned document bearing his name.

But Friedman's further “how did the forger know?” argument is less decisive. It depends not merely on the document being unsigned, but on assumptions about what a forger would normally have done and what historical information was realistically discoverable before the memorandum surfaced. An unsigned document does not require advance knowledge of Cutler’s whereabouts: a fabricator could omit a signature for many reasons, including uncertainty about reproducing one convincingly. The absence is therefore compatible with Friedman's scenario without being uniquely predicted by it.

## Why archivists expected Lay or Coyne to identify themselves

The National Archives drew almost the reverse inference from Cutler’s delegation. Its 1987 reference report noted both Cutler’s overseas trip and his 3 July memorandum directing James Lay and J. Patrick Coyne to deal with NSC administrative matters during his absence. It then stated that, if the Twining memorandum were genuine, one would expect Lay or Coyne to have signed it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://raids.org/files/ufo2/mj12doc6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raids.org">[Raids]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raids.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATIONAL ARCHIVES Washington, DC 20408 Date: July 22, 1987 Reply to attn of: Military Refe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 1987</span></span></span>

That is an important criticism because it does not depend on denying that Lay could act while Cutler was absent. It accepts precisely the administrative circumstance on which the Lay theory depends and asks what documentary trace that circumstance should have produced. If Lay was the responsible official preparing and transmitting the message, why does the document give the reader no visible indication of him?

A contemporary comparison makes that question harder to dismiss. A separate NSC memorandum dated **14 July 1954**, concerning the Aircraft Nuclear Propulsion Program, was issued to the Secretary of Defense and the Chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission under **James S. Lay Jr.’s own name and title as Executive Secretary**. The surviving material therefore provides an example from the very same day of Lay communicating a presidential decision through an NSC memorandum while openly identifying himself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/just-cause-new-series-just-cause-1988-03-no-15-new-series/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Magazine Archive JUST CAUSE — Just Cause</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Magazine ArchiveJUST CAUSE — Just Cause - New Series &#124; UFO Magazine Archive...</span></span></span>

That comparison does not prove that every message Lay handled had to follow the same format. The aircraft nuclear propulsion memorandum had two senior recipients and dealt with formal NSC and atomic-energy business; the alleged Cutler–Twining memorandum is only a few sentences changing arrangements for a briefing. Friedman explicitly argued that this difference mattered: he portrayed the latter as a brief notification that Lay could plausibly have sent in Cutler’s name rather than as a formal Lay memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/IUR%20issues/IUR%20Vol.%2015%20No.%202%20March-April%201990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies.,4a,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a.,f i &amp;:,&amp; I nternational UF O Reporter...</span></span></span>

Even so, the same-day document changes the evidential balance. It shows that Lay was not generally obliged to disappear behind Cutler’s name when communicating presidential or NSC business during Cutler’s absence. Consequently, “Lay was handling Cutler’s work” and “Lay would naturally have issued this particular memorandum with only Cutler’s typed name” are two different propositions. The historical evidence supports the first much better than the second.

## The unsigned carbon cuts both ways

The most useful way to assess the signature issue is to separate three questions that can otherwise become blurred: whether Cutler personally signed the memorandum, whether Lay could have prepared it, and whether the surviving document provides evidence that Lay actually did so.

On the first question, the document favours the Lay explanation in a modest but real sense. There is no visible Cutler signature to reconcile with his documented absence. Friedman was therefore justified in pointing out that the memorandum does not literally claim, through a signature or substitute notation, that Cutler personally executed the surviving communication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

On the second question, the wider record establishes administrative plausibility. Cutler left instructions for Lay and Coyne to keep his work moving, and Friedman later obtained a 16 July message from Lay to Cutler that discussed ongoing NSC matters and said Lay would try to have matters tidy before Cutler returned. Friedman's published account treats that message as evidence that Lay was actively looking after business while Cutler was away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/IUR%20issues/IUR%20Vol.%2015%20No.%202%20March-April%201990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies.,4a,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a.,f i &amp;:,&amp; I nternational UF O Reporter...</span></span></span> This makes delegation a historically grounded possibility rather than an explanation invented solely because the MJ-12 memorandum required one.

The third question is where the case becomes much weaker. Nothing visible on the Cutler–Twining sheet identifies Lay. There is no “James S. Lay”, no initials, no “for” notation, no acting-official notation and no Lay signature. The National Archives’ objection therefore remains pertinent: once Lay is proposed as the actual author or issuing official, the memorandum itself offers no affirmative authorship marker connecting him to it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This distinction is easy to miss because an absence of evidence can perform two different argumentative roles. The missing Cutler signature is **negative evidence against personal execution by Cutler**, which fits what is independently known about his trip. But it is not **positive evidence for execution by Lay**. To make that second inference requires external information about delegation, working relationships and Lay’s activities.

Friedman supplied some of that external context. He reported that Lay met President Eisenhower on 14 July and spoke with him by telephone later that afternoon; he also cited former White House aide George Elsey as considering it natural that Lay could have prepared such a brief notice for Cutler.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span> Those points strengthen the possibility of Lay’s involvement. They still do not convert the blank signature area into an identifying feature.

## What the carbon copy can and cannot prove

The fact that the disputed sheet has been described as a carbon or [ribbon copy]({{ 'ribbon-copy/' | relative_url }}) further [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) what can safely be inferred from its lack of handwriting. A copy need not reproduce every physical feature of an original, and administrative files can preserve copies rather than the dispatched version. The National Archives’ own examination described the sheet as a ribbon copy on dictation onionskin, while Friedman described it as an onionskin carbon and emphasised the absence of both signature and “/s/”. The descriptions differ in terminology, another reason not to make the signature question carry more weight than the surviving artefact permits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://raids.org/files/ufo2/mj12doc6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raids.org">[Raids]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raids.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATIONAL ARCHIVES Washington, DC 20408 Date: July 22, 1987 Reply to attn of: Military Refe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 1987</span></span></span>

More importantly, the missing signature cannot independently distinguish several competing scenarios. An authentic unsigned office copy prepared by Lay could look this way. An authentic draft never sent could potentially be unsigned. A misplaced or incompletely processed document could be unsigned. And a fabricated document could deliberately or accidentally omit a signature. The blank space alone does not discriminate among those possibilities.

That is why the archival expectation of a Lay or Coyne signature is better understood as a **documentary anomaly**, not a standalone proof of fabrication. Office practice is rarely so uniform that one anomalous signature treatment establishes forgery by itself. Friedman's same-day distinction — a formal memorandum may have been handled differently from a short [scheduling note]({{ 'scheduling-note/' | relative_url }}) — is a legitimate caution against turning an expected convention into an absolute rule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/IUR%20issues/IUR%20Vol.%2015%20No.%202%20March-April%201990.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Center for UFO Studies.,4a,</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">a.,f i &amp;:,&amp; I nternational UF O Reporter...</span></span></span>

Yet the same caution applies in the other direction. The fact that an unsigned memorandum *could* have been produced for Cutler does not authenticate it. The National Archives found no other document in the folder concerning “NSC/MJ-12”, no corresponding MJ-12 entry in NSC indices, and no record of the alleged project in its searches. Those broader problems cannot be solved simply by identifying an administrative scenario under which the lack of a signature would be possible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://raids.org/files/ufo2/mj12doc6.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raids.org">[Raids]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raids.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NATIONAL ARCHIVES Washington, DC 20408 Date: July 22, 1987 Reply to attn of: Military Refe...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 22, 1987</span></span></span>

## Does the missing signature help the Lay theory?

Yes, but chiefly as a defensive argument. The absence of Cutler’s signature prevents his overseas trip from becoming the straightforward contradiction it would have been had the memorandum visibly represented him as personally signing it on 14 July 1954. Together with Cutler’s documented delegation of work, it leaves open a plausible route by which somebody in Washington — including Lay — could have handled a brief communication associated with Cutler’s responsibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024</span></span></span>

It is considerably weaker as affirmative evidence that Lay actually wrote the Cutler–Twining memorandum. The document contains no mark identifying him, while the National Archives specifically expected Lay or Coyne to identify themselves if acting for the absent Cutler. The existence of a separate 14 July memorandum openly issued under Lay’s own name demonstrates that Lay was capable of communicating presidential decisions that day without concealing his role behind Cutler’s typed signature block.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The fairest conclusion is therefore asymmetric. **The missing signature helps rebut one objection to the Lay theory more effectively than it proves the theory itself.** It makes Lay authorship administratively possible and avoids a false Cutler signature, but it supplies no internal attribution to Lay. For evaluating the alleged MJ-12 document, that leaves the unsigned ending as an ambiguity rather than an authentication feature: compatible with the delegation explanation, compatible with other explanations, and insufficient on its own to establish who prepared the memorandum.
