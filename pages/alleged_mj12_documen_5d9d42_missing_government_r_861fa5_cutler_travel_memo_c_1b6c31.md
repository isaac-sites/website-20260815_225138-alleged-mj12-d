## Introduction

The disputed Cutler–[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum is dated 14 July 1954 and carries the typed name of Robert Cutler, President Dwight D. Eisenhower's Special Assistant for National Security Affairs. Yet contemporary government records place Cutler overseas from 3 to 15 July, visiting military installations in Europe and North Africa. Before leaving, he had explicitly delegated National Security Council (NSC) administrative work to James S. Lay Jr and J. Patrick Coyne. The National Archives therefore identified a straightforward provenance problem: if a genuine memorandum had to be sent from Cutler's office while he was away, why does it appear under Cutler's name rather than that of an official handling his work?<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_missing_government_r_861fa5_cutler_travel_memo_c_1b6c31-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Cutler Travel illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_missing_government_r_861fa5_cutler_travel_memo_c_1b6c31-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/alleged_mj12_documen_5d9d42_missing_government_r_861fa5_cutler_travel_memo_c_1b6c31-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

That discrepancy does not, by itself, prove that the memorandum was fabricated. An aide could conceivably have drafted correspondence in an absent superior's name. Indeed, MJ-12 advocate [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}) argued that Lay was the likely actual author. But that explanation changes the authentication question rather than resolving it: evidence is then needed that Lay really prepared or authorised this particular memorandum. The surviving government record establishes that Lay was doing NSC business in Washington; it does not independently connect him to an MJ-12 communication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

## Cutler's July 1954 travel schedule

The key chronology comes from records preserved in the Eisenhower administration's files. According to the National Archives' MJ-12 reference report, Cutler travelled at Eisenhower's direction to inspect overseas military installations between **3 and 15 July 1954**. A report to the President dated 20 July documented his visits in Europe and North Africa. That places Cutler abroad throughout the period immediately preceding the supposed 16 July briefing and, crucially, on **14 July**, the date printed on the Cutler–Twining memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This is stronger evidence than simply noting that Cutler was absent from an appointment diary on one particular day. The Archives cited both his post-trip report and a second document created as he was leaving: a **3 July memorandum** in the NSC Staff Papers in which Cutler told James S. Lay Jr and J. Patrick Coyne how administrative matters were to be handled during his absence. The travel and the delegation arrangements therefore reinforce one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The President's surviving appointment books fit the same chronology. At the regular NSC meeting on 15 July, the recorded participants included Lay and Coyne but not Cutler. General Nathan Twining, the supposed recipient of the disputed memorandum, appears elsewhere in that day's appointments, attending a luncheon for Libyan Prime Minister Mustafa Ben Halim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

The resulting timeline is unusually specific:

* **3 July:** Cutler's overseas trip begins, and he leaves instructions for Lay and Coyne concerning NSC administrative work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>
* **14 July:** the disputed memorandum to Twining is dated, while Cutler is still overseas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>
* **15 July:** Cutler's recorded travel period ends; Lay and Coyne are present for NSC business in Washington.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>
* **16 July:** the date on which the disputed memorandum says the changed briefing arrangements were to take effect. Eisenhower's appointment book records Cabinet business and several other engagements, but no MJ-12 briefing or corresponding special [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}) meeting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>
* **20 July:** Cutler's report to Eisenhower records the recently completed European and North African trip.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The central point is therefore not merely that Cutler might have been difficult to reach. He had planned an extended official absence, documented it, and arranged for other officials to deal with his NSC workload.

## Who was handling Cutler's NSC business?

The strongest alternative to direct Cutler authorship centres on **James S. Lay Jr**, the NSC's Executive Secretary. The contemporary appointment book confirms that Lay remained active at the White House while Cutler was away. On 14 July itself, Lay and Deputy Executive Secretary S. Everett Gleason met Eisenhower **off the record** and briefed the President on NSC affairs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

That entry matters because it prevents an overly simple argument against the disputed memorandum. Cutler's absence does not establish that his office was incapable of sending a message to Twining. Senior NSC personnel were in Washington, dealing directly with Eisenhower, and Lay was one of the people whom Cutler had designated to keep administrative business moving. A genuine last-minute scheduling instruction could therefore have been transmitted without Cutler physically being in Washington.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

This is essentially Friedman's defence of the authorship anomaly. He acknowledged that Cutler was abroad and that the memorandum carries neither Cutler's handwritten signature nor an `/s/` notation indicating a signed original. Friedman proposed that Lay could have prepared the short [scheduling note]({{ 'scheduling-note/' | relative_url }}) on Cutler's behalf. He further pointed to Lay's documented contact with Eisenhower on 14 July and to evidence that Lay was taking care of Cutler's business during the trip.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

There is a real distinction, however, between showing that **Lay could have sent such a message** and demonstrating that **Lay sent this message**. The official appointment book corroborates Lay's presence and his NSC briefing of Eisenhower, but it says nothing about MJ-12, a special-studies project or an instruction to Twining. Friedman's proposed authorship is consequently a plausible administrative scenario rather than an independently documented chain of provenance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

Coyne presents a similar issue. Cutler's 3 July instructions identified him alongside Lay as someone responsible for matters during the absence, and the 15 July NSC roster confirms Coyne's presence as the NSC's internal-security representative. The National Archives accordingly reasoned that an authentic memorandum generated during Cutler's absence might be expected to bear Lay's or Coyne's name.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## The unsigned memo cuts both ways

The disputed document itself is a very short scheduling memorandum addressed to General Twining. It says that the President has decided an “MJ-12 SSP” briefing should occur **during** an already scheduled White House meeting on 16 July rather than afterwards, and asks Twining to change his plans. At the bottom appear Cutler's typed name and title. The surviving document has no Cutler signature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://luforu.org/cutler-twining-memo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luforu.org">[LUFORU]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luforu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Cutler-Twining memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cutler-Twining memoJuly 14, 1954...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 14, 1954</span></span></span>

For critics, the lack of a signature does not solve the travel problem. It instead leaves the memorandum without a named individual demonstrably authorising it on 14 July. Cutler was overseas, while the people formally assigned to handle his work are not identified on the document. The National Archives summarised the difficulty by observing that one would expect Lay or Coyne to have signed an authentic memorandum issued in these circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

For Friedman, however, the same feature could be interpreted in the opposite direction. If Lay prepared a routine scheduling note from Cutler's office while Cutler was abroad, the absence of a false Cutler signature might be exactly what one would expect. Friedman argued that this was consistent with Lay acting for Cutler rather than evidence against authenticity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

That defence has an important limitation. It can explain why **Cutler did not sign** the memorandum, but it does not supply affirmative evidence that **Lay did write it**. There is no cited contemporaneous routing slip, diary entry, outgoing correspondence register, carbon-file copy or receiving-office record identifying Lay as the author of this particular communication. The official evidence establishes the administrative possibility; the disputed sheet remains the evidence for the alleged MJ-12 content.

## The White House records sharpen the conflict

The appointment books provide a useful control because they show that the Eisenhower White House did record sensitive or informal activity rather than restricting the diary to ceremonial engagements. On 14 July, Lay and Gleason's NSC briefing is explicitly marked **“OFF THE RECORD”**. Other off-the-record meetings also appear throughout the book.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

This undercuts the idea that an unlisted event necessarily disappeared simply because it was secret or informal. The Eisenhower [Presidential Library]({{ 'library-searches/' | relative_url }}) specifically told the National Archives that even the President's off-the-record meetings were normally represented in the appointment books by their times and participants. Its search found no entry for a special 16 July meeting that might correspond to an MJ-12 briefing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The surviving 16 July schedule is quite detailed. It records breakfast with Senator William Knowland, a meeting with the Federal Farm Credit Board, a Cabinet meeting, an off-the-record budget discussion, legislation-related appearances, another off-the-record engagement and Eisenhower's departure for Congressional Country Club shortly after 1 p.m. The record does not identify the meeting described by the Cutler–Twining memorandum.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

There is nevertheless a nuance worth preserving. The disputed text says only that the briefing would occur during an **“already scheduled White House meeting”**; it does not explicitly call that event a formal NSC meeting. Friedman and other defenders have therefore argued that searching only for an NSC meeting sets too narrow a test.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span>

That qualification is fair, but it does not remove the larger evidential gap. The National Archives also searched the NSC meeting minutes for July and the NSC indexes for “MJ-12”, “Majestic” and several UFO-related terms without finding corroboration. Thus neither the presidential schedule nor the associated [NSC records]({{ 'nsc-records/' | relative_url }}) supply the independent administrative trace that would turn the Lay hypothesis into documented provenance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## What the timing means for the memorandum's provenance


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Cutler's travel should not be treated as a standalone “gotcha” proving forgery. Government offices routinely produce documents for absent senior officials, and the contemporary records show that Lay was exactly where an acting intermediary would need to be: in Washington, handling NSC affairs and briefing Eisenhower on 14 July.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/online-documents/presidential-appointment-books/1954/july-1954.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eisenhowerlibrary.gov">[Eisenhower Presidential Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eisenhowerlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Eisenhower Presidential Library</span></span></span>

</div>

But authentication works in the other direction as well. Once direct authorship by Cutler becomes unlikely, the document needs another credible route of creation. The obvious candidate is Lay, yet the historical record located so far supports only the surrounding circumstances: Cutler delegated work to him; Lay remained on duty; and Lay met Eisenhower on the memorandum's date. None of those facts independently records the alleged MJ-12 message.

That distinction is particularly important because the National Archives does **not** claim that physical custody authenticates the memorandum. Its reference report expressly distinguishes certifying that a reproduction matches a document held in its files from authenticating the document's origin or the truth of its contents. The Cutler–Twining sheet was found in government records, but the Archives also found no additional MJ-12 material in its folder and no further corroborating information in related Defence Department, Joint Chiefs, Air Force or NSC records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The most defensible conclusion is therefore narrower than either “Cutler was abroad, so the memo must be fake” or “Lay could have written it, so the travel problem disappears”. **Cutler almost certainly was not personally composing and signing White House correspondence in Washington on 14 July 1954; his documented travel establishes that much. An authorised deputy could theoretically have produced the memorandum in his name, and Lay's activities make that scenario administratively plausible. But no independent government record yet identified establishes that Lay or Coyne actually created, cleared, dispatched or received this particular MJ-12 communication.**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

Within the wider missing-records problem surrounding [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}), that is why Cutler's journey matters. It does more than create an awkward date. It transfers the burden of provenance from a named senior official who was demonstrably abroad to an unnamed or hypothetical intermediary — precisely where an authentic administrative trail would be most useful, and where the surviving corroborating record is conspicuously thin.
