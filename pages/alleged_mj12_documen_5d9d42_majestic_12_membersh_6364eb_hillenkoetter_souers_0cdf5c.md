## Introduction

Roscoe H. [Hillenkoetter]({{ 'hillenkoetter/' | relative_url }}) and Sidney W. Souers were unusually effective names for the alleged Majestic-12 roster because they represented two successive stages in the creation of America’s post-war central intelligence system. Souers was the first Director of Central Intelligence (DCI), heading the Central Intelligence Group (CIG) in 1946, and later became the first Executive Secretary of the National Security Council (NSC). Hillenkoetter became DCI in May 1947 and then the first director of the Central Intelligence Agency when the CIA replaced the CIG that September.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/intro4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the Historian...</span></span></span>

That chronology made both men plausible choices for an alleged secret organisation supposedly created by President Harry Truman on 24 September 1947. The purported 1952 MJ-12 briefing names Hillenkoetter as both a member and its briefing officer, designated “MJ-1”, while listing Souers among [the twelve]({{ 'the-twelve/' | relative_url }}) members.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span> Yet their genuine positions demonstrate plausibility, not authenticity. Federal archival searches have found no official record establishing MJ-12, and the FBI says an [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) investigation determined the principal document to be fake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## Souers and the Central Intelligence Group

Souers matters to the alleged roster because he was not merely an early intelligence official who happened to precede the CIA. He helped shape the institutional system from which the CIA emerged. The State Department’s official historical record says that, while serving in naval intelligence, Souers contributed to the Eberstadt Report, advocated a unified intelligence system and helped Clark Clifford transform an interdepartmental proposal into the presidential directive Truman signed on 22 January 1946. Truman appointed him the first Director of Central Intelligence the following day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/intro4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the Historian...</span></span></span>

The organisation Souers initially headed was the Central Intelligence Group rather than the CIA. That distinction is important when evaluating the logic of the MJ-12 list. The CIG was an institutional predecessor of the CIA, created while Washington was still working out how a permanent peacetime central-intelligence apparatus should operate. CIA historical material accordingly treats Souers and his successor Hoyt Vandenberg as DCIs who ran the CIG before the CIA formally existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-counting-cia-directors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ask Molly: Counting CIA Directors</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ask Molly: Counting CIA Directors - CIAFebruary 10, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2025</span></span></span>

Souers served only briefly as DCI, from January to June 1946, but his relevance to the national-security system did not end when he left the CIG. After the National Security Act of 1947 created the National Security Council, he became its first Executive Secretary. The Truman Library dates that service from 1947 to 1950, after which Souers continued as a special consultant to Truman on military and foreign affairs through 1953.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/personal-papers/sidney-w-souers-papers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Truman Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Truman Library Souers, Sidney W. Papers &#124; Harry S. Truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Truman Library Souers, Sidney W. Papers &#124; Harry S. Truman</span></span></span> CIA historical scholarship likewise describes him as an important figure in the emergence of CIA authority even after his short tenure as DCI.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/volume-65-no-2-june-2021/the-quiet-warrior-rear-admiral-sidney-souers-and-the-emergence-of-cias-covert-action-authority-dr-bianca-adair/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cia.gov.</span></span></span>

That later position is especially significant for understanding why Souers fits the alleged MJ-12 narrative so neatly. The supposed committee was described as answerable directly to the President. Souers had genuine links on both sides of precisely that imagined institutional boundary: he had headed the new central-intelligence organisation and then worked at the NSC, the body established to coordinate national-security policy for the President. His name therefore supplied the alleged roster with continuity between intelligence organisation and presidential-level national-security coordination.

There is even a surviving documentary example of Souers occupying that connecting role alongside Hillenkoetter. On 17 December 1947, Executive Secretary Souers transmitted an NSC directive on psychological operations to DCI Hillenkoetter. Hillenkoetter had corresponded with Souers about the proposed directive two days earlier, discussing security for covert operations and interdepartmental coordination. These are genuine Top Secret national-security records preserved through official archival channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d257" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span></span></span> They do not mention MJ-12, but they show why placing the two men together in a fictional secret committee could appear institutionally credible.

## Hillenkoetter and the new CIA

Hillenkoetter supplied an even stronger chronological anchor. He succeeded Hoyt Vandenberg as Director of Central Intelligence on 1 May 1947, several months before the date on which the alleged MJ-12 document says Truman created the committee. When the National Security Act reorganised the intelligence structure that September, the CIG became the Central Intelligence Agency and Hillenkoetter continued as DCI, making him the CIA’s first director.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp81s00991r000200060007-9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[cia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cia.gov.</span></span></span>

That timing helps explain why the alleged briefing does more than simply include him among twelve prominent officials. It identifies Hillenkoetter as “MJ-1” and makes him the officer supposedly briefing President-elect Dwight Eisenhower in November 1952.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/MJ-12_Document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: en.wikisource.org">[Wikisource]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">en.wikisource.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MJ-12 Document</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MJ-12 Document - Wikisource, the free online libraryNovember 18, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 18, 1952</span></span></span> In narrative terms, this assigns the central intelligence chief serving at the alleged committee’s creation a position of continuity and authority within the secret programme.

His real tenure also coincided with the rapid expansion of early Cold War intelligence responsibilities. CIA’s historical account describes Hillenkoetter’s years as encompassing growing demands for intelligence analysis and the development of a covert-action mission, including the creation of the Office of Policy Coordination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/books-monographs/cia-cold-war-records-the-cia-under-harry-truman-1945-1952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cia cold war records the cia under harry truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CIA Cold War Records: The CIA Under Harry Truman, 1945–1952 - CSI...</span></span></span> A US government history of the intelligence community likewise records that Hillenkoetter organised the new CIA around intelligence operations and analysis, although he struggled to secure effective coordination across the older departmental intelligence services.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-INTELLIGENCE/html/int022.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on govinfo.gov.</span></span></span>

The historical Hillenkoetter was therefore not quite the all-powerful intelligence chief that the “MJ-1” label might suggest. CIA’s own retrospective account says he exercised little control over the semi-autonomous Office of Policy Coordination and that the Agency came under substantial criticism during his tenure. The 1949 review associated with Allen Dulles, William Jackson and Mathias Correa pressed for significant reforms, while Hillenkoetter eventually left the DCI post in October 1950.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/books-monographs/cia-cold-war-records-the-cia-under-harry-truman-1945-1952/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cia cold war records the cia under harry truman</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CIA Cold War Records: The CIA Under Harry Truman, 1945–1952 - CSI...</span></span></span> This contrast matters: the alleged document can exploit the prestige of his job title without reproducing the messier institutional [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) of the real early CIA.

Hillenkoetter has another feature that later made his presence in MJ-12 lore particularly compelling: after retiring from government intelligence, he became associated with civilian UFO investigation. The Naval History and Heritage Command records him among the governors of the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP), a prominent civilian UFO organisation founded in the 1950s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/bibliographies-and-research-guides/research-guides/ufo-research-guide.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command UFO Research Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command UFO Research Guide</span></span></span> That is a genuine later connection, but it is not evidence that he belonged to MJ-12 in 1947. The chronology instead requires care: Hillenkoetter’s public involvement with NICAP came roughly a decade after the alleged committee’s formation.

## Why both names made the roster persuasive

Taken together, Souers and Hillenkoetter create an unusually convincing piece of institutional scenery. Souers represents the construction of central intelligence before the CIA existed; Hillenkoetter represents the transition into the statutory CIA. Their careers overlap the exact period in which the alleged MJ-12 story places the creation of its secret presidential committee.

The pairing works on several levels:

* **Institutional continuity:** Souers was the first DCI of the CIG, while Hillenkoetter was the DCI who carried the central-intelligence system into the newly created CIA.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/ask-molly-counting-cia-directors/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ask Molly: Counting CIA Directors</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ask Molly: Counting CIA Directors - CIAFebruary 10, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 10, 2025</span></span></span>
* **Access to presidential national-security machinery:** Souers moved from central intelligence into the NSC executive secretariat, while Hillenkoetter headed the central intelligence organisation serving that new national-security structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/truman-papers/papers-harry-s-truman-staff-member-and-office-files-national-security-council" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trumanlibrary.gov">[Truman Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trumanlibrary.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on trumanlibrary.gov.</span></span></span>
* **A genuine working relationship:** official records preserve direct exchanges between Souers and Hillenkoetter on classified national-security matters in late 1947.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1945-50Intel/d254" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span></span></span>
* **Chronological fit:** Hillenkoetter was DCI and Souers was entering his NSC role when the alleged document places MJ-12’s establishment in September 1947.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/document/cia-rdp81s00991r000200060007-9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cia.gov.</span></span></span>
* **Later UFO resonance:** Hillenkoetter’s subsequent NICAP involvement gave his name an authentic connection with the UFO controversy, although that later activity cannot authenticate claims about an earlier secret committee.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/bibliographies-and-research-guides/research-guides/ufo-research-guide.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Naval History and Heritage Command UFO Research Guide</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage Command UFO Research Guide</span></span></span>

This is precisely where plausibility can be mistaken for corroboration. A fabricated classified document is more convincing when it assigns secret duties to people whose public careers make those duties seem possible. Souers and Hillenkoetter were not arbitrary selections: contemporary records confirm that both genuinely operated close to Truman-era intelligence and national-security decision-making.

But the same archival record that establishes those impressive credentials has not supplied the missing link to Majestic-12 itself. The National Archives reports extensive searches of Air Force, Joint Chiefs of Staff, NSC, Truman Library and Eisenhower Library holdings without finding records establishing the alleged organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span> The Government Accountability Office similarly reported that federal archivists found no evidence that the submitted Majestic-12 material originated in the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAO</span></span></span> The FBI states more directly that the Air Force investigation of the purported briefing document concluded that it was fake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=Majestic" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI — Federal Bureau of Investigation...</span></span></span>

The distinction is therefore fundamental to reading the claimed membership list. Hillenkoetter and Souers make excellent *intelligence choices* because their real careers map remarkably well onto the sort of presidentially controlled, interdepartmental intelligence body described by the alleged papers. Their documented positions explain why the roster sounds authentic. They do not establish that the roster itself was authentic.
