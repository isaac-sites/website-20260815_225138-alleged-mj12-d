## Introduction

The typewriter argument over [the alleged MJ-12 documents]({{ 'the-alleged-mj-12-documents/' | relative_url }}) is narrower than it is sometimes made to sound. A typeface that did not exist in 1947 or 1952 would be powerful evidence against a document carrying those dates. But the reverse does not follow: showing that the lettering *could* have been produced by a typewriter available in the 1940s merely removes one possible anachronism. It does not demonstrate that the sheet was actually typed then, inside the [White House]({{ 'white-house/' | relative_url }}), or anywhere else in government.

That distinction is especially important for the original MJ-12 material because researchers did not receive the purported government originals. They received photographs on anonymously mailed 35 mm film. The Government Accountability Office (GAO) later reported that federal agencies found no evidence that the circulated MJ-12 material constituted documents originating in the executive branch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAOJuly 28, 1995...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 28, 1995</span></span></span> Typography can therefore test compatibility with a claimed period, but it cannot repair the [missing chain]({{ 'missing-chain/' | relative_url }}) of [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}).

## Claims that the typography was anachronistic

One of the strongest-looking objections to the MJ-12 papers concerned the purported 24 September 1947 Truman-to-[Forrestal]({{ 'forrestal/' | relative_url }}) memorandum, reproduced as an attachment to the Eisenhower briefing material. The dispute centred on whether its typewriting came from a machine or type design introduced only years after 1947.

Philip J. Klass reported in *Skeptical Inquirer* that questioned-document examiner Peter Tytell had identified the typeface as belonging to a Smith-Corona machine that first appeared in 1963. If correct, that finding would be devastating: a document could not have been typed in September 1947 on a machine first available more than 15 years later. Klass also reported that Tytell had examined the disputed Truman signature and regarded it as a transplanted reproduction of Truman's signature on an authentic 1 October 1947 letter to Vannevar Bush.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-02-12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Skeptical Inquirer SKEPTICAL</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">INQUIRER Vol. 14 No. 2/Winter 1990 $6June 27, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 27, 2006</span></span></span>

Later accounts of Tytell's conclusion are not completely uniform about the precise Smith-Corona identification and date. Kevin Randle, who spoke with Tytell, reported the more specific recollection that the face was most consistent with a Smith-Corona P102 used after 1966.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/798430882/Kevin-D-Randle-Case-MJ-12-the-True-Story-Behind-the-Governments-s-UFO-Conspiracies-Speaking-Volumes-LLC-2018" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin D. Randle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin D. Randle - Case MJ-12 - The True Story Behind The Governments&#x27;s UFO Conspiracies-Speaking Volumes, LLC (2018) &#124; PDF &#124; Securi...</span></span></span> That variation is a reason not to present the model identification as an uncontested laboratory result. [Stanton Friedman]({{ 'friedman-s-case/' | relative_url }}), the leading defender of the original MJ-12 documents, emphasised that Tytell's judgement was informal and that no formal written examination report had been produced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/update-on-operation-majestic-12-documents/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Update on Operation Majestic 12 Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultUpdate on Operation Majestic 12 Documents - The Black Vault Case FilesJanuary 17, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 17, 2024</span></span></span>

There was a related but distinct dispute over the typeface of the 1954 Cutler-[Twining]({{ 'twining/' | relative_url }}) memorandum. Klass had argued from samples that its pica typography was inconsistent with National Security Council material of the period. Friedman searched the Eisenhower Library and produced numerous authentic period memoranda that he said met the relevant pica criteria; contemporary correspondence records him saying that Klass paid the agreed $1,000 after Friedman supplied qualifying examples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufomagazines.com/just-cause-new-series-just-cause-1989-09-no-21-new-series/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomagazines.com">[UFO Magazine Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomagazines.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Magazine Archive JUST CAUSE — Just Cause</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Magazine ArchiveJUST CAUSE — Just Cause - New Series &#124; UFO Magazine ArchiveSeptember 1, 1989...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 1, 1989</span></span></span>

That episode is useful because it shows exactly what a successful typewriter rebuttal can accomplish. If authentic 1950s government documents exhibit the supposedly impossible type style, the claim of typographical impossibility fails. It does **not** follow that the disputed MJ-12 document is genuine. Indeed, the National Archives lists independent problems with the Cutler-Twining memorandum: its filing context is anomalous, searches found no supporting MJ-12 records, and Robert Cutler was overseas on the date he supposedly issued it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

## The Underwood comparison cited by defenders

The most direct answer to the late-Smith-Corona allegation came years later. Robert Wood commissioned questioned-document examiner James A. Black to examine the Truman-Forrestal memorandum's typography. In a letter dated 13 November 1998, Black concluded that the visible portions of its type font were consistent with an Underwood Standard and matched an exemplar produced by an Underwood Standard in May 1940.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span>

For defenders such as Friedman, this mattered because an Underwood capable of producing the relevant characters before 1947 directly challenged the simple argument that the typography *must* have originated in the 1960s. Friedman subsequently cited Black's examination when rejecting the assertion that the memorandum necessarily used a 1963 typeface.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theufochronicles.com/2009/04/bird-droppings-and-mj-12-stanton.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufochronicles.com">[The UFO Chronicles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufochronicles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The UFO Chronicles Bird Droppings and MJ-12, Stanton Friedman Responds</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO Chronicles Bird Droppings and MJ-12, Stanton Friedman Responds</span></span></span>

Black's conclusion should nevertheless be stated precisely. It was a conclusion about the compatibility of visible type characteristics with a period machine. The surviving material available to MJ-12 researchers was not the purported original Truman memorandum with an independently documented history. The initial material had arrived photographically, on film from an unidentified sender. A finding that photographed characters resemble output from an Underwood Standard is consequently different from demonstrating that a particular historical Underwood produced an original sheet in Truman's administration.

Black's examination also illustrates why document authentication cannot sensibly be reduced to one typographical feature. According to the published account of his findings, although he regarded the typing as compatible with the Underwood exemplar, he considered the Truman signature most likely to be a reproduction because of characteristics of its reproduced line.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://medium.com/stanton-friedmans-articles/book-review-kevin-d-randles-case-mj-12-the-true-story-behind-the-government-s-ufo-conspiracies-9e06f4e82250" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: medium.com">[Medium]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">medium.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Book Review: Kevin D. Randle’s CASE MJ-12: THE TRUE STORY BEHIND THE GOVERNMENT’S UFO CONSPIRACIES &#124; by Richard Geldreich, Jr. &#124; Ph...</span></span></span> In other words, the same examination cited to defend the period compatibility of the typing did not convert the document as a whole into an authenticated original.

## Why period compatibility cannot date a photographed page

Forensic document examination itself supplies the key distinction. Typewriting contains both **class characteristics** and potentially **individual characteristics**. Class characteristics can help an examiner determine such matters as type style, spacing, manufacturer or possible machine family. Individual characteristics can arise from wear, damaged type, alignment defects and other peculiarities of a particular machine. ASTM's standard guide for typewritten-item examination accordingly covers comparisons between questioned material and known material and procedures for obtaining exemplars from actual typewriters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://store.astm.org/e2494-07.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: store.astm.org">[ASTM Store]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">store.astm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on astm.org.</span></span></span>

That means there are several very different propositions that can easily be blurred together in the MJ-12 debate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Period exclusion:** if a feature demonstrably did not exist until after 1947, a document supposedly typed in 1947 cannot be an original from that date.
* **Period compatibility:** if an Underwood or another suitable machine existed before 1947, the typography is possible for 1947. This establishes a *not-earlier-than* constraint only when an innovation can be securely dated; an older design can still be used decades later.
* **Machine identification:** matching distinctive defects against specimens from a particular physical typewriter can provide much stronger evidence about which machine produced a document.
* **Historical provenance:** even identifying a particular machine would still require evidence connecting that machine, document and transaction to the claimed office, author and date.

</div>

The photographic form of the MJ-12 evidence makes the distinction sharper. FBI guidance has long recognised that a good true-size photograph or photocopy can sometimes suffice to determine the make or spacing of type, while ordinarily being inadequate for identification against a particular machine because minute defects and indentations may not survive reproduction. For that stronger comparison, the original questioned document is preferable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://leb.fbi.gov/file-repository/archives/february-1967.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: leb.fbi.gov">[FBI: Law Enforcement Bulletin]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">leb.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">february 1967</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">february 1967</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: february 1967</span></span></span> Modern FBI material likewise treats typewriting examinations as comparisons of questioned and known materials, including typewriter components, rather than as a method that automatically assigns a calendar date to typed text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fbilabqsd.fbi.gov/file-repository/questioned-documents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbilabqsd.fbi.gov">[FBI Lab Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbilabqsd.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lab Vault Questioned Documents — FBI Lab Vault</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lab Vault Questioned Documents — FBI Lab Vault</span></span></span>

This matters greatly for MJ-12. Suppose, for the sake of argument, that Black's Underwood comparison is completely correct. It would establish that a typewriter capable of producing the observed class characteristics existed by 1940. But an Underwood made before the Second World War could still be obtained and used in 1950, 1960, 1984 or later. Old typewriter technology does not cease to function when a decade ends. A modern fabricator possessing a suitable vintage machine could therefore create text that passes the basic test of period compatibility.

The [anonymous film]({{ 'anonymous-film/' | relative_url }}) adds another layer. With no original sheet, investigators could not straightforwardly examine the paper, its watermark, typing impressions, ribbon transfer, erasures, corrections, ink relationships or other physical relationships that might distinguish an old original from a later production photographed to resemble one. FBI laboratory practice explicitly treats paper comparisons, watermark searches, typewriting examinations, photocopier examinations and document-age questions as distinct forms of questioned-document work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fbilabqsd.fbi.gov/file-repository/questioned-documents" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbilabqsd.fbi.gov">[FBI Lab Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbilabqsd.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Lab Vault Questioned Documents — FBI Lab Vault</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lab Vault Questioned Documents — FBI Lab Vault</span></span></span> A typeface match cannot substitute for all of them.

Reproduction also matters because copies can preserve some useful characteristics while obscuring others. Forensic research on photocopied material shows that copying introduces its own evidential layer: toner, copier defects and reproduction artefacts may become relevant, while details of the source document may be degraded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/defect-marks-and-identification-photocopying-machines" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ojp.gov">[Office of Justice Programs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ojp.gov.</span></span></span> In the MJ-12 case, researchers were another generation removed because the disputed pages were encountered through photographic images rather than through uncontested originals.

## Compatibility is an exclusion test, not a provenance test

The fairest reading of the typewriter controversy is therefore asymmetrical. An unequivocally post-1947 typeface on the Truman-Forrestal memorandum would be strong falsifying evidence. Evidence that a matching Underwood typeface existed in 1940 answers that particular accusation of impossibility, but it does not provide equivalent positive evidence for a 1947 origin.

This is a familiar logical difference in authentication work. A historical document can fail because it contains something that could not yet have existed. Passing that test merely keeps it in consideration. A purported 1947 memorandum also needs evidence that positively ties it to 1947: a trustworthy archival location, contemporary registration or filing information, an accountable custody history, corroborating copies or references, or physical features whose dating is meaningful and resistant to later reproduction.

For the anonymously filmed MJ-12 papers, those stronger forms of provenance are precisely what remain missing. GAO's 1995 inquiry found that the Information Security Oversight Office, Air Force and National Archives knew the MJ-12 material through submissions from non-governmental sources, and that there was no evidence the circulated material represented actual executive-branch documents. Searches of relevant archival holdings also failed to find material fitting the MJ-12 description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/154832" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability Office[Comments on Majestic 12 Material] &#124; U.S. GAOJuly 28, 1995...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 28, 1995</span></span></span> The National Archives similarly reports extensive negative searches of Air Force, Joint Chiefs, presidential-library and National Security Council records for the claimed project.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>

The typewriter evidence should therefore be used for what it can genuinely tell us. The Underwood comparison is relevant because it weakens a categorical claim that the Truman-Forrestal typography was technologically impossible in 1947. It cannot tell us who typed the page, where it was typed, whether the photographed sheet existed in 1947, when it entered government custody, or whether it ever entered government custody at all. In the MJ-12 provenance mystery, a period-compatible typewriter clears one hurdle; it does not establish the document's origin.
