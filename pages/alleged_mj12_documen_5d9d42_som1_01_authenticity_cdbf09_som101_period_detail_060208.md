## Introduction

The period details in SOM1-01 do matter, but they prove considerably less than defenders sometimes claim. References to Kirtland [Air Force]({{ 'air-force/' | relative_url }}) Base, a real Building 21, apparently plausible personnel initials, an older abbreviation for New Mexico and period-looking military language can make the alleged 1954 manual harder to dismiss as a careless modern invention. They show, at most, that its creator had access to some historically appropriate information and took care over the setting.

The difficulty is that historical consistency is not provenance. SOM1-01 reached researcher Don Berliner anonymously in 1994 as photographs on undeveloped 35 mm film; no authenticated government original or documented chain of [custody]({{ 'custody/' | relative_url }}) accompanies it. Even the manual's defenders acknowledge that the absence of the original prevents decisive paper-and-ink testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/source/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on specialoperationsmanual.com.</span></span></span> [Period accuracy]({{ 'period-accuracy/' | relative_url }}) therefore raises the quality of the authenticity argument without establishing that SOM1-01 was genuinely produced by the alleged organisation in the 1950s.

## The Kirtland references are suggestive, not a paper trail

One of the strongest-looking details is the stamp associated with Kirtland Air Force Base. The SOM1-01 proponents' site reports wording identifying “Unit KB-88, Bldg. 21” at Kirtland, while the manual's technology-classification table also directs printed material, recordings, maps, charts, photographs and film to Building 21 KB-88. Robert and Ryan Wood argue that an investigator subsequently confirmed that a Building 21 existed at Kirtland in the relevant era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/source/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on specialoperationsmanual.com.</span></span></span>

That is a worthwhile check. If “Building 21” had proved nonexistent, or if Kirtland had used a completely incompatible numbering system, the discrepancy could have counted against the document. Confirmation removes one possible error. It also suggests that whoever prepared SOM1-01 knew enough about Kirtland to use a location that would survive at least that level of scrutiny.

The wording of the address provides another small period-consistency point. Defenders emphasise the use of “N. MEX.” rather than the modern two-letter postal abbreviation “NM”. “N Mex” is indeed a recognised abbreviation for New Mexico, and period publications provide examples of “N. Mex.” usage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/N%20Mex" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: merriam-webster.com">[Merriam-Webster]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">merriam-webster.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">N MEX Definition &amp; Meaning</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">N MEX Definition &amp; Meaning - Merriam-Webster...</span></span></span> The Woods accordingly treat the form as another sign that the document was composed with 1950s conventions in mind.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/2014/12/30/som1-01-overview-authentication/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOM1-01SOM1-01 Overview &amp; Authentication</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOM1-01December 30, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 30, 2014</span></span></span>

The evidential limit is easy to miss, however. Confirming that a building existed does not confirm “KB-88” as a genuine secret unit, much less establish that an MJ-12 organisation occupied the building. An accessible modern summary of the dispute notes that designations such as “Building 21 KB-88” and the manual's other purported special facilities have not been independently located in authenticated government records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wikidisc.org/wiki/Special_Operations_Manual_%28SOM1-01%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wikidisc.org">[WikiDisc]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wikidisc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wiki Disc Special Operations Manual (SOM1-01) &#124; Wiki Disc</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Special Operations Manual (SOM1-01) &#124; WikiDiscJuly 4, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 2026</span></span></span> In other words, the evidence independently supports part of the address — Kirtland and the building — rather than the extraordinary institutional claim attached to it.

There is also an important difference between **origin evidence** and **content that claims an origin**. A stamp saying that a manual belonged at Kirtland is internal evidence. An archival accession record showing that the manual was recovered from Kirtland files would be external provenance. SOM1-01 has the former, not the latter. Its documented public history instead begins with the anonymous 1994 mailing to Berliner.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/source/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on specialoperationsmanual.com.</span></span></span>

## Personnel initials make the document more interesting, but not authenticated

The change-control page produces a more striking coincidence. It records changes between 1954 and 1957 and associates them with initials rendered as “JRT” and “EWL”. The Woods report searching a 1955 Albuquerque telephone directory and identifying Lieutenant J. R. Totten and Colonel Edward Levine, both residing on Perimeter Road at Kirtland Air Force Base. They have presented that match as supporting evidence that the initials belonged to real personnel connected with the base.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/source/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on specialoperationsmanual.com.</span></span></span>

This is more interesting than simply finding an era-appropriate word. A fabrication whose initials unexpectedly corresponded to appropriately located military personnel would be a genuine coincidence worth investigating. But the strength of that evidence depends on questions that the claimed match does not itself answer: whether JRT and EWL uniquely identify those two men, whether either performed the relevant records function, and whether any independent record connects either man with “KB-88”, SOM1-01 or an MJ-12 organisation.

The distinction matters because the manual itself supplies only initials. Finding people with matching initials afterwards is an identification hypothesis, not the same thing as finding their signatures on an independently authenticated copy or discovering a service record placing them in the alleged programme. The proponents' own earlier presentation described the effort as a process of identifying people with those initials and trying to determine who might have been responsible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/2014/12/30/som1-01-overview-authentication/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOM1-01SOM1-01 Overview &amp; Authentication</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOM1-01December 30, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 30, 2014</span></span></span> Later proponent accounts use stronger language about “near-confirmation” of JRT and EWL, but the supporting case remains dependent on the inferred correspondence between initials and names.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/2014/12/28/why-the-critics-are-wrong-about-som1-01-mufon-journal-jan-2015/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOM1-01Why The Critics Are Wrong About SOM1-01</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOM1-01Why The Critics Are Wrong About SOM1-01</span></span></span>

That makes the initials useful as **corroborative consistency**, but weak as independent authentication. They become substantially stronger only if evidence independent of SOM1-01 links Totten or Levine to the specific unit, records office or document.

## Period terminology shows research, not necessarily authorship

Defenders also point to small linguistic details that seem more natural in a 1950s manual than in a document casually composed in the 1990s. Robert Wood has highlighted “First Aid” with initial capitals, “screw driver” as two words and “Kraft tape” as examples of usage he regards as appropriate to the claimed period. He has also compared SOM1-01's layout and typography with contemporary military manuals and the Government Printing Office's style conventions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/2014/12/30/som1-01-overview-authentication/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOM1-01SOM1-01 Overview &amp; Authentication</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOM1-01December 30, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 30, 2014</span></span></span>

These details have legitimate evidential value. A supposed 1954 document packed with vocabulary, typography or bureaucratic conventions that arose decades later would face a serious authenticity problem. Conversely, sustained period accuracy means that a fabrication hypothesis must accommodate a reasonably knowledgeable or careful fabricator.

But the logic is asymmetrical. An anachronism can sometimes strongly falsify a claimed date; the absence of anachronisms does not comparably prove authenticity. A modern historical novelist can use the correct ranks, addresses, slang and equipment for 1944 without having written the novel in 1944. The same principle applies to documentary fabrication. Correct period details establish that the author *could reproduce* historical information, not that the document itself has the history it claims.

This point was explicitly raised in the 1999 joint statement by Jan Aldrich, Don Berliner, Thomas Deuley, Richard Hall and Mark Rodeghier, researchers with backgrounds that included military intelligence, classified-document handling, cryptology and government-document research. They regarded SOM1-01 as a deliberate fake and stressed the absence of an identifiable source and an original capable of forensic examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://groups.google.com/g/alt.paranet.ufo/c/Kms-l8AywQw" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: groups.google.com">[Google Groups]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">groups.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Groups The Happening!!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google GroupsThe Happening!!March 31, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 31, 2002</span></span></span> Their objection to accurate details was essentially that genuine information can be incorporated into a constructed document; the Woods responded that this possibility should not itself be treated as evidence that such fabrication occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/faq/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Authenticity - SOM1-01...</span></span></span>

That exchange identifies the correct evidential balance. Critics cannot prove fabrication merely by saying that a knowledgeable person *could* have researched the details. But proponents likewise cannot turn successful historical research into proof of official authorship. The period details shift probabilities only to the extent that they would have been genuinely difficult for a prospective fabricator to discover or reproduce.

## Some details are stronger tests than others

Not every historically plausible feature deserves equal weight. It helps to separate SOM1-01's period evidence into different levels.

**Ordinary period facts** — the existence of Kirtland AFB, contemporary postal abbreviations and broadly appropriate military terminology — are weak authentication evidence because they were potentially obtainable from public records, old manuals, directories and libraries.

**Less obvious correlations**, such as the alleged correspondence between JRT/EWL and people living at Kirtland, are more interesting because they require more specialised research or coincidence. Their value nevertheless depends on proving that the people identified are actually the people represented by the initials.

**Production characteristics** can potentially be stronger still. The Woods argue that SOM1-01 resembles contemporary Government Printing Office work, uses Monotype Modern and contains raised lower-case “z” characters characteristic of hot-metal typesetting. They have compared those features with a genuine period publication and regard them as particularly difficult to simulate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/2014/12/28/summary-skeptic-objections-to-authenticity/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOM1-01Summary Skeptic Objections to Authenticity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOM1-01Summary Skeptic Objections to Authenticity</span></span></span>

Yet even this last category addresses the age and method of *production* more directly than official *provenance*. A document made using period printing technology could still contain invented information, and a later reproduction could imitate earlier production features. More fundamentally, investigators do not possess an authenticated original SOM1-01 whose paper, ink, impressions and other physical properties can be examined directly. The proponents themselves concede that original paper-and-ink testing would provide decisive evidence about whether the material came from the appropriate period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/faq/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Authenticity - SOM1-01...</span></span></span>

That concession is important because photographs inevitably discard physical information. Researchers can study visible typography and layout in the film images, but cannot obtain from those images the same forensic evidence that would be available from the purported paper manual.

## Why accurate details can still appear in a fabrication

A convincing historical fabrication normally works by combining true background with false attribution. Real places, genuine bureaucratic conventions and correctly dated terminology make the invented element less conspicuous. SOM1-01's period detail therefore has to be evaluated according to how independently inaccessible that information would have been before the document surfaced in 1994.

This is where claims such as the Kirtland building and telephone-directory matches have a built-in limitation. Old telephone directories, military publications, government printing conventions and historical records are precisely the kinds of sources from which a determined document creator can gather authentic texture. The 1999 critics consequently argued that accurate information within SOM1-01 could not by itself establish authenticity and called for [original documents]({{ 'original-document/' | relative_url }}), a certifiable archival trail and forensic examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/faq/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Authenticity - SOM1-01...</span></span></span>

The alternative explanation does not require imagining an omniscient forger. It requires only enough historical knowledge to avoid obvious mistakes. Indeed, the very mixture of verifiable ordinary details and unverifiable extraordinary designations is what makes provenance so important. “Kirtland AFB” can be checked; “Building 21” can apparently be checked; but the leap from those facts to “KB-88” and ultimately to an extraterrestrial-recovery organisation is the part that requires independent evidence.

This also explains why correcting an alleged anachronism has limited positive force. Defenders have answered objections involving phrases such as “downed satellites” by pointing out that artificial satellites were widely discussed before Sputnik, even though the first successful orbital launch came in 1957.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/2014/12/30/som1-01-overview-authentication/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SOM1-01SOM1-01 Overview &amp; Authentication</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SOM1-01December 30, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 30, 2014</span></span></span> If that rebuttal is accepted, it removes a proposed reason for calling the passage impossible in 1954. It does not establish that an MJ-12 writer actually composed it in 1954. Removing evidence against authenticity is not automatically evidence sufficient to prove authenticity.

## What the period details really establish

The strongest defensible conclusion is narrower than either “the details prove SOM1-01” or “the details mean nothing”. They do mean something. The Kirtland references, older address style, change-control entries, plausible initials and period-looking language collectively suggest that SOM1-01 was not thrown together without historical research. Some details are sufficiently specific to make the document a more sophisticated authenticity problem than a crude UFO hoax.

They still stop short of the crucial evidential bridge. No independently authenticated military record produced in the normal archival chain has been shown to connect SOM1-01 to Building 21, KB-88, Totten, Levine or an MJ-12 recovery programme. The publicly documented object arrived anonymously in 1994 as film, and the alleged paper original remains unavailable for independent forensic examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.specialoperationsmanual.com/source/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: specialoperationsmanual.com">[SOM1-01]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">specialoperationsmanual.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on specialoperationsmanual.com.</span></span></span>

That is why **consistency and provenance should not be scored as though they were interchangeable**. Period consistency asks, “Could this detail plausibly belong in the 1950s?” Provenance asks, “Can we establish that this particular document actually came from the institution, people and period it claims?” SOM1-01 contains several details for which the first answer may reasonably be yes. The evidence presently available does not convert those successes into a secure answer to the second question.

For the wider SOM1-01 authenticity dispute, that makes the period details supporting evidence rather than a foundation. They can raise the cost of the fabrication hypothesis — requiring a knowledgeable, careful creator — but they cannot replace the [missing chain]({{ 'missing-chain/' | relative_url }}) of custody. The most consequential discovery would therefore not be one more correct 1950s expression or another real building. It would be an independently authenticated record tying one of SOM1-01's distinctive, supposedly secret identifiers to the contemporary government system that allegedly produced the manual.
